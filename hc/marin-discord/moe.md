# #moe

Archive coverage: 2025-04-29–2026-09-19 (UTC); 1,734 messages, including 559 thread replies. [Open channel in Discord](https://discord.com/channels/1354881461060243556/1365044508546568372).

## The research question

The channel asks when sparse mixture-of-experts (MoE) models deliver better quality per unit of compute than dense models, and what routing and systems choices make that gain real. Early discussion weighed training experts from scratch against upcycling a dense checkpoint. An OLMoE comparison cited in the channel found a scratch MoE catching an upcycled one after roughly 500–600B tokens; participants suspected that upcycling from a cooled-down checkpoint could inhibit expert specialization. That was a hypothesis for Marin, not a Marin result. [Scratch/upcycling comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1366843605913829376); [checkpoint concern](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1367289246196568215).

Several attractive designs remained proposals rather than results. For example, independently trained math and code experts could theoretically be attached to a base model, with a router learning when each specialist helps, but the channel did not establish that such “hot-pluggable” experts work. Likewise, a low-loss MoE is not automatically the best deployed model: equal training FLOPs, elapsed time, total parameters and high-bandwidth-memory demand, active parameters, inference cost, and downstream quality answer different questions. Some early dense comparisons also had unmatched batches, auxiliary losses folded into the training-loss curve, missing evaluations, or runs still in progress. [Specialist-expert idea](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1373032519993266257); [comparison caution](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1437550421597094050); [incomplete comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1463277041964023880).

By March 2026, the group proposed an iterative baseline: change one part of the recipe, compare it at equal FLOPs, and promote it only if projected loss, routing stability, training stability, and hardware cost improve together. The first recorded iteration predicted uncheatable macro loss 1.8324 at 1e21 FLOPs. Prediction quality still had to be checked at larger scales. [Baseline criteria](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1481488141012697190); [first iteration](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1482069994643783991).

## Routing and optimization

Small MoEs initially routed poorly enough to make scaling-law fits misleading. Setting embedding initialization standard deviation to 1 and zero-initializing output projections produced nearly balanced initial routing in one test, but the balance did not survive longer training without embedding-specific learning-rate treatment. A missing embedding RMSNorm was subsequently noticed during a template refactor, another reason not to attribute every difference to the optimizer. A later variant explored row-wise AdamH embeddings, muP-like initialization, a PID controller for router bias, auxiliary-loss-free balancing, and sigmoid routing. Those changes were still ablations, not a settled recipe. [Initialization test](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1477949253299994706); [longer-run failure](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1478489849244680313); [RMSNorm omission](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1478492388614737930); [variant plan](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1484269845758349412).

The stability work repeatedly distinguished a real model failure from a measurement or data-pipeline fault. A GPT-OSS-style 20B MoE reached 34.4% model-FLOP utilization (MFU) at batch 640 and sequence length 4096 without expert parallelism, yet an early Nemotron-CC run hit NaNs while FineWeb did not. The later diagnosis implicated a linear-permutation/shuffling footgun; a pure-Nemotron run with Feistel shuffling did not show the same spikes. This does not prove Nemotron is universally safe, but the original “dataset causes NaNs” interpretation did not stand. Similarly, a reported static load-violation metric accidentally included the first dense blocks. One aux-free-balancing attempt even produced negative training loss because of an implementation bug. [Initial run](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1460369792593822000); [shuffling correction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1466550532612165807); [Feistel test](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1473415907467329650); [metric correction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1468764584402878505); [aux-free bug](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1470586359956770937).

An expert does not receive the same effective batch as a dense parameter: its approximate tokens per update are `num_tokens × experts_per_token / num_experts`. Participants used this to question direct hyperparameter transfer from dense models, especially with many fine-grained experts. The candidate stability recipe included fp32 router logits, top-k followed by softmax, QK normalization, dense initial blocks, and load-balancing controls; each added complexity or compute and required an ablation. One AdamC sweep removed divergence and expert-death symptoms on the corrected dataset setup, but was not proof it should replace AdamH in every run. [Effective batch](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1473414770542776503); [AdamC sweep](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1477023596760141929).

At 3e18 FLOPs, changing the learning-rate schedule to linear decay lowered reported bits per byte (BPB) from 1.047 to 1.016. A proposed explanation was that AdamW's growing parameter norms implicitly lower its effective learning rate, while AdamH needs an explicit decay; the mechanism was not established by this one comparison. Quantile balancing (QB) solved for expert-bias adjustments. A 64-iteration exact solve balanced well but damaged MFU; a cheaper small-batch approximation remained slightly slower than PID balancing, yet required fewer hyperparameters and improved balance. QB then let one setup remove 0.25× expert padding with roughly 10–15% faster training and nearly unchanged BPB (0.8756 versus 0.8769). An apparent earlier QB speedup had to be rejected because the computation had moved outside the timing harness. [Schedule](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1485898126454820907); [QB iterations](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1481340778033844286); [corrected benchmark](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1485444002638594231); [padding result](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1487197835580412055); [timing correction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1485443721788129340).

Router statistics remained ambiguous even when training loss looked smooth. In one run, initially near-orthogonal router vectors reached roughly 60% cosine alignment, especially in early layers. Two experts in layer 3 took about 70% of assignments; participants cautioned that a Zipfian token distribution may make exactly uniform loads an inappropriate target. September ladder tests showed router excursions concentrated in single layers, later found in older runs too. Their cause and quality impact remained open, but they did not automatically block deployment. [Alignment](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1489131651920564244); [load interpretation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1489356958967398480); [later investigation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1550559747042840706).

Optimizer comparisons exposed a quality-versus-throughput trade-off. A first Muon setup retained a small-model in-domain gain but lost on out-of-domain code (GitHub/C++ loss 2.55 versus 2.2 for AdamH). Code was present in the training mixture, so the discussion did not reduce this to “no code data”; it prompted closer data-mixture and held-out-domain evaluation. A MuonH variant kept AdamH on the language-model head and reported 20–30% fewer steps to comparable quality, including code, without lower token throughput in that small-scale sweep. At larger width, the advantage was closer to 15%, and the projected 1e23 gain was smaller than the scaling-fit error. Optimizer assignment to gated norms and warmup also remained unsettled. [Muon regression](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1502491222105915472); [data caution](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1502739733686517950); [MuonH](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1502824423936491550); [fit uncertainty](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1504274951564431462); [gated-norm routing](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1503604346409975988).

A June claim that MuonH remained ahead after 5,000 training tokens per active parameter was later invalidated: the AdamH baseline used 64 experts, while MuonH used 256. The channel called for a matched-expert rerun. Learning-rate cooldown differences could also mimic apparent long-run saturation. The usable lesson is methodological: optimizer and architecture must be controlled together before turning a training-curve gap into a long-overtraining recommendation. [Initial claim](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1516145091734016162); [correction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1518686897667440782); [cooldown concern](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1516218007091679413).

## Systems and scale

Fine-grained experts exposed a recurrent systems cost. An early 8-of-64 setup lost roughly half its MFU when the expert count rose and each expert became smaller. Later profiling of OLMoE-style routing found permutation, unpermutation, top-k sorting and gathers substantial enough that fewer, larger experts raised measured MFU from about 18% to 27% in one comparison; Mixtral-style larger experts reached about 44.5% in another context. Replacing `argsort` and `take` with `lax.sort`, and retaining the permutation for the reverse scatter, improved the MLP forward microbenchmark by about 20%, not necessarily the whole training step. An expert-count sweep therefore needs both quality and throughput, with enough tokens per expert to keep kernels busy. [Early granularity](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1369823526323748864); [routing profile](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1466234076762738875); [expert-size comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1466354822444744724); [microbenchmark](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1467099461133144085).

The expert kernel choice changed with workload and implementation. A July 2025 first look found `ragged_dot` faster but more memory-hungry; an August Mixtral-style comparison measured GMM at about 17 MFU versus 9.5 for ragged dot, despite similar standard evaluations. A later benchmark again favored GMM, found higher top-k expensive, and saw a modest gain from shared-expert fusion. The differing measurements are a reason to preserve hardware, shapes, rematerialization, and timing method with any throughput claim. [First look](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1398504982000701480); [Mixtral comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1410155862533603339); [later benchmark](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1470278088041562346).

Not every severe regression came from the MoE design. In August 2025, a tracer leak was tied to auxiliary-loss logging, while a multislice failure reproduced on a dense model. A later explicit mesh-axis change pushed one Mixtral path below 2% MFU; reverting it recovered approximately 38–41% on v5p-32. In October, Levanter's small Mixtral variants were at or below 23% MFU while an official MaxText example reported about 54% on v5p-128; a later MaxText reproduction reached approximately 53% on v5p-64 with synthetic data. These were useful engineering baselines, not controlled model-to-model comparisons with identical hardware and data. [Tracer leak](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1410415775319330908); [dense reproduction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1410682758346571839); [mesh regression](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1472395667446956104); [revert](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1473454272661164074); [MaxText reproduction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1432963734065643611).

Expert parallelism (EP) was similarly workload-dependent. A small-batch microbenchmark favored EP, while a 10× larger batch favored data parallelism; a later EP bug fix did not materially change that observation. Sender-to-expert all-to-all communication, expert occupancy, and memory pressure have to be evaluated at the intended batch size. TPU scoped-VMEM limits also differed by generation: one v5p case could raise the limit with `LIBTPU_INIT_ARGS`, whereas a v4 case could not use the same escape hatch and needed a different backend or block size. This is distinct from model-parameter high-bandwidth memory. [EP/DP sweep](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1476324578941734932); [post-fix result](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1476333692589703169); [VMEM limits](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1475947545456021767); [v4 workaround](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1476991311864139910).

Architecture sweeps generated qualified, often scale-specific conclusions. More routed-expert granularity could improve loss while imposing a much larger MFU penalty: one four-to-eight-expert-granularity change was judged a poor exchange. A latent-MoE design raised MFU but worsened loss at small width, with wider models more promising; the group wanted equal-FLOP, equal-wall-clock and inference comparisons before promotion. Alternating dense and MoE layers instead of combining shared and routed experts in each layer produced similar loss with around 20% more tokens per second in one small TPU test, but still needed GPU and larger-scale checks. A May review favored 128 experts at its tested scale, shared experts, an attention gate, XSA, gated normalization and 2,048-token sliding-window attention; it did not settle the full expert-count, top-k, latent-MoE, or optimizer sweeps. [Granularity](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1504700612187652147); [latent MoE](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1490772930802483270); [alternating layers](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1516827092334149684); [May review](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1506065354877702186); [unresolved items](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1506065532313403433).

Renormalized sigmoid routing illustrates why small-scale gains need qualification. Earlier sigmoid scaling without renormalization was unhelpful; a later setting around `X=2.5` was reported to give roughly a 5% effective speedup at one scale, with larger-scale validation pending. Discussions of low-probability routed experts and null routing were design questions, not established wins. Similarly, partial key offset offered about 10% compute efficiency in a small-to-mid-scale test but created posttraining/inference-kernel complexity; the channel wanted a midtrain-to-SFT-to-RL evaluation before putting it into a large run. [Sigmoid result](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1505932024291004528); [null-routing question](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1505633305993805955); [partial key offset](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1515148637322350804); [end-to-end caveat](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1515302076211793931).

## Evaluation must account for capacity and token drops

The clearest evaluation failure appeared after a 1e23-scale run resumed with EP=8 and a ragged all-to-all implementation. Throughput improved and training loss matched, but held-out GitHub/C++ perplexity worsened. The investigation found out-of-distribution samples concentrating on particular experts, overflowing their capacity and dropping evaluation tokens. In a cited 1e22 checkpoint, raising the evaluation capacity factor from 1 to 4 after training changed GitHub/C++ BPB from 0.4882 to 0.4259. That improvement did not require retraining; it exposed a measurement artifact. A “same training loss” check therefore cannot certify downstream quality if the inference/evaluation routing path discards tokens. [Resumed run](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1495530492001386496); [overflow diagnosis](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1495583676283486269); [capacity experiment](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1495585893459820665).

Later long-context tests showed why global load balance was insufficient. QB could keep aggregate expert loads within roughly 10%, yet an EP implementation enforced capacity per sender across hundreds of senders; local overload and dropping could persist. At 65K context, one setup tolerated a reported 40% training-token drop without obvious loss spikes, a surprising result rather than evidence that dropping is harmless. Out-of-distribution evaluation dropping could be severe enough to invalidate the metric, and shuffling evaluation inputs closed about half one observed gap. Participants also worried that sequence-position-dependent dropping could change what the model learns or sees at inference. Training, held-out evaluation, and serving may need different capacity policies, with dropless evaluation needed to judge model quality. [Global versus sender-local balance](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1540519787938058332); [40% observation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1540520505717559316); [eval warning](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1540566827225055233); [shuffle test](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1536503625227051128); [position concern](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1540568271361679420).

## The larger training program

The larger program moved from dense Delphi models to a roughly 129B-total/15B-active MoE trained on 1T tokens, then a 67B-total/2B-active model planned for 10T tokens with a new data mix. At 300B tokens, the latter had reached loss around 2.55, comparable to the finished 1e22 dense model in the channel's account; by August its first 8T tokens were described as scaling predictably. That does not establish final downstream superiority or isolate architecture from the data-mix change. [Model comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1521726976120066048); [10T-token launch](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1521008406184460288); [later scaling observation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1537559415052439692).

The next scaling ladder could not assume the old data distribution: phase-dependent mixtures and repeated high-quality subsets might change the best architecture, optimizer, or schedule. One proposed loop alternated architecture improvement with re-fitting the data mix. Participants also warned that a fixed-FLOP sweep near the usual training optimum may not identify the best model for thousands of tokens per active parameter. Those were scaling-program proposals, not established laws. [Architecture–data loop](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1511227646715887626); [overtraining target](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1511573274348486849).

The 67B-total/2B-active run made those scale effects concrete. A phase-two data mixture and a sequence-length move from 8K to 65K reduced measured tokens per second by about 30%; with roughly one sequence per chip, QB's per-chip updates had less data and load balance became harder. The new mixture also changed the baseline training loss, so a discontinuity was not automatically a model regression. An Iris restart coincided with a step-time change from 26.15 to 30.05 seconds, with TPU topology suspected but not proved. By August the channel reported the lowest Paloma macro loss in its comparison, while noting its 65K context made that comparison non-identical. These reports support continuing the run and measuring downstream behavior, not a settled architecture ranking. [Phase-two effects](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1525009152290586686); [QB constraint](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1525037311409262604); [restart timing](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1524108412894318603); [Paloma observation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1539041994008039516).
