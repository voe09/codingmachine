# #moe

Archive coverage: 2025-04-29–2026-09-19 (UTC); 1,734 messages, including 559 thread replies. [Open channel in Discord](https://discord.com/channels/1354881461060243556/1365044508546568372).

## The research question

The channel asks when sparse mixture-of-experts (MoE) models deliver better quality per unit of compute than dense models, and what routing and systems choices make that gain real. Early discussion weighed training experts from scratch against upcycling a dense checkpoint. An OLMoE comparison cited in the channel found a scratch MoE catching an upcycled one after roughly 500–600B tokens; participants suspected that upcycling from a cooled-down checkpoint could inhibit expert specialization. That was a hypothesis for Marin, not a Marin result. [Scratch/upcycling comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1366843605913829376); [checkpoint concern](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1367289246196568215).

By March 2026, the group proposed an iterative baseline: change one part of the recipe, compare it at equal FLOPs, and promote it only if projected loss, routing stability, training stability, and hardware cost improve together. The first recorded iteration predicted uncheatable macro loss 1.8324 at 1e21 FLOPs. Prediction quality still had to be checked at larger scales. [Baseline criteria](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1481488141012697190); [first iteration](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1482069994643783991).

## Routing and optimization

Small MoEs initially routed poorly enough to make scaling-law fits misleading. Setting embedding initialization standard deviation to 1 and zero-initializing output projections produced nearly balanced initial routing in one test. A later variant explored row-wise AdamH embeddings, muP-like initialization, a PID controller for router bias, auxiliary-loss-free balancing, and sigmoid routing. Those changes were still ablations, not a settled recipe. [Initialization test](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1477949253299994706); [variant plan](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1484269845758349412).

At 3e18 FLOPs, changing the learning-rate schedule to linear decay lowered reported BPB from 1.047 to 1.016. QB load balancing let one setup remove 0.25x expert padding with about 10–15% faster training and little BPB change. One apparent QB speedup had to be rejected because the implementation had moved QB outside the timing harness; the corrected measurement is the relevant one. [Schedule](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1485898126454820907); [load balancing](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1487197835580412055); [timing correction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1485443721788129340).

Router statistics remained ambiguous even when training loss looked smooth. In one run, initially near-orthogonal router vectors reached roughly 60% cosine alignment, especially in early layers. Two experts in layer 3 took about 70% of assignments; participants cautioned that a Zipfian token distribution may make exactly uniform loads an inappropriate target. September ladder tests showed router excursions concentrated in single layers, later found in older runs too. Their cause and quality impact remained open, but they did not automatically block deployment. [Alignment](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1489131651920564244); [load interpretation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1489356958967398480); [later investigation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1550547629958504620/1550559747042840706).

Optimizer comparisons exposed a quality-versus-throughput trade-off. AdamC removed divergence and expert-death symptoms in one OlMoE sweep. A first Muon setup retained a small-model in-domain gain but lost on out-of-domain code (GitHub/C++ loss 2.55 versus 2.2 for AdamH). A MuonH variant kept AdamH on the language-model head and reported 20–30% fewer steps to comparable quality, including code, without lower token throughput in that sweep. These are configuration-specific comparisons; larger hardware layouts have their own optimizer cost. [AdamC sweep](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1477023596760141929); [Muon regression](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1502491222105915472); [MuonH](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1502824423936491550).

## Systems and scale

The expert kernel choice changed with workload and implementation. A July 2025 first look found `ragged_dot` faster but more memory-hungry; an August Mixtral-style comparison measured GMM at about 17 MFU versus 9.5 for ragged dot, despite similar standard evaluations. A later benchmark again favored GMM, found higher top-k expensive, and saw a modest gain from shared-expert fusion. The differing measurements are a reason to preserve hardware, shapes, rematerialization, and timing method with any throughput claim. [First look](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1398504982000701480); [Mixtral comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1410155862533603339); [later benchmark](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1470278088041562346).

The larger program moved from dense Delphi models to a roughly 129B-total/15B-active MoE trained on 1T tokens, then a 67B-total/2B-active model planned for 10T tokens with a new data mix. At 300B tokens, the latter had reached loss around 2.55, comparable to the finished 1e22 dense model in the channel's account; by August its first 8T tokens were described as scaling predictably. That does not establish final downstream superiority or isolate architecture from the data-mix change. [Model comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1521726976120066048); [10T-token launch](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1521008406184460288); [later scaling observation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1537559415052439692).

The next scaling ladder could not assume the old data distribution: phase-dependent mixtures and repeated high-quality subsets might change the best architecture, optimizer, or schedule. One proposed loop alternated architecture improvement with re-fitting the data mix. Long-context training also raised a possible train/test mismatch if the effective number of routed experts depends on sequence position; the channel reported that earlier stress tests had not found a large effect at their tested scales. [Architecture–data loop](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1365044508546568372/1511227646715887626); [long-context concern](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1540202379301879838/1540568271361679420).
