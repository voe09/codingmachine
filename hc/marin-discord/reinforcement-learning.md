# #reinforcement-learning

Archive coverage: 2025-05-22–2026-09-23 (UTC); 1,852 messages, including 1,010 thread replies. [Open channel in Discord](https://discord.com/channels/1354881461060243556/1374989195109466122).

## From environments to a stable training loop

The initial roadmap separated three requirements: put different RL environments behind a common interface, build TPU training and rollout infrastructure, and support GPU backends. A `MarinEnv` callable did not fit external environments expecting an OpenAI-compatible endpoint, so adapters and hot-swappable inference weights became part of the design. In asynchronous training, rollouts also needed a policy-step identifier to discard stale samples and an environment ID to maintain a deliberate task mixture. [Roadmap](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1377493531018395770); [environment adapter](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1389427590376325131); [replay-buffer design](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1405688723827331264/1405689463878975549).

Algorithm discussion compared synchronous GRPO-style two-sided clipping, asynchronous truncated importance sampling, and KL-regularized policy-improvement formulations. The recommendation was to implement at least the first two for comparison, not to declare a universal winner. Value-based and replay-based methods were also proposed from the wider RL literature, but the cited messages are research suggestions rather than Marin benchmark results. [Algorithm comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1385442628694183996); [value-based proposal](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1388765139213484033); [replay proposal](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1493297286338318406).

The June discussion of CISPO made the clipping choice concrete. In a PPO-style clipped objective, tokens past the ratio boundary can receive zero gradient in that direction; CISPO instead clips the importance ratio multiplying a REINFORCE gradient, so those tokens can still contribute while their weight is bounded. Participants speculated that this could help repeated updates on sparse binary-reward successes, but the channel did not report a controlled Marin CISPO win at that point. A September replay discussion exposed the opposite pressure: keeping rare positive examples increases informativeness, while using old policy samples raises off-policy risk. One paper was reported to degrade after about 16 batches of delay; another method's reference-Q estimation was said to need roughly 800 samples per problem. The resulting preference was to curate tasks with non-negligible base-policy pass@k and measure freshness rather than assume deep replay helps LLM RL. [Clipping distinction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1384831971137552516/1385090904225742898); [freshness versus positives](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1420577590195781772); [delay comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1420577188478189760); [Q-estimation cost](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1420603393579286630); [task-curation argument](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1420602452146917507).

The earliest architecture decision was whether training and inference should be separate workers with weight transfers, or combined workers with a custom inference path. The choice depended on profiling weight movement, rollout throughput, and TPU inference features, including log-probability support. A working June 2025 GRPO implementation used group mean and standard deviation for advantages and KL regularization to a static reference policy on Hendrycks MATH. That milestone established a training loop, not that policy freshness and off-policy correction were solved. Participants later argued that robust use of off-policy samples could matter more than small changes to the REINFORCE/GRPO objective because inference and training inevitably diverge in asynchronous and tool-using systems. [Architecture trade-off](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1382624973394284587); [first working loop](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1386751923352371311); [off-policy motivation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1422653231196274821).

The environment interface was deliberately kept compatible with existing tools: many candidate environments accepted an OpenAI-style endpoint, whereas the early `MarinEnv` expected a callable. The group treated an OpenAI endpoint and function-calling adapter as the immediate bridge, with optional MCP translation outside the critical path; forcing all environments to become MCP servers was not the plan. The older single-worker path remained useful for math and code sanity checks while the distributed `marin/rl` path was built, but participants wanted shared environments and algorithms so these would converge. A July back-of-the-envelope estimate put transfer of a 32B model's roughly 64 GB of weights to one inference host at a few seconds on v6e; it was explicitly an optimistic estimate requiring a real bandwidth benchmark, not a measured transfer time. [Interface discussion](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1389734196167184497); [two-path migration](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1390106606833762305); [transfer estimate](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1390241311239573524); [benchmark request](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1390366062633025536).

vLLM rollout generation entered the RL pipeline in October 2025, with prebuilt regional images to reduce job startup time. Correctness remained open at that milestone. In March 2026, moving a repeatedly crashing Ray setup to on-demand v5p-8 workers produced multiple 500-step tests. Throughput improved from roughly eight minutes per step to three with larger batches and in-flight updates, then to 1.68 minutes with a second sampler. The author still reported unexplained entropy collapse: a stable executor did not imply a stable learning algorithm. [vLLM integration](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1430965818241454254); [stability and throughput report](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1486914443060449320).

The vLLM bring-up also separated two meanings of determinism. Batch invariance—getting the same output when a request is run alone or alongside other requests—would improve reproducibility and reduce one source of policy drift, but was not sufficient for on-policy training if inference and trainer implementations still computed different probabilities. The team observed small synchronous log-probability differences and kept both a vLLM path and a simpler built-in inference path for diagnosis. [Log-probability observation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1430965818241454254/1431001132062212210); [batch-invariance distinction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1430965818241454254/1431027192699289751); [reference path](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1431020798633840670).

October's vLLM bring-up shows several distinct ways an apparent algorithm problem was actually a protocol or state bug. The rollout engine prepended a BOS token differently from the trainer, shifting sampled-token log probabilities and increasing clipping; matching tokenization improved the curves, though evaluation gains still needed checking. A separate fp32-versus-bf16 suspicion did not survive an ablation: converting trainer weights to bf16 was not required. Replay sampling then proved to accept rollouts from weight step `N−1` while training `N→N+1`, despite the intended on-policy setting. Participants also found that a restarted rollout worker could accept initial Hugging Face weights instead of the trainer's latest checkpoint if weight IDs were not compared correctly. Log probabilities, prompt tokens, policy-step IDs, and restart state all needed explicit checks before interpreting an importance-ratio spike as model instability. [BOS finding](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1430965818241454254/1431133038229721138); [precision correction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1430965818241454254/1432397493680734249); [replay off-by-one](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1432905684441370904); [restart hazard](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1433130731235708979).

The objective itself needed the same scrutiny. In one October implementation labeled RLOO, the loss used only the clipped importance-ratio branch and normalized by all output tokens rather than each response's output length. The author identified it as closer to CISPO than intended RLOO. When a reward curve later rose and collapsed, the team considered KL and importance clipping but first inspected these implementation and replay details; no single algorithmic cause was established in the cited thread. [Loss audit](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1433581210461798481); [collapse investigation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1432805623682039939); [replay finding](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1432859673517887621).

The infrastructure direction shifted again in mid-2026. The GPU agent-RL work used a maintained `MarinSkyRL` fork and TaskTrove/Harbor environments, while the earlier JAX/TPU stack remained separate. The public plan was to improve that fork for current workloads while exploring a JAX training backend to align posttraining with Marin pretraining and preserve accelerator flexibility. A June Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B attempt got initial steps on Jupiter but could not sustain a reliable cadence because of node memory/topology limits; a smaller 30B-A3B MoE reached about 14 steps per hour on six nodes in that test. By late July, an agentic fleet report cited roughly 1,000 seconds median step time for 30B-A3B Qwen3-Coder with 32 H100s, 32K context, router replay and the OpenCode harness. Those are stack-specific throughput observations, not comparable MFU benchmarks. [SkyRL fork](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1512425813088276643); [JAX direction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1520132015213772941); [MoE hardware limit](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1513608485709938718/1514922629604315159); [fleet timing](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1531406747951042731/1531407442620186795).

The backend decision remained empirical. An August report found a broad range of hyperparameters could reach a similar short-run peak, while long-run stability was more sensitive; in that comparison, Megatron trained the policy faster per 1,000 response tokens than FSDP2 but had worse entropy instability. A September Slime sanity check showed early gains in two agentic RL runs, without a clear held-out or speed advantage over the integrated MarinSkyRL setup; the runs were not matched closely enough for a definitive backend ranking. [Backend/HPO report](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1539328824502648914); [Slime comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1545555200889192508).

The agentic campaign also turned dataset preparation into a measurable part of RL development. In a two-week TaskTrove effort, the team reported repairing about 195,000 task instances and making roughly 155,000 RL-ready, while reducing each experimental arm from eight nodes/64 GPUs to four nodes/32 GPUs and expanding its context budget. These numbers describe an infrastructure/data campaign, not evidence that the repaired tasks all transferred to held-out benchmarks. The proposed next tests were regularization and shaped-reward verifiers to sustain long runs. [Campaign close-out](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1532430279950467142/1532431422931992768).

Task sourcing still needed empirical screening. A teacher-model comparison found higher task reward on 24-plus sources when moving from Qwen3-Coder through a larger Qwen3.5 model to GLM-5.2, but that was evidence about teachers, not yet that students trained on those outputs improved. A PyMethods2Test run had flat training reward and no downstream benefit in one report. Likewise, the first completed few percent of asynchronous rollout groups were faster than typical groups, so using them as an early quality estimate selected for duration. Some apparent verifier timeouts were traced instead to Harbor retries. [Teacher comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1539990362716897372); [PyMethods2Test](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1539940426558083082); [async-selection and retry finding](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1537877729452630027).

## Base models, task selection, and collapse

Early math trials illustrated base-model dependence. Qwen3-1.7B learned a format reward and had better pass@64 than the tested Llama-1B-Instruct; another report got Qwen3-8B to roughly 0.7 on its task while Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct stagnated near 0.43 before failure. These are setup-specific observations, but they show why a weak RL outcome cannot be attributed solely to the algorithm or dataset. [1.7B comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1439700923654864998); [8B report](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1445586750264311929).

Even when pass@64 exceeded pass@1, early math training often showed little gradient signal. A November 2025 investigation suspected many rollout groups had all-zero or all-one reward and proposed logging within-group reward variance, masking truncated completions, and sampling prompts likely to produce informative groups. A contemporaneous Qwen3-8B Math-500 comparison differed sharply across two stacks—about 60% versus 20% in the report—with incomplete, truncated answers among the suspected causes. This was a reason to log actual generations and align environment, chat template, and output limits before interpreting an RL curve as an algorithm result. [Reward-variance investigation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1438965911800385617); [cross-stack discrepancy](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1440512154020216964).

The debugging program then produced a stronger reference point. A bare-bones loop designed to make training and inference bitwise-consistent supplied a way to locate divergence step by step. After porting the Tinker math environment and aligning the setup, a Marin Llama-3.1-8B run reached the same reported test accuracy as the Tinker baseline, but needed about twice as many optimization steps—not twice the wall-clock time, which was a separate question. The team listed remaining differences: opaque details of Tinker's built-in loss, training-versus-sampling numerics, full fine-tuning versus Tinker's LoRA setup, and data shuffle. A later async vLLM Qwen-1.7B report rose from 0.546 to 0.66 with a 4,096-token output cap and from 0.598 to 0.694 with an 8,192-token cap over roughly 400 steps; those are different settings and starting points, not a clean output-length ablation. [Bitwise reference loop](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1438647942079582480); [Tinker reproduction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1441572678082367560); [step-count correction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1441573318770692147); [remaining differences](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1441584018507304991); [async Qwen run](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1442581562263863426).

Training/sampling consistency had more than one source. In November, trainer log probabilities omitted the generation temperature, and adding it reduced one prompt's maximum discrepancy from about 0.41 to 0.14 and mean discrepancy from 0.024 to 0.006; it did not eliminate the difference. In another trial, longer math generations seemed to amplify mismatch even when short synthetic tasks learned. The answer parser and prompt format mattered too: a model expected to emit `<answer>` tags could receive zero reward for a mathematically correct answer in another format. Before changing clipping or learning rates, the channel recommended saving generations alongside ground truth and reward so a human could audit the grader. [Temperature finding](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1436435366327484456); [longer-horizon concern](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1436408729733038160); [format mismatch](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1439053972836188281/1439064138566340638); [audit recommendation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1439053972836188281/1439370351921332224).

For agent training, dataset quality depended on the starting model and its reachable tasks. One proposal argued that a fixed-data comparison can mislead when an already competent model finds the tasks too easy or a weaker model cannot solve any of them. A controlled R2E-gym experiment filtered 4,500 tasks to about 1,600 with learnable headroom based on the base model's pass@4. The filtered run reached roughly 45 SWE-Bench pass@1 in 60 rather than 120 steps and used 48,000 versus 60,000 total rollouts including filtering, but did not exceed the final performance of the raw pool. Filtering bought efficiency in that setup, not a higher ceiling. [Base-dependent argument](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1518700249902743773/1518725530751602758); [filtering experiment](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1531644649230438591).

Further passes showed the distinction between in-domain learning and transfer. Three epochs on a 784-task learnable band raised pass@1 from 27.64% to 34.64% and pass@8 from 61.45% to 65.99%, with most gain in the first one or two epochs. On BugsInPy, fewer than 25 steps brought pass@1 up to the base model's pass@16 within that task source; that is a striking sample-efficiency result, not evidence of broad software-engineering transfer. A later experiment found that a supposedly saturated SFT model still learned on medium-difficulty tasks when binary reward was replaced by pass-rate reward and zero-variance groups were filtered, but reported training reward rather than downstream validation. [Learnable-band reuse](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1546795289174605844); [BugsInPy](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1546476043081224193); [reward change](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1549723132821119008).

Reward and held-out performance were monitored through training because models could collapse after an initial gain. In one SFT-8B/PyMethods sweep, a run with learning rate 4e-6 reached 33% on SWE-Bench-100 at step 20 and then declined; an even higher learning rate also collapsed. In-training evaluation and reward curves moved together in that report, under a shorter, unsummarized harness. The measurement was useful for detecting collapse but was not identical to the final evaluation protocol. [Collapse experiment](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1513172211991838901/1513838512670249102).

The follow-up cautioned against treating the late gradient-norm spike as the root cause. A custom adaptive learning-rate/clipping mechanism suppressed that spike, yet the policy still converged prematurely; simple z-clipping had not helped. TIS, sequence normalization, and reward shaping showed more stable training dynamics in an ablation report, but its author had not yet checked held-out evaluations. In particular, sequence normalization alone was weak, and a naive reward based on the fraction of pytest tests passing did not reproduce the favorable agent behavior. These are reasons to measure within-group reward variance, importance ratios, and validation together rather than call a smooth reward curve success. [Clipping and trailing indicator](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1513172211991838901/1514259652639195136); [remaining convergence problem](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1513172211991838901/1514259734616866826); [ablation caveat](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1512791848882536529/1514742401837826160); [pytest shaping](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1515148788929396737/1515364358010572912).

Hardware-stable training was not automatically scientifically better, but an apparent example was substantially revised. An 8B agent-RL run reproduced on Perlmutter but not Jupiter under matched settings and seeds. The Perlmutter run had a gradient/entropy spike and more exploratory tool use; Jupiter trained smoothly yet produced shorter, earlier submissions. This initially motivated an objective/evaluation-mismatch hypothesis and shaped-reward and sequence-normalization ablations. In July, however, the group found that the evaluation harness was failing while summarizing many other runs, selectively depressing their scores. The earlier claim that this “hero” run alone transferred should therefore not be treated as a controlled result. [Original interpretation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1512791848882536529); [correction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1523287925654159360/1523289161245655142).

The summarization failure itself had a correction. A first description called it a timeout. A later log audit found no timeout lines: the summary completion hit a `max_tokens` length limit when invoked with too little remaining context, raising `OutputLengthExceededError` and aborting trials as failures. In one cited base evaluation, 206 of 300 trials aborted. After re-evaluation without this failure mode, SFT plus RL still generally beat SFT alone in the analyzed comparison, but the best distillation-only approaches were close at 8B and differences among exploration-inducing RL variants were not strongly statistically significant. A behavioral finding survived: RL helped the agent complete a diagnose–edit–test–submit loop instead of stopping at inspection. [Initial timeout description](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1523287925654159360/1524112005945102446); [log correction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1523287925654159360/1524119657634595117); [corrected comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1523287925654159360/1523290322870861985); [behavioral finding](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1523287925654159360/1523290587862667354).

The pre-correction trajectory analysis is still useful as a hypothesis about *what* changed: the favored run made more tool calls and more source edits, while several other RL runs lengthened deliberation without increasing action. The authors also noted that their measured thought tokens omitted reasoning placed in a harness-specific `analysis` field. Because the evaluation bug changed the comparison, those traces should guide controlled action-versus-inspection measurements, not be presented as proof that one recipe uniquely transferred. [Trajectory analysis](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1515148788929396737/1515148923289731163); [thought-token caveat](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1515148788929396737/1515451747424932051).

## Snowball results and transfer limits

The Delphi reasoning-RL close-out offered a different, non-agentic scaling point. Three large GRPO runs on two math datasets used batch 256, group size 16 and 128 A100s, taking roughly 80–100 hours each. All improved on the best midtraining-plus-SFT checkpoint overall, by up to nine points in the reported comparison, but each regressed on at least one benchmark. The complete midtraining→SFT→RL path lifted Delphi 1e22 from 1.6 to 53.4 on MATH-500, 0 to 6.6 on AIME24, and 11.0 to 68.6 on GSM8K. Those end-to-end gains should not be attributed to the RL stage alone. [Setup](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1521285492602048542/1521286343571804241); [stage-specific caveat](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1521285492602048542/1521287129819517048); [end-to-end scores](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1521285492602048542/1521288546697085150).

A reported Snowball math-RL experiment improved AIME24 from 17.67% to 27%, MATH-500 from 64% to 78%, and OlympiadBench from 12.67% to 20% on the specified checkpoint and recipe. An accompanying pass@k analysis found that successful RL runs often broadened the set of solvable samples as well as raising pass@1; switching to a stronger agentic SFT base raised the apparent ceiling and narrowed the remaining gap. These results should not be extrapolated to every base model or environment. [Math results](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1542681739497832499); [pass@k analysis](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1544006720496599131).

Snowball optimizer and routing ablations also resisted a simple training-reward story. With harmonized parameter-group learning rates, AdamW learned faster and peaked higher than Muon-H in one posttraining comparison, without a consistent held-out Muon-H advantage. A reproduction with higher *training* reward than the reference still scored lower on all three held-out math suites. For an MoE run, moving from expert parallelism 1 to 8 coincided with an extreme early gradient-norm spike, treated as a likely bug rather than an inherent parallelism penalty. Freezing router bias left many experts almost unchanged, whereas updating it broadened routing but initially hurt held-out tests until the SFT bias was restored. The routing interpretation remained a hypothesis about coadaptation, not a settled rule. [Optimizer and reward comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1542683953805402122); [expert-parallel spike](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1542685341113385041); [router-bias test](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1542689784143675505).

A September 2026 exploratory curriculum study found that strict grade-level ordering was a poor guide to what a model could learn: estimated difficulty could waste rollouts, while the first attempt on a problem provided direct information. Adaptive Thompson sampling on expected batch informativeness did slightly better than naive sampling at matched tokens in the author's Qwen and Snowball tests, but the effect was noisy. Aggressive sampling could also miss easy-looking formatting failures—for example, a model trained to emit boxed answers failed GSM8K instructions without lacking the underlying math skill. The proposed direction was conservative adaptation, not an always-hardest curriculum. [Curriculum experiment and limitations](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1544482297237995591).

The agent-RL stack comparison raised pass@1 on 265 held-out r2egym tasks from 50.1% to 59.8%, with pass@8 moving from 92.1% to 95.1%. The author explicitly listed concurrent Harbor, tooling, and data fixes as confounders, so this was a system comparison rather than an isolated algorithm ablation. A separate Snowball run roughly doubled SWE-Bench-100 pass@1 after r2egym RL but did not improve Terminal-Bench 2. It also made the model submit solutions more often, including wrong ones. Later, SFT followed by RL was reported to add more than eight SWE-Bench points over either stage alone in that setup. The result points toward task-specific environments and testing the SFT-to-RL handoff. [Stack comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1549558259713703986); [transfer test](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1550202720433086504); [SFT-to-RL result](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1552048522403250178).

The transfer limit became sharper in September. On Snowball, RTS was mostly unsolvable by the base model within eight attempts, leaving little RL signal. Endless Terminals was relatively easy and gained 13 points in-domain, yet Terminal-Bench 2 changed only from 6.7% to 7.9% (paired difference 1.1 ± 1.6 points, not significant). Instruction-following tasks packaged as a sandbox gained about 21 points in-domain without measurable RTS transfer; packaging the same tasks as ordinary chat exposed a severe format mismatch. A later SFT checkpoint markedly improved ordinary-prompt format compliance, so the mismatch should not be treated as a permanent model limitation. The general lesson is to inspect *both* learnable-band coverage and the deployment protocol before declaring cross-domain agent gains. [RTS and Endless Terminals](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1551722069488836719); [instruction-following packaging](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1551722531038433361); [later format improvement](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1552102893576921119).

## Preference optimization beside RL

The channel also carried DPO/SimPO experiments. An initial SimPO trial appeared to score zero on AlpacaEval; after fixing a chat-template issue, the author found it could approach DPO with more tuning of its beta and margin parameters, while output length varied substantially. The posted sweep still showed the particular DPO configuration ahead on reported win-rate metrics, so the claim was not that SimPO unconditionally matched DPO. In February, LoRA+DPO made 70B fine-tuning feasible on a v5p-128 but lagged full fine-tuning on an early AlpacaEval comparison, about 5 versus 15, leading to rank-tuning questions. By May, a broader DPO study reported LoRA matching or slightly exceeding full fine-tuning on the tested metrics with a different, zero-A/Gaussian-B initialization. The sequence illustrates why adapter versus full-tuning conclusions depend on configuration and evaluation; the later report does not retroactively erase the earlier gap. [SimPO correction and sweep](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1465467211228512458); [early LoRA gap](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1470299955145998531); [later DPO report](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1364827114670657616/1507158614127808693).

## Context and protocol effects

Agent success also depended on the conversation renderer. Before RL, more than 90% of one 64k-context Snowball model's r2egym attempts ended in context overflow, with re-fed reasoning traces accounting for over 30% of input tokens. In a 160-plus-task probe, trimming that history did not initially hurt pass@8 but increased turns until attempts again overflowed; it also shifted the model toward less reasoning later in the conversation. A short RL run with dropped history reinforced that behavior. This made simple trace truncation an unsafe general fix, even though a future 260k-context model might change the practical constraint. [Reasoning-history ablation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1547120190758584320).

The 32k-to-64k context change was not merely a larger number on a model card: a pre-RL SFT probe reported that 98.3% of 224 Snowball tasks overflowed 32k, while expanding the cap allowed many runs to continue. Attempts to train a learned condensation policy imposed a 15–20-fold slowdown in an early test because context management interrupted append-only generation. Context budget, history rendering, and summarization behavior therefore belong in the experiment definition. [Overflow measurement](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1545106639206621304); [append-only concern](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1551685893448798229).

The agent harness itself altered what was learned and measured. On the same dataset, one comparison reported the minimal SWE-Bench prompt 9 percentage points above an R2E-gym preamble that demanded exploration, a reproduction script, verification, and final review; the longer instructions increased verbosity and timeouts. Another contributor said DAPO RL improved a weak SFT model mainly by teaching the harness, without additional downstream gain once a stronger SFT model already knew it. These are setup-specific observations, but they warn against mistaking harness adaptation for new problem-solving ability. [Prompt-format comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1518700249902743773/1518721396019167444); [weak-SFT interpretation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1513172211991838901/1513464608579653726).

An August harness ablation likewise found no universally best protocol: native tool calling was not automatically superior, longer context helped only weakly in that comparison, and summarization had no reliable positive effect. A prompt-distillation/P2O attempt later underperformed the base model without hyperparameter tuning, while a well-chosen prompt nearly matched an RL checkpoint on the tested tasks. These comparisons show why a model checkpoint and its harness should be evaluated as a pair. [Harness ablation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1539326927628673064); [P2O attempt](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1549571672426807367).

For a later SFT Snowball checkpoint on r2egym, an initial pass@1 of 13% versus pass@8 of 37% suggested learnable headroom; many failed attempts identified the right bug but submitted a patch in a format inherited from earlier SFT. A preliminary 20–30-step RL run raised a different validation readout from 8.7% to 16.6%, mainly by making the agent finish turns earlier, not by visibly improving debugging. The author also flagged a broken KL term and a sampling/loss mismatch associated with entropy collapse, so that short run cannot establish a clean RL capability gain. [Pre-RL behavior probe](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1545315846342189190); [preliminary RL report](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1546613497779982507).

A September token-in/token-out audit showed why high-level trace equality can be misleading. Forty of 64 sampled Snowball trajectories had token-ID mismatches even though the logged `exact_match_fraction` was 1: Harbor passed prompt text to vLLM, which re-tokenized it. A subsequent audit found a Qwen chat-format divergence on most samples because an empty `stop_reason` was rendered inconsistently. Harbor had also failed to pass configured temperature, top-p, and top-k in one runner path. The group moved toward canonical rendering and token-level checks at each boundary; numbers from before those fixes need their specific protocol stated. [Token audit](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1546788165954568223); [chat-format bug](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1546942783942692964); [sampling-parameter bug](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1546637343702913064); [canonical boundary](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1547982974149070919).

Two September proof-of-life tests for on-policy distillation and its multi-teacher variant reportedly reproduced or improved their reference task scores end to end, but ran roughly two to three times slower than one reference setup. Cross-tokenizer training and retaining teacher traces were still open engineering work, so this was a feasibility result rather than a ready replacement for the main agent-RL path. [Distillation tests](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1550519485062971428); [remaining work](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1550520123490705490).

Rollout speed was separately improved with EAGLE-3 speculative decoding: one report measured about 50% speedup with a base draft and 61% with a task-adapted draft against that stack's baseline, after draft-data generation and fine-tuning. The draft reportedly stayed useful for at least 36 RL steps; a later 60-step check appears in [#mtp](mtp.md). The speed result should be kept separate from policy-quality claims and from the one-time GPU cost of building the draft. [Speed and training-cost report](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1548067373070946324).

The recurring lesson is to report the base checkpoint, task distribution, rollout freshness, inference/tokenization protocol, context budget, reward, pass@k, and held-out tasks together. A rising reward curve, lower step count, or better in-domain pass@1 alone did not settle whether a recipe transferred. [Task-selection experiment](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1531644649230438591); [transfer test](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1550202720433086504).
