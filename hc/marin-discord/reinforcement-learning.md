# #reinforcement-learning

Archive coverage: 2025-05-22–2026-09-23 (UTC); 1,852 messages, including 1,010 thread replies. [Open channel in Discord](https://discord.com/channels/1354881461060243556/1374989195109466122).

## From environments to a stable training loop

The initial roadmap separated three requirements: put different RL environments behind a common interface, build TPU training and rollout infrastructure, and support GPU backends. A `MarinEnv` callable did not fit external environments expecting an OpenAI-compatible endpoint, so adapters and hot-swappable inference weights became part of the design. In asynchronous training, rollouts also needed a policy-step identifier to discard stale samples and an environment ID to maintain a deliberate task mixture. [Roadmap](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1377493531018395770); [environment adapter](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1389427590376325131); [replay-buffer design](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1405688723827331264/1405689463878975549).

Algorithm discussion compared synchronous GRPO-style two-sided clipping, asynchronous truncated importance sampling, and KL-regularized policy-improvement formulations. The recommendation was to implement at least the first two for comparison, not to declare a universal winner. Value-based and replay-based methods were also proposed from the wider RL literature, but the cited messages are research suggestions rather than Marin benchmark results. [Algorithm comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1385442628694183996); [value-based proposal](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1388765139213484033); [replay proposal](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1493297286338318406).

The earliest architecture decision was whether training and inference should be separate workers with weight transfers, or combined workers with a custom inference path. The choice depended on profiling weight movement, rollout throughput, and TPU inference features, including log-probability support. A working June 2025 GRPO implementation used group mean and standard deviation for advantages and KL regularization to a static reference policy on Hendrycks MATH. That milestone established a training loop, not that policy freshness and off-policy correction were solved. Participants later argued that robust use of off-policy samples could matter more than small changes to the REINFORCE/GRPO objective because inference and training inevitably diverge in asynchronous and tool-using systems. [Architecture trade-off](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1382624973394284587); [first working loop](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1386751923352371311); [off-policy motivation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1422653231196274821).

vLLM rollout generation entered the RL pipeline in October 2025, with prebuilt regional images to reduce job startup time. Correctness remained open at that milestone. In March 2026, moving a repeatedly crashing Ray setup to on-demand v5p-8 workers produced multiple 500-step tests. Throughput improved from roughly eight minutes per step to three with larger batches and in-flight updates, then to 1.68 minutes with a second sampler. The author still reported unexplained entropy collapse: a stable executor did not imply a stable learning algorithm. [vLLM integration](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1430965818241454254); [stability and throughput report](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1486914443060449320).

## Base models, task selection, and collapse

Early math trials illustrated base-model dependence. Qwen3-1.7B learned a format reward and had better pass@64 than the tested Llama-1B-Instruct; another report got Qwen3-8B to roughly 0.7 on its task while Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct stagnated near 0.43 before failure. These are setup-specific observations, but they show why a weak RL outcome cannot be attributed solely to the algorithm or dataset. [1.7B comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1439700923654864998); [8B report](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1445586750264311929).

Even when pass@64 exceeded pass@1, early math training often showed little gradient signal. A November 2025 investigation suspected many rollout groups had all-zero or all-one reward and proposed logging within-group reward variance, masking truncated completions, and sampling prompts likely to produce informative groups. A contemporaneous Qwen3-8B Math-500 comparison differed sharply across two stacks—about 60% versus 20% in the report—with incomplete, truncated answers among the suspected causes. This was a reason to log actual generations and align environment, chat template, and output limits before interpreting an RL curve as an algorithm result. [Reward-variance investigation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1438965911800385617); [cross-stack discrepancy](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1440512154020216964).

For agent training, dataset quality depended on the starting model and its reachable tasks. One proposal argued that a fixed-data comparison can mislead when an already competent model finds the tasks too easy or a weaker model cannot solve any of them. A controlled R2E-gym experiment filtered 4,500 tasks to about 1,600 with learnable headroom based on the base model's pass@4. The filtered run reached roughly 45 SWE-Bench pass@1 in 60 rather than 120 steps and used 48,000 versus 60,000 total rollouts including filtering, but did not exceed the final performance of the raw pool. Filtering bought efficiency in that setup, not a higher ceiling. [Base-dependent argument](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1518700249902743773/1518725530751602758); [filtering experiment](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1531644649230438591).

Reward and held-out performance were monitored through training because models could collapse after an initial gain. In one SFT-8B/PyMethods sweep, a run with learning rate 4e-6 reached 33% on SWE-Bench-100 at step 20 and then declined; an even higher learning rate also collapsed. In-training evaluation and reward curves moved together in that report, under a shorter, unsummarized harness. The measurement was useful for detecting collapse but was not identical to the final evaluation protocol. [Collapse experiment](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1513172211991838901/1513838512670249102).

Hardware-stable training was not automatically scientifically better. An 8B agent-RL run reproduced on Perlmutter but not on Jupiter under matched settings and seeds. The Perlmutter run showed a gradient/entropy spike that made the policy unusually exploratory and verbose before eventual collapse; those extra attempts helped hard evaluations. Jupiter trained more smoothly yet learned shorter, earlier submissions and worse downstream scores. The author's interpretation was an objective/evaluation mismatch exposed by greater numerical stability, with shaped task reward and sequence-normalized objectives proposed for testing. The hardware mechanism was a hypothesis, not a confirmed causal result. [Cross-machine behavior and proposed ablations](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1512791848882536529).

## Snowball results and transfer limits

A reported Snowball math-RL experiment improved AIME24 from 17.67% to 27%, MATH-500 from 64% to 78%, and OlympiadBench from 12.67% to 20% on the specified checkpoint and recipe. An accompanying pass@k analysis found that successful RL runs often broadened the set of solvable samples as well as raising pass@1; switching to a stronger agentic SFT base raised the apparent ceiling and narrowed the remaining gap. These results should not be extrapolated to every base model or environment. [Math results](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1542681739497832499); [pass@k analysis](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1544006720496599131).

A September 2026 exploratory curriculum study found that strict grade-level ordering was a poor guide to what a model could learn: estimated difficulty could waste rollouts, while the first attempt on a problem provided direct information. Adaptive Thompson sampling on expected batch informativeness did slightly better than naive sampling at matched tokens in the author's Qwen and Snowball tests, but the effect was noisy. Aggressive sampling could also miss easy-looking formatting failures—for example, a model trained to emit boxed answers failed GSM8K instructions without lacking the underlying math skill. The proposed direction was conservative adaptation, not an always-hardest curriculum. [Curriculum experiment and limitations](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1544482297237995591).

The agent-RL stack comparison raised pass@1 on 265 held-out r2egym tasks from 50.1% to 59.8% at eight attempts, with pass@8 moving from 92.1% to 95.1%. The author explicitly listed concurrent Harbor, tooling, and data fixes as confounders, so this was a system comparison rather than an isolated algorithm ablation. A separate Snowball run roughly doubled SWE-Bench-100 pass@1 after r2egym RL but did not improve Terminal-Bench 2. It also made the model submit solutions more often, including wrong ones. Later, SFT followed by RL was reported to add more than eight SWE-Bench points over either stage alone in that setup. The result points toward task-specific environments and testing the SFT-to-RL handoff. [Stack comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1549558259713703986); [transfer test](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1550202720433086504); [SFT-to-RL result](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1552048522403250178).

## Preference optimization beside RL

The channel also carried DPO/SimPO experiments. An initial SimPO trial appeared to score zero on AlpacaEval; after fixing a chat-template issue, the author found it could approach DPO with more tuning of its beta and margin parameters, while output length varied substantially. The posted sweep still showed the particular DPO configuration ahead on reported win-rate metrics, so the claim was not that SimPO unconditionally matched DPO. In February, LoRA+DPO made 70B fine-tuning feasible on a v5p-128 but lagged full fine-tuning on an early AlpacaEval comparison, about 5 versus 15, leading to rank-tuning questions. By May, a broader DPO study reported LoRA matching or slightly exceeding full fine-tuning on the tested metrics with a different, zero-A/Gaussian-B initialization. The sequence illustrates why adapter versus full-tuning conclusions depend on configuration and evaluation; the later report does not retroactively erase the earlier gap. [SimPO correction and sweep](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1465467211228512458); [early LoRA gap](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1470299955145998531); [later DPO report](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1364827114670657616/1507158614127808693).

## Context and protocol effects

Agent success also depended on the conversation renderer. Before RL, more than 90% of one 64k-context Snowball model's r2egym attempts ended in context overflow, with re-fed reasoning traces accounting for over 30% of input tokens. In a 160-plus-task probe, trimming that history did not initially hurt pass@8 but increased turns until attempts again overflowed; it also shifted the model toward less reasoning later in the conversation. A short RL run with dropped history reinforced that behavior. This made simple trace truncation an unsafe general fix, even though a future 260k-context model might change the practical constraint. [Reasoning-history ablation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1547120190758584320).

The agent harness itself altered what was learned and measured. On the same dataset, one comparison reported the minimal SWE-Bench prompt 9 percentage points above an R2E-gym preamble that demanded exploration, a reproduction script, verification, and final review; the longer instructions increased verbosity and timeouts. Another contributor said DAPO RL improved a weak SFT model mainly by teaching the harness, without additional downstream gain once a stronger SFT model already knew it. These are setup-specific observations, but they warn against mistaking harness adaptation for new problem-solving ability. [Prompt-format comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1518700249902743773/1518721396019167444); [weak-SFT interpretation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1513172211991838901/1513464608579653726).

For a later SFT Snowball checkpoint on r2egym, an initial pass@1 of 13% versus pass@8 of 37% suggested learnable headroom; many failed attempts identified the right bug but submitted a patch in a format inherited from earlier SFT. A preliminary 20–30-step RL run raised a different validation readout from 8.7% to 16.6%, mainly by making the agent finish turns earlier, not by visibly improving debugging. The author also flagged a broken KL term and a sampling/loss mismatch associated with entropy collapse, so that short run cannot establish a clean RL capability gain. [Pre-RL behavior probe](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1545315846342189190); [preliminary RL report](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1546613497779982507).

Rollout speed was separately improved with EAGLE-3 speculative decoding: one report measured about 50% speedup with a base draft and 61% with a task-adapted draft against that stack's baseline, after draft-data generation and fine-tuning. The draft reportedly stayed useful for at least 36 RL steps; a later 60-step check appears in [#mtp](mtp.md). The speed result should be kept separate from policy-quality claims and from the one-time GPU cost of building the draft. [Speed and training-cost report](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1548067373070946324).

The recurring lesson is to report the base checkpoint, task distribution, rollout freshness, inference/tokenization protocol, context budget, reward, pass@k, and held-out tasks together. A rising reward curve, lower step count, or better in-domain pass@1 alone did not settle whether a recipe transferred. [Task-selection experiment](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1531644649230438591); [transfer test](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1374989195109466122/1550202720433086504).
