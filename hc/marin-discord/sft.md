## Reasoning format and teacher data

Early SFT discussions separated teaching a model to produce reasoning from showing reasoning text in a dataset. The group considered reserving single-token thinking delimiters and suppressing them at generation time for benchmarks that should not expose internal reasoning. Tests of a Nemotron/OpenThoughts SFT model showed that applying the chat template was essential: training examples could elicit a thinking delimiter, while generic prompts did not necessarily do so. Short generation caps could hide the closing delimiter. A subsequent AIME trace looked locally sensible but ended with an unsupported heuristic, showing why plausible prose is not the same as a solved problem. [Thinking-token proposal](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1355318637854199848/1377753038110068829) · [Template observation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1405655976832532560/1405993542245154938) · [Trace inspection](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1355318637854199848/1428086596481191997).

Teacher selection was not simply “use the strongest teacher.” OpenThoughts contributors reported that QwQ-32B, despite being weaker than R1 as a model, had worked better for their dataset generation in one comparison. With the larger OpenThoughts4 candidate, annotation expense motivated experiments to predict useful teachers before paying to label the full collection; Qwen3-32B served as an interim default teacher. This remains a data-generation hypothesis to test, not a universal teacher ranking. [Teacher comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1433664962903146609/1433671573293764690) · [OT4 plan](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1355318637854199848/1481494687901548625).

## Training trajectories and fair comparisons

A Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct experiment compared a mixed reasoning dataset with OpenThoughts3 alone. AIME accuracy varied substantially by checkpoint; the team had not replicated OpenThoughts3's reported AIME24 result, and OT3-only looked slightly better in that initial run. That called for evaluation-pipeline checks and more trials rather than a conclusion that extra data was harmful. [Comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1355318637854199848/1440255448774348880).

An initial final-checkpoint comparison reported 20.0% AIME24 and 13.3% AIME25 for a long-context-extended Marin 8B fine-tune, versus 53.3% on both for Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct. The hyperparameters were tuned for Qwen, so those numbers were a baseline, not Marin's best achievable result. More intermediate checkpoints and trials later showed Marin was closer earlier in training and declined in the second half; learning-rate decay and overtraining were hypotheses, not proven causes. The practical lesson is to retain checkpoint trajectories and tune each base model rather than compare only endpoint scores. [Initial result](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1355318637854199848/1458203611543900190) · [Reevaluation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1355318637854199848/1465858152133169182).

By August 2026, a staged cold-start pipeline for cooled-down 67B-A2B checkpoints separated chat, thinking, and agentic SFT. Its reported best checkpoints reached 89% GSM8K zero-shot, 78% MATH500, and 38% AIME24; an agentic comparison reported 20.4% on OT-TBLite for one Snowball checkpoint versus 23.0% for Qwen3 8B on the same SFT data. These are checkpoint- and protocol-specific measurements, not a blanket quality rank. [Pipeline report](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1355318637854199848/1538575722598105149).

The latest discussions explored how to initialize newly introduced chat-template tokens and whether rank-surprisal statistics could predict which teacher trajectories will be useful before a full SFT run. The latter showed promising ranking agreement in small reported comparisons, but its trajectory-selection experiment was still being evaluated. [Special-token question](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1355318637854199848/1549961026949353582) · [Teacher-data proxy](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1355318637854199848/1552085529741238394).
