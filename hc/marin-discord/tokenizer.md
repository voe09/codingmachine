# #tokenizer

Archive coverage: 2026-05-04–2026-09-18 (UTC); 127 messages, including 21 thread replies. [Open channel in Discord](https://discord.com/channels/1354881461060243556/1500987824206254120).

## What the tokenizer objective should measure

Tokenizer work began from observed gaps in bits per byte, including unusual whitespace and long ideographic-space sequences that might reflect poor multilingual coverage. The proposed diagnostic suite compared document-length distributions, distinct whitespace tokens, and model/tokenizer pairs on targeted evaluation sets. The group also noted that byte compression is not the only objective: vocabulary choices impose an inductive bias on semantics and affect long-context cost, numerical text, and interoperability with existing model families. [Whitespace concern](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1500987824206254120/1500989687257366640) · [Diagnostic plan](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1500987824206254120/1501012177417539684) · [Beyond compression](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1500987824206254120/1538013493180112986).

Vocabulary size had a direct parameter cost in the small-model ladder. For a few-hundred-million-parameter model, a 128k-token embedding and output matrix could dominate the budget; the models under discussion used widths of 512, 768, and 1024. That motivated 8k, 32k, and larger vocabulary comparisons at matched model scale, rather than optimizing compression alone. One 32k candidate was made by dropping infrequent merges from a 128k Marin tokenizer. Early throughput discussion also showed why rates need a denominator: a reported aggregate of about 0.6M tokens/s across 19 workers was not a single-CPU rate; later single-CPU measurements exceeded 0.8M tokens/s while strong scaling remained unresolved. [Small-model budget](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1500987824206254120/1507584746488725614); [ladder widths](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1500987824206254120/1509048390909034597); [32k construction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1500987824206254120/1507516633684512799); [throughput clarification](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1500987824206254120/1507514736844079327); [single-CPU rate](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1500987824206254120/1507531411748552865).

Large training-corpus jobs compared Llama, GPT-OSS, and initially Tokenmonster-style vocabularies at several sizes. Tokenization took days until checkpointing was added; 100B-token tokenizer-training attempts also ran out of memory, while 50B-token subsets were feasible. Tokenmonster training itself proved too slow for this sweep and was dropped. The group noted that the 50B subset needed adequate shuffling. Its source pool mixed web, books, code, math, and synthetic data, but a contributor corrected a description of the larger 1.1T sample: it was normalized, not deduplicated. That affects how representative both tokenizer training and held-out validation are. [Training setup](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1500987824206254120/1511886225622171719); [50B feasibility](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1500987824206254120/1513673864616480778); [shuffling caveat](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1500987824206254120/1513700869810684044); [source mix](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1500987824206254120/1516207744225513603); [deduplication correction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1500987824206254120/1516234212724969642).

Numeric variants explored right-to-left digit tokens, place-value alignment, and a cap on pathological long digit strings. An early comparison suggested GPT-OSS-style tokenization benefited while the Llama variant did not, but a later audit found that the wrong model size had been passed for digit variants. That could account for an apparent bits-per-byte advantage of about 0.03 in some runs, so the experiment needed a matched rerun before claiming a numeric-tokenization benefit. The team also questioned whether standard held-out sets such as C4 or Paloma had leaked into the source mixture and proposed decontaminated evaluation data. [Numeric design](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1500987824206254120/1514778482620629074); [early comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1500987824206254120/1518833471449989170); [model-size confounder](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1500987824206254120/1525252850408620142); [held-out concern](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1500987824206254120/1516236855023108226).

## Teacher compatibility and corpus coverage

Sharing a tokenizer with a strong teacher could simplify on-policy distillation and pass@k forecasting. GPT-OSS was proposed partly for that reason; GLM was mentioned as another candidate. A response cautioned against making shared vocabulary a prerequisite: for ordinary text in one Llama/Qwen comparison, the author recalled that over 98% of tokens had direct counterparts across tokenizers, and the forecasting method did not fundamentally require identical vocabularies. Math and code might differ, and the cross-tokenizer forecast was still awaiting experimental validation. The channel also proposed comparing language- and code-specific vocabulary coverage, because two tokenizers can both encode text while allocating merges differently. [Teacher-alignment argument](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1500987824206254120/1523812385544995101); [distillation reason](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1500987824206254120/1523812430008811540); [cross-tokenizer observation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1500987824206254120/1524293725944021073); [math/code caveat](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1500987824206254120/1524294943575642244); [coverage plan](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1500987824206254120/1524304326267375687).

A whole-corpus Llama-3 versus Tekken comparison held vocabulary size fixed and reported Tekken emitting more tokens on code, worsening aggregate compression on the tested mix. A participant argued that semantic coverage and long-context behavior also matter; the thread did not show a controlled downstream training comparison. The corpus analysis used embeddings to cluster roughly 14B documents, with an LLM naming clusters from examples. Those cluster names and third-party “high quality” labels are descriptive aids, not independent quality measurements by Marin. [Fixed-size comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1500987824206254120/1537944059111407716); [code compression](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1500987824206254120/1537944427463450624); [non-compression criteria](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1500987824206254120/1538013493180112986); [cluster method](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1538014315083202590/1538036966845583360); [document count](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1538014315083202590/1538037252108718171).

A later BPE sweep on the hero pretraining mixture found small compression gains beyond 16k vocabulary entries. After a question about whether the test text had appeared in tokenizer training, the author repeated the comparison on 150,000 new documents and reported a 12.5% rather than 11% gap in the metric under discussion. The visible message does not specify the exact denominator for that gap, so this page does not convert it into a universal savings claim. Larger vocabularies still cost more output-layer parameters at small model scales, while multilingual and agentic behavior may depend on coverage and merge rules that compression alone misses. The channel did not establish 16k as a universal optimum.
