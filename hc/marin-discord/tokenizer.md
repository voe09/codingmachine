## What the tokenizer objective should measure

Tokenizer work began from observed gaps in bits per byte, including unusual whitespace and long ideographic-space sequences that might reflect poor multilingual coverage. The proposed diagnostic suite compared document-length distributions, distinct whitespace tokens, and model/tokenizer pairs on targeted evaluation sets. The group also noted that byte compression is not the only objective: vocabulary choices impose an inductive bias on semantics and affect long-context cost, numerical text, and interoperability with existing model families. [Whitespace concern](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1500987824206254120/1500989687257366640) · [Diagnostic plan](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1500987824206254120/1501012177417539684) · [Beyond compression](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1500987824206254120/1538013493180112986).

Large training-corpus jobs compared Llama, GPT-OSS, and Tokenmonster-style vocabularies at several sizes. Tokenization itself took days until checkpointing was added. Numeric variants explored right-to-left digit tokens, place-value alignment, and a cap on pathological long digit strings. An apparent bits-per-byte gain of up to about 0.03 for some digit variants was confounded by passing the wrong model size, so the experiments had to be rerun on a clean comparable configuration. [Training setup](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1500987824206254120/1511886225622171719) · [Numeric design](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1500987824206254120/1514778482620629074) · [Confounder](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1500987824206254120/1525252850408620142).

A later BPE sweep on the hero pretraining mixture found surprisingly small compression gains beyond 16k vocabulary entries. The cost of a larger output vocabulary at smaller model scales must be weighed against any downstream or multilingual benefit; the channel did not establish 16k as a universal optimum.
