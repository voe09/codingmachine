## Why serving and training needed separate validation

The initial goal was a usable Levanter inference path, potentially using JAX ragged paged attention for mixed prefill and decode, without assuming Levanter itself had to become a production-grade server. As that path matured, the group found that a model which trained correctly could still differ at inference because of its attention kernel, layer-folding strategy, padding, and prompt construction. Numerical differences between trainer dot-product attention and ragged paged attention, plus differences between folded and sequential layers, prompted parity tests before blaming the weights or training recipe. [Initial design](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1387192869286056037) · [Numerical comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1437252801750106185).

Early model support depended on TPU kernel shapes. One serving path required head dimensions divisible by 128, so an 8B model with 128-dimensional heads worked while a 1B model with 64-dimensional heads did not. Later weight-transfer tests covered Llama 3.2 1B and Qwen3 0.6B base and instruct variants, checking greedy output before and after transfer to vLLM; padding the Llama head dimension was part of the fix. The reusable lesson is to test *conversion plus generation*, not only whether a checkpoint loads. [Shape diagnosis](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1420198905940934686) · [Transfer tests](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1430075252901417035).

Evaluation correctness was equally sensitive to the serving wrapper. A stop-sequence padding bug was fixed; chat-template and few-shot-multiturn options were exposed to the evaluation harness; generated prompts and outputs were saved for inspection. With comparable setup, the team reproduced Llama 3.1 8B Instruct's GSM8K chain-of-thought result at 85.6 versus a reported 84.5. That is a validation of one protocol, not a guarantee that all benchmark invocations are equivalent. [Harness result](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1420612223822200934).

## Throughput, capacity, and engine choices

One controlled benchmark used 1,024 input and 1,024 output tokens with 256 concurrent requests. It reported 1,000 tokens/s for Levanter, 2,630 for vLLM in eager mode, and 5,427 for standard vLLM—about 2.6× and 5.4× the Levanter throughput respectively. The prior short summary incorrectly attached the 2.6× ratio to the 5,427 number. These figures are tied to that hardware, model, concurrency, and benchmark driver. Other posts reported faster Levanter batches under different conditions, reinforcing that isolated throughput figures should not be compared across workloads. [Controlled comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1427163698522488832) · [Separate Levanter observation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1418488013872304128).

The team investigated page-table creation, buffer reuse, ragged-attention VMEM limits, and compilation cost as performance and capacity bottlenecks. A tuned SGLang-JAX experiment remained about 26% slower than out-of-box vLLM-TPU in its reported setup, compared with 79% slower before tuning. That made further tuning possible but not obviously worth the engineering cost. [Buffer reuse](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1425229496214356001) · [VMEM failure](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1426986942838607882) · [SGLang-JAX comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1436417272959733872).

By March 2026, multi-host vLLM on a v5p-16 was serving Qwen3-235B-A22B with roughly 1,500 generated tokens/s at a reported peak, but required Ray again for that deployment. Later work separated generic vLLM scheduling and KV-cache logic from the TPU-specific kernels and sharding in `tpu-inference`; a Marin fork was driven partly by package/dependency constraints, not by a need to rewrite the generic engine. The group discussed a shared distributed inference service and an OpenAI-compatible boundary to make engines replaceable. [Multi-host test](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1483286422806069319) · [Fork rationale](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1503181528979603558/1503198478715588730) · [Service proposal](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1504212410381111326) · [Interface discussion](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1511034914059976784/1511447100884516936).

For RL and MoE, a plain text-generation API may not expose enough: router replay and other inference internals can affect the validity of training updates. The group also examined per-request seeding in vLLM-TPU, where a model-level seed is not the same as independently controlled completion seeds. Later GPU vLLM support for the Grug/hero architecture was reported as landed but still lightly tested; reports explicitly asked for reproducible correctness or performance issues. [Router-information discussion](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1511034914059976784/1511447100884516936) · [Seeding analysis](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1498818086512623767) · [GPU serving status](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1543333342529851484).
