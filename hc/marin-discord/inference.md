## Why serving and training needed separate validation

The initial goal was a usable Levanter inference path, potentially using JAX ragged paged attention, a page-managed KV cache, and mixed prefill/decode batching, without assuming Levanter itself had to become a production-grade server. The team also considered EasyDeL VSurge, MaxText/JetStream, and vLLM, primarily for TPU-based evaluation and RL rollouts. As the Levanter path matured, the group found that a model which trained correctly could still differ at inference because of its attention kernel, layer-folding strategy, padding, and prompt construction. Numerical differences between trainer dot-product attention and ragged paged attention, plus differences between folded and sequential layers, prompted parity tests before blaming the weights or training recipe. [Initial design](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1387192869286056037) · [JAX design](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1388305440521322726) · [Numerical comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1437252801750106185).

Early model support depended on TPU kernel shapes. One optimized serving path required head dimensions divisible by 128, so an 8B model with 128-dimensional heads used it while a 1B model with 64-dimensional heads fell back. Refactoring the kernel produced suspicious outputs, requiring correctness checks against a simpler path. An apparent 1B throughput disaster was later traced to a run without `--extra=tpu`: it was executing on CPU, not demonstrating a TPU regression. Later weight-transfer tests covered Llama 3.2 1B and Qwen3 0.6B base and instruct variants, checking greedy output before and after transfer to vLLM; padding the Llama head dimension was part of the fix. The reusable lesson is to test *conversion plus generation*, not only whether a checkpoint loads. [Shape diagnosis](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1420198905940934686) · [Kernel concern](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1420220525212336148) · [CPU correction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1420630585340071956) · [Transfer tests](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1430075252901417035).

Evaluation correctness was equally sensitive to the serving wrapper. The team distinguished non-generative HellaSwag from generative GSM8K, fixed a stop-sequence padding bug, exposed chat-template and few-shot-multiturn options to the harness, and saved generated prompts and outputs for inspection. With comparable setup, they reproduced Llama 3.1 8B Instruct's GSM8K chain-of-thought result at 85.6 versus a reported 84.5. That validates one protocol, not all benchmark invocations. [Harness result](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1420612223822200934).

## Cache, capacity, and numerical failure modes

Several apparent model failures were resource or state-management failures. The engine needed enough KV pages for active sequences and decode steps; running out could strand requests. A service-side admission check did not protect direct engine calls, pointing toward engine-level rejection or rebatching. In October, increasing `InferenceEngineConfig` limits made silent corruption disappear, but the author explicitly called that a workaround pending a proper error. A small configuration also produced a zero-token eval loop. [Page exhaustion](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1420553373937176621) · [Engine concern](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1420589488610082906) · [Silent-corruption workaround](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1423083816477524050) · [Zero-token loop](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1425647561494237265).

XLA buffer aliasing caused avoidable KV-cache copies. A workaround made one unspecified workload roughly six times faster, but the author expected a deeper cache redesign. A separate 32B run exhausted the Pallas kernel's *scoped VMEM* by about 164 KiB, prompting suggestions to tune the limit or reduce block sizes. HBM capacity, scoped VMEM, and excess buffer copies are different bottlenecks and need different remedies. [Buffer investigation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1425229496214356001) · [Sixfold result](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1425876480075763763) · [Scoped VMEM failure](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1426986942838607882).

November parity experiments isolated ragged paged attention and `layers.fold` as separate sources of trainer–inference logit differences. Vanilla attention without folding matched trainer logits in that test. Matching attention-operation order reduced one comparison's relative tolerance from `1e-2` to `1e-3`, though the team planned further testing. Folding promised speed but a folded decode path had OOM problems; JAX array references were proposed to update the cache without copying. [Parity isolation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1437252801750106185/1437317483475238983) · [Operation-order result](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1437252801750106185/1437610407358627952) · [ArrayRef approach](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1437252801750106185/1437510016134549809).

The issue remained open in May 2026: one comparison found vLLM-TPU log probabilities about 0.5 nats from Hugging Face CPU, while Levanter TPU was closer to the CPU result. Participants considered floating-point order and attention implementations but did not establish the cause; CPU versus TPU is itself a confound. A June experiment found a maximum log-probability difference around 0.159 from scan-versus-non-scan layer lowering. These numbers motivate model-specific golden tests and tolerance analysis, not a universal verdict on one engine. [May comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1501700540050968687) · [Unresolved discussion](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1501702728207044819) · [June scan result](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1511035565527531633).

## Throughput, capacity, and engine choices

Early throughput reports used different models, TP layouts, hardware, prompt distributions, and token lengths. The team therefore built a common load generator. One October benchmark used Llama 3.1 8B on the same v4-8, TP=4, 1,024 input and 1,024 output tokens, and 256 concurrent requests. It reported 1,000 tokens/s for Levanter, 2,630 for vLLM in eager mode, and 5,427 for standard vLLM—about 2.6× and 5.4× the Levanter throughput respectively. The original post called the gap 2.6×, which corresponds to eager mode, not its standard vLLM result. EasyDeL initially reached only 65 tokens/s in a setup suspected to be misconfigured, so that was not accepted as a platform comparison. [Controlled comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1427163698522488832) · [EasyDeL caveat](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1427163912658227282).

Warmup and load changed the result: with 1,000 requests, Levanter rose from 1,000 to 1,195 tokens/s and one vLLM measurement from 2,630 to 4,304. Inputs were independently randomized; disabling prefix caching did not explain vLLM's gain. Levanter's OpenAI-compatible server had an untuned batching window and safety debatching, potential additional confounds. The durable method is to match prompt/output distributions, warm up compilation, record batching and cache settings, and distinguish total from output tokens. [Longer-run result](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1427191427422617692) · [Cache check](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1427197733340581893) · [Batching caveat](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1427344967306580131).

A tuned SGLang-JAX experiment remained about 26% slower than out-of-box vLLM-TPU in its reported setup, compared with 79% slower before tuning. A contributor argued SGLang was not tuned for that TPU v4 setup and suggested v6e, but quota limited follow-up. The observation informed a local engine choice, not a universal ranking across hardware generations. [SGLang-JAX comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1436417272959733872) · [Hardware caveat](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1437148886354366505).

The engine choice did not remain one-way. In June 2026, an optimization pass on Levanter inference was reported to make it roughly ten times faster and within about 12% of vLLM-TPU on its workload. The channel does not provide a matched benchmark table establishing broad parity; participants requested MoE benchmarking and review of the optimization PRs. Engine choice remained a tradeoff among speed, startup, model coverage, maintenance, and access to internal signals such as MoE routing. [Levanter update](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1511034914059976784) · [Verification request](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1511034914059976784/1511440935672746025) · [Ownership discussion](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1511034914059976784/1511435851207282718).

## vLLM integration and deployment

The October benchmark shifted effort toward a vLLM OpenAI server and weight sync from Levanter training. The proposed route served Levanter-format weights via Arrow Flight, converted them to NNX state with model-specific key mappings and transposes on the rollout worker, then called the vLLM runner's sync function. Tests for Llama 3.2 1B and Qwen3 0.6B base/instruct checked greedy generation before and after transfer, not merely tensor shapes. They passed locally on v4-8, but the author had not yet tested the Marin/Ray path. [Integration split](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1429156354630881280) · [Weight-sync architecture](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1430039507188584539) · [Transfer-test scope](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1430075252901417035).

Version skew repeatedly masqueraded as inference failure. An October Llama 1B issue first looked like tokenization, mesh, or JAX behavior, but comparing Haliax versions and testing a fix established a tied-weight muP bug. A JAX 0.7.1 change separately altered how an empty mesh appeared to Levanter's `best_effort_sharding`, breaking a `None` check during safetensor loading. In February, vLLM jobs became unreliable during runtime-environment setup; the channel treated import/dependency causality as a working hypothesis, not a resolved diagnosis. [Tied-weight diagnosis](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1425701813436551230) · [Mesh change](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1429669941241708596) · [February uncertainty](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1468083647264329882).

Multi-host capability evolved. In October 2025 the team believed vLLM-TPU lacked the needed route, and a December v4-64 test did not find a working configuration. A Levanter multi-host attempt in February 2026 handled a few prompts, then slowed and OOMed, with KV-page cleanup suspected. By March, vLLM-TPU served Qwen3-235B-A22B on v5p-16 and reported about 1,500 generated tokens/s at peak with 256 active requests and substantial prefix-cache hits. That is a working reported case, not proof for every topology; it used Ray internally. [Earlier limitation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1430221799139840091/1430344327678132244) · [Levanter attempt](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1468101378487488664) · [March vLLM test](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1483286422806069319).

Iris containers made native per-job dependency installation possible, weakening the rationale for an older Docker sidecar. The fork debate distinguished generic vLLM scheduling and KV abstractions from TPU kernels, sharding, and features in `tpu-inference`. A vLLM fork was partly needed because its distributed package pulled CUDA dependencies on TPU, not because Marin needed to rewrite the generic engine. To manage drift, they proposed rebasing paired forks onto known upstream releases, smoke-testing, then updating Marin pins; the channel records the plan, not completion of every proposed upgrade. [Iris-native discussion](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1485493178382422046/1485495048702394398) · [Fork rationale](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1503181528979603558/1503198478715588730) · [Release process](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1511835505308733641).

## Sampling semantics and distributed service boundaries

An April 2026 report of identical generations despite seed and temperature changes first prompted concern that vLLM-TPU did not support per-request seeds and that RL rollouts had used a common model-level seed. The thread then identified the immediate explanation for the identical completions: the old pinned `vllm_tpu` bundle still had a default top-k that made sampling greedy, despite an upstream fix from January. The author said this wasted TPU time generating duplicate math completions. These are related but distinct issues: per-request seed control remained a capability question; top-k was the diagnosed cause of temperature-insensitive repeated output in that run. Test the *effective* sampler configuration, not just the requested temperature and seed. [Seed analysis](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1498818086512623767) · [Top-k diagnosis](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1498816946156601454/1498818238627319899) · [Stale package](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1498819516459782247) · [Duplicate generations](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1498819526845009981).

For distributed evaluation, a May proposal put an OpenAI-compatible local Iris actor in front of a queue of preemptible TPU workers, each running vLLM. That could make an evaluator look local while spreading generation across machines or regions; possible later consumers included RL rollouts and synthetic data. The discussion links to a separate design document but does not establish that the service was deployed. An OpenAI-compatible boundary makes engines more replaceable, but RL/MoE methods needing router replay or other inference internals may require richer responses and tighter trainer–sampler parity than plain text generation provides. [Service concept](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1504212410381111326/1504220284335689830) · [Scope caveat](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1504212410381111326/1504220701492514919) · [RL interface tradeoff](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1511034914059976784/1511447100884516936).

By July and August, the group was also serving Grug/hero-family models on GPU vLLM. A first Grug 67B-A2B path landed with acknowledged rough edges; RL still depended on aligning its serving fork and a compatible training model. Later H100 support for a hero architecture landed after testing on a large ladder checkpoint, with an explicit warning that correctness and performance coverage was limited. These reports are useful starting points, not production guarantees. [Grug v0](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1526388201915289642) · [RL dependency](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1526394430637080576) · [Hero serving status](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385733711013871729/1543333342529851484).
