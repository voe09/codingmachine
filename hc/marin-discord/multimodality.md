## Audio as ordinary next-token prediction

The first audio experiment used a pretrained **Mimi audio tokenizer** to convert speech into discrete codes, then trained a Marin transformer with the existing next-token-prediction stack. That made an audio baseline cheap to build: no new continuous encoder or custom training objective was needed. The initial result was described as surprisingly workable despite concerns about the YODAS source data, but the channel's early message did not give a numeric metric. This was a baseline for audio-token modeling, not a claim of a full “omni” model. [Workflow](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1435048655894872248/1435049636371890258) · [Initial assessment](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1435048655894872248/1435049776625221692) · [Scope caveat](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1435048655894872248/1435054077871657051).

The work grew into the **SODA discrete-audio study**: interleaved speech and text tokens supported speech continuation, text continuation, ASR, and TTS through the same prediction objective. Its author reported experiments on speech/text mixture, token type, warm versus cold initialization, ISO-FLOP scaling, and whether validation loss predicted downstream tasks. Longer runs ranged from 135M to 4B parameters over 500B training tokens. They reported publishing an experiment log, checkpoints, and roughly 900,000 hours of tokenized multilingual speech (about 700B tokens), while using an English subset of about 350B tokens. These numbers describe released study scope, not performance results; detailed measurements are in linked external artifacts and were not inferred from Discord alone. [Study design](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1435048655894872248/1474067712186777839) · [Scale and released artifacts](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1435048655894872248/1474068015988605203) · [Project page](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1435048655894872248/1474068513386922044).

## Why discrete tokens may not be the final architecture

The audio researcher identified two limitations of the flattened discrete stream. For **input**, continuous audio features may preserve fine-grained acoustic information that a tokenizer discards; a cited external system reportedly added a continuous encoder during pretraining for that reason. For **output**, flattening multiple audio codebooks into one long sequence makes generation slow. Other designs have the main LLM predict the first codebook token per time step and a smaller secondary module predict the rest. Both alternatives would require more specialized infrastructure than Marin's baseline. The channel did not contain a head-to-head Marin test proving either alternative better. [Input/output limitations](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1435048655894872248/1435355611612057754).

The same tradeoff shaped possible vision work. A discrete image tokenizer could reuse much of the text/audio stack and serve as a fast baseline; a continuous vision encoder might be stronger on understanding but would introduce significant systems work. Contributors pointed to discrete-token research and vision datasets, while expressing a long-term interest in a unified model. No completed Marin vision result appears in this channel.
