# #scaling-suite

Archive coverage: 2025-05-22–2026-06-10 (UTC); 187 messages, including 123 thread replies. [Open channel in Discord](https://discord.com/channels/1354881461060243556/1375005693899309126).

## Why build a suite instead of one large run?

The suite began as a proposal for a modern Pythia-like collection of model sizes and intermediate checkpoints. That would let researchers study when behaviors emerge during training, not only compare final losses. Participants also wanted to test whether the compute-optimal model/data allocation held across tasks. The design trade-off was between equal-token trajectories, useful for comparing behavior at a common training position, and isoFLOP runs, useful for predicting the best loss under a compute budget. The exact ladder depended on which downstream question it should answer. [Original proposal](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1375005693899309126/1375005846852997201); [compute-optimality question](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1375005693899309126/1375703378000347209); [checkpoint/size motivation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1375005693899309126/1375708024882204773).

One January discussion pushed beyond loss prediction toward forecasting social and behavioral properties before a model finished training. A cited dialect-bias analysis found some English-dialect gaps forecast to shrink with scale while others grew. The participants explicitly noted that this example did not prove an equal-token suite could not support the analysis; schedule choice, especially cosine versus warmup-stable-decay cooldowns, was a possible confounder. This was a motivation for a more informative suite, not an experimental result produced by the suite itself. [Forecasting goal](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1466117938359697460/1466131905505394751); [dialect result](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1466117938359697460/1466133989420040303); [design caveat](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1466117938359697460/1466131720553365569).

## Forecasts, calibration, and the loss floor

The Delphi runs recorded Paloma macro-loss forecasts before the larger evaluations. The 1e21-FLOP run finished 0.0002 loss from its forecast. The 1e22 run reached about 2.53 versus a forecast near 2.55 despite transient loss spikes. This 0.02 difference prompted a discussion of uncertainty: over roughly two orders of magnitude of extrapolation, it was within the reported 95% bootstrap interval and was an *overestimate* of loss, useful for conservative compute planning. Forecast quality should be judged against an uncertainty interval and the decision it informs, not only a rounded point error. [Pre-registration](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1375005693899309126/1478447834943586428); [1e21 result](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1375005693899309126/1479166845788356760); [1e22 result](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1375005693899309126/1479862333739372707); [uncertainty interpretation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1375005693899309126/1481304884547551512).

The floor/asymptote in the fitted loss curve was a substantive modeling choice. An initial fixed value near 1.9 came from earlier DCLM-based work; its author warned that the same floor need not transfer to a different data mix. Allowing the floor to vary sometimes produced very different estimates across optimizers, and one free-floor forecast for an AdamW series underestimated loss by about 0.1. At 1e23 scale, the fixed-floor forecast appeared pessimistic and a learned-floor forecast optimistic, with the observed cooldown trend between them. The team leaned toward conservative forecasts for resource commitments, while continuing to compare fits. The channel did not establish a universal loss floor. [Source-data caveat](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1479862333739372707/1480320651079454812); [free-fit instability](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1479862333739372707/1480613642029371564); [AdamW example](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1480896428015489085/1481049531134251070); [1e23 comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1375005693899309126/1487556665875107870).

## Loss spikes and data inspection

The 1e22 trace had spikes whose batches contained a wall of digits and a repeatedly emitted citation error. A later spike included a firmware hex dump and repetitive generated HTML from StarCoder. Those examples made data quality a plausible cause, but hex dumps were common in that source, so finding one in a spike batch did not prove it caused the spike. Other participants suggested attention-path sensitivity and asked why similar data in Common Pile did not show the same pattern. AdamH appeared to stabilize some larger runs on the same data, but the discussion did not isolate optimizer, architecture, and data effects. [First suspect batch](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1375005693899309126/1479206554124292146); [later batch](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1375005693899309126/1479363883813830717); [hex-dump caveat](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1375005693899309126/1479368677273894912); [attention hypothesis](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1479206554124292146/1479245271169896469); [optimizer observation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1375005693899309126/1478433205651505347).

The practical follow-up was to add metrics for suspect samples to a data viewer, then consider a filtering pipeline. The author said that filtering pipeline did not yet exist in March 2026. The 1e23 run continued despite spikes because it was preregistered; the point was to learn whether those spikes would break the forecast rather than quietly discard an inconvenient run. A conjecture that small-scale signals might predict later spikes remained untested in the cited exchange. [Data-viewer step](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1375005693899309126/1481672627390124224); [continue-run decision](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1375005693899309126/1483907687862370470); [untested conjecture](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1375005693899309126/1488577736321667133).

## Comparing recipes without mixing checkpoints

Nemotron was chosen over Common Pile for the larger Delphi ladder because it had produced higher numbers on benchmarks of interest and had prior Marin training experience. The channel says isoFLOP runs existed for Common Pile, Nemotron, and Dolma 3, but compute did not cover the large end of each ladder; additional random seeds were prioritized to estimate variance and preserve Pythia-like utility. One attempted comparison accidentally used an older Comma/Common Pile sweep with a different training recipe, so it could not be interpreted as a pure dataset ablation. Another midtraining attempt selected a similarly named 3e20 checkpoint from a different scaling recipe; participants pointed to the pinned suite definition and model collection to identify the intended run. Loss comparisons require the data mix, optimizer, token-scaling factor, and checkpoint identity together. [Nemotron choice](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1502118389370589337/1502119018385899570); [existing isoFLOPs](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1502118389370589337/1502119775462101093); [compute/seed trade-off](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1502118389370589337/1502120258641723472); [recipe mismatch](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1502118389370589337/1506775968432521319); [checkpoint correction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1504287020292505701/1504292004874682448).

The final thread questioned why Delphi's learning-rate scaling looked closer to `T^-0.3` than `T^-0.5`. Participants cited both empirical and theoretical arguments for different exponents and noted that the optimum learning-rate basin becomes shallow at larger scales. No controlled comparison in this channel resolved whether the exponent transfers across optimizers or tasks.
