## Comparing optimizers under a real training budget

The channel compared Adam-family variants, Muon, PSGD, and normalized-update ideas, but repeatedly returned to the need for matched wall-clock, loss, and scaling tests. An early report found Muon ahead of alternatives tried in wall-clock time; a new PSGD formulation then appeared promising in that contributor's tests. These were candidate methods for Speedrun sweeps, not a settled universal ranking. AdamC later showed a marginal Speedrun benefit and was used in stability-focused larger runs. [Muon/PSGD discussion](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1366632114316906506/1374184575772790824) · [AdamC observation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1382240679765217342/1389315980815110375).

Architecture and numerical details can change which stability mechanism is practical. A discussed alternative to QK normalization addressed a similar problem but avoided its performance cost with multi-head latent attention. Projections or clipping in weight/update space were considered for Muon- and PSGD-like methods, but the archived posts are proposals rather than controlled Marin ablations. [QK-norm tradeoff](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1382240679765217342/1397621229942210720) · [Projection idea](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1382240679765217342/1402434258022170728).

The hyperball experiments highlighted why copying an Adam learning rate is unsafe: update normalization changes what the learning-rate number means. A corrected rate made small Speedrun experiments look smooth, but a trial with bfloat16 *parameters* regressed relative to Marin's usual float32 parameters with bfloat16 computation/output. Rounding or TPU-specific behavior was suspected, not established. A follow-up noted that the Adam-to-hyperball rate ratio differed between DCLM and FineWeb-Edu, prompting a call to recheck on the new data mix. Optimizer tuning depends on precision, dataset, and architecture as well as the update formula.
