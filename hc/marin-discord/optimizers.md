# #optimizers

Archive coverage: 2025-06-11–2026-08-31 (UTC); 282 messages, including 58 thread replies. [Open channel in Discord](https://discord.com/channels/1354881461060243556/1382240679765217342).

## Compare loss, tokens, and wall-clock together

Early Speedrun sweeps compared Adam-family methods, Muon, Caution, and PSGD variants. AdamC's corrected weight decay gave a marginal benefit under Marin's shorter runs and smaller tuned weight decay; the group did not observe the large gain one might infer from a paper using a different setup. They also questioned whether an external sign-SOAP comparison had applied weight exponential averaging only to the new optimizer, a confounder rather than a verified flaw in that paper. The practical control is to match schedules, weight decay, averaging, and compute before ranking update rules. [AdamC result](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1382240679765217342/1389315980815110375); [setup explanation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1382240679765217342/1389667210624237820); [averaging concern](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1382240679765217342/1382744896649166878).

At small scale, Muon appeared to improve loss per token, but its implementation had lower model FLOP utilization, so the wall-clock advantage was less clear. The plotted optimizer curves appeared to converge as model size grew. Caution made a 1.2B run less spiky without clearly winning the Speedrun loss comparison. Muon weight decay was present in the cited experiments and was reported to matter for performance; its absence from a method name should not be read as zero decay. These observations argued for faster Muon kernels and larger matched runs, not a universal winner. [Scale comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1382240679765217342/1392284887620522085); [Muon per-token/MFU trade-off](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1382240679765217342/1392285242282479658); [Caution observation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1382240679765217342/1390541345243926619); [Muon weight decay](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1382240679765217342/1413220176542830612).

PSGD-Quad was proposed to maintain a useful preconditioner as gradient matrices became ill-conditioned during training. An 18-layer Qwen trial on v4-8 reportedly ran well; the author expected memory near Adam with bfloat16 momentum and preconditioners but had not established larger-model stability or a final wall-clock/loss ranking. A promising small run still needed memory and convergence checks at the target scale. [PSGD-Quad rationale](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1382240679765217342/1392873017947852841); [small-model trial](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1382240679765217342/1397759460998647951); [memory expectation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1382240679765217342/1397761121716408502).

## Stability depends on the model and schedule

Optimizer and attention stabilization were discussed together. MuonClip was seen as addressing a problem similar to QK normalization while avoiding QK-norm overhead under multi-head latent attention; applying it in Marin would have required passing attention logits into the optimizer. The earlier Marin 32B instability used grouped-query attention, so its resemblance to that mechanism was a hypothesis. A simpler rescaling of query/key weight norms was proposed but not validated in this thread. A separate isoFLOP run developed spikes after applying a square-root-of-batch-size learning-rate rule, whereas the smaller-batch, smaller-rate run stayed stable. The scaling shortcut was not safe in that configuration. [QK-norm/MuonClip trade-off](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1382240679765217342/1397621229942210720); [implementation cost](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1382240679765217342/1397745652238389299); [32B architecture](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1382240679765217342/1397622189158567986); [batch-size counterexample](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1382240679765217342/1395911730265198664).

In June 2026, participants traced the source of several Newton–Schulz coefficient sets used by Muon and considered a Polar Express variant. One small-scale experimenter reported that switching among the original, tuned quintic, and other coefficients had produced gains within noise. Further runs were proposed, so a theoretically better orthogonalization approximation should not be reported as a Marin training gain from this conversation. The channel also clarified a mathematical distinction between Muon and one-sided Shampoo: the outer product is formed after versus before exponential averaging of the gradient matrix. [Coefficient provenance discussion](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1382240679765217342/1516208057766383706); [reported noise-level differences](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1382240679765217342/1516263218421563522); [Muon/Shampoo comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1382240679765217342/1513677658213453954).

## Hyperball precision and learning-rate semantics

Changing from Adam to Hyperball/AdamH mid-run was reported smooth in a small Speedrun test after correcting the learning rate for update normalization. The same numerical rate has a different effective meaning under the two optimizers. The proposed empirical conversion used the original rate multiplied by optimizer-update norm divided by weight norm. Initial radius also mattered: simply copying the equilibrium norm of an AdamC run was not optimal in the cited experience, perhaps because the preceding norm transition had acted as warmup. [Switching result](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1382240679765217342/1517016423119978496); [rate conversion](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1517037017853071440/1517037611103948900); [radius heuristic](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1382240679765217342/1517017069785190453).

Using bfloat16 *parameters* instead of the usual float32 parameters with bfloat16 compute/output initially hurt loss. A follow-up found that using bfloat16 for roughly 90% of training, then float32 for the final 10%, nearly recovered the full-precision result at the tested small scale. Another trial ended bfloat16 training at a nonzero learning rate and reported loss 3.5437 versus 3.5422 for the float32 zero-end-rate baseline, without the float32 tail. The mechanism remained under investigation; rounding near a decayed learning rate was one hypothesis. The author saw little tokens-per-second improvement from bfloat16 weights in that setup, so neither quality nor speed benefits should be assumed. [Initial regression](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1382240679765217342/1517017643952111737); [float32-tail observation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1517017643952111737/1517296993024741397); [nonzero-end-rate comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1517017643952111737/1517342315751149588); [throughput observation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1517017643952111737/1517578475878486170).

Several hyperparameters were carried from earlier runs rather than exhaustively swept in Marin. The Muon momentum value 0.95 came from prior NanoGPT experience; the author said it had not been swept outside 0.95 in Marin. An AdamH beta2 floor of 0.95 was described as safe, not necessarily optimal. A practical learning-rate sweep widened by factors of two or ten until both ends did worse than an interior point. Later participants disagreed about how much an optimal rate changed across data mixes: one found substantial shifts after changing natural/synthetic composition, while another had seen relatively stable rates across the mixes used in their runs. A DCLM versus FineWeb-Edu comparison also found different Adam-to-Hyperball rate ratios. The channel does not settle a transferable default. [Momentum provenance](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1382240679765217342/1520675022354513991); [sweep heuristic](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1382240679765217342/1521602718400254052); [data-mix disagreement](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1382240679765217342/1538684255662514216); [DCLM retuning](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1538684255662514216/1538696289846300794); [rate-ratio example](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1382240679765217342/1521669798679805973).

## Stale gradients remain a cost

A delayed-gradient pipeline-parallel experiment found Muon more tolerant of staleness than AdamH, with roughly one or two delayed steps appearing cheap in that test. It still incurred a substantial loss penalty against synchronous training. The author did not propose adopting it for production runs; the discussion connected it to off-policy RL, where weight divergence likewise needs measurement and compensation. The result is about a specific training setup, not a license for arbitrary pipeline depth or stale rollout reuse. [Initial comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1382240679765217342/1516554135267180586); [final report discussion](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1516554135267180586/1516826225127985242).
