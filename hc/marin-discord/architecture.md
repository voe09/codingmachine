# #architecture

Archive coverage: 2026-07-17–2026-09-23 (UTC); 150 messages, including 79 thread replies. Every exported message body and reply in this channel was reviewed. [Open channel in Discord](https://discord.com/channels/1354881461060243556/1527756652890161292).

## An architecture change has to survive the hardware and model-scale test

The group used a shared MoE experiment protocol to compare architecture ideas, rather than accepting a result from a small standalone model. TPU, GPU, training throughput, and inference behavior all mattered. For example, TPU Splash Attention expects batch–head–sequence–dimension layout, which can imply a transpose for a new linear-attention kernel; participants expected XLA might eliminate the copy on TPU but had not checked the compiler output, and warned GPU behavior could differ. A Kimi Delta Attention proposal was also described as too expensive on GB200 in one participant's assessment because of its exponentials, while a TPU Mamba2/linear-attention implementation was being developed. These were feasibility discussions, not evidence that a particular attention family won in Marin. [Experiment protocol](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1527756652890161292/1529186868166524969) · [Layout and compiler caveat](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1528270222329380915/1528414867340394778) · [GB200 concern](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1527756652890161292/1528092240570880152).

One reported TPU measurement made the systems cost concrete: on four v6e chips with a 1B model, a linear-attention implementation processed about **29B tokens/day** versus **32B/day** for softmax attention. Adding its still-unoptimized causal convolution brought the rate down to **17B/day**. The convolution kernel was later posted for review, but the channel does not report the final integrated linear-attention throughput. At inference, a short causal convolution needs the previous `K−1` states; another participant warned from a different small-model setting that cache and runtime effects had made inference about 20% slower. The lesson was to benchmark generation, not only the training step. [Throughput report](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1527756652890161292/1533808278734245939) · [Kernel PR](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1527756652890161292/1538526449244573736) · [Inference warning](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1534568314200916159/1534654347030433943).

Two smaller ablations were promising but not final recipe decisions. An additional “over embedding” table plus a small input linear layer reportedly cut loss by 0.025 against the July baseline with under 5% throughput overhead in a small experiment. An “identity HC” attention-residual variant reportedly had a roughly 10% step-wise gain, but the final gain was only mildly positive and tuning or B200 memory impact remained uncertain. The authors explicitly treated deployment on a larger MoE as a further test. A related optimizer debate made the same point: forcing Newton–Schulz singular values toward one helped a NanoGPT experiment, but no MoE improvement had yet been seen. [Over-embedding result](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1527756652890161292/1528469931555950733) · [Identity HC result and caveats](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1527756652890161292/1528576455469174784) · [Small-to-MoE transfer warning](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1529186064537882635/1529619976850374830).

## Share a midpoint to reduce prefill work?

A YOCO-inspired proposal kept each upper attention layer's parameters but made its keys and values come from the activation at the network midpoint, while queries still came from the current residual stream. This could let upper layers compute their K/V caches from the shared midpoint and process only the final prompt token during prefill. At an 8k-token context, the author **estimated**, rather than measured, 62% prefill-FLOP savings for d512 and 49% for d768. In three smaller training-ladder comparisons, reported loss fell from 3.5794→3.5570, 3.2272→3.2223, and 3.0234→3.0128; their combined loss/throughput estimates were 1.121×, 1.025×, and 1.077× effective speedups. A more complex token-dependent mixture over first-half outputs was flat at the two tested sizes. [Mechanism, FLOP estimate, and ladder results](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1527756652890161292/1537565848108015687).

The unresolved question was how far from the top the split can be placed in a deep model. Cutting only the final three layers would make it a modest optimization; cutting half could materially reduce prefill. A contributor recalled late instability in a **30B dense** model using a similar approach, while the Marin author saw no early instability in an ongoing 8B-total run. That is a reason for a long-run scale test, not a contradiction resolved by the channel. [Depth-of-cut question](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1527756652890161292/1537576167559667862) · [Prior late instability](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1537565848108015687/1538346748710359151) · [Early Marin status](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1537565848108015687/1538335895219077171).

## Memory accounting and serving cost can reverse a choice

For a large MoE on H100s, one participant sketched a minimum pipeline-parallel node count from parameter count, bytes of training state per parameter, expert-parallel degree, HBM per GPU, and activation/buffer reservation. Under an assumed 8 bytes per parameter and eight-way expert parallelism, a 350B model needed at least five nodes with no activation allowance, or six with 20 GB per H100 reserved. A correction immediately mattered: Marin training then kept **FP32 master weights**, whereas the sketch assumed BF16 weights for an RL-like configuration. The arithmetic was a capacity planning example, not a measured deployment requirement. [Capacity model and assumptions](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1527756652890161292/1536880244156272801) · [Master-weight correction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1536880244156272801/1536933316345729134).

The same training/serving split appeared in attention normalization. QK normalization with MLA was proposed to control attention-logit growth, but one estimate said caching an extra key norm per head could enlarge the highly compressed MLA KV cache by roughly **33%** in a cited 384-dimensional/128-head configuration. A mathematically clean stabilization can therefore make long-context inference more expensive. The participants compared this with Marin's different KV compression and global-attention pattern; the 33% was not a measured Marin cache increase. [MLA/QK-norm proposal](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1527756652890161292/1551795271732568124) · [Cache estimate](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1527756652890161292/1551997294805520455) · [Marin configuration comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1527756652890161292/1552013898620731394).

## Further directions, not established results

The group discussed looped transformers for depth scaling, parameter reuse, and possible HBM or communication benefits. A suggestion to route tokens through fewer experts multiple times was explicitly half-formed, and another participant questioned whether looping's gains in a data-limited regime would carry over to Marin's compute-bound setting. In latent MoE, a separate RMS normalization after down-projection was defended as giving routed experts their own scale degree of freedom under hyperball constraints; alternatives such as a learned scalar or different initialization remained possible. These are useful hypotheses to test, not conclusions from completed ablations in this channel.
