## Ablations and data specialization

An early 1.4B/42B-token ablation tested a z-loss coefficient of 1e-4 under cosine and WSD schedules. No discernible token-loss or evaluation improvement was reported in those configurations, while the language-model-head norm increased. The conclusion was about those runs and metrics, not that z-loss is universally useless; later MoE discussions found different stability and evaluation tradeoffs. [Z-loss experiment](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1357769641132298321/1357769970385424435).

A medical-data anneal using about 500M unique tokens raised the reported MMLU medical subset average from 0.599 to 0.721 versus its control. The team immediately checked train/test overlap: at 10-grams, one combined medical dataset showed 0.195 overlap against MMLU while the MedMCQA and PubMed subsets were much lower. Much of the inspected overlap was in question wording, and different n-gram lengths changed the measured rate. The lesson is to investigate contamination before attributing a specialized-eval gain entirely to learned medical knowledge. [Anneal result](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1357769641132298321/1377336112866922657) · [Overlap audit](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1357769641132298321/1377378103067938976).

Post-training experiments exposed evaluation and truncation pitfalls. One HumanEval improvement/drop report was later corrected as likely a spreadsheet transcription error; the source logs were lost in a terminated Colab session. Another discussion noted that 4,096-token training truncation could cut off long reasoning trajectories, so the model might see beginnings of chains of thought without final answers. Small evaluation samples of 100 examples per task were useful for triage but produced wide measurement error. These cases argue for retaining raw logs, prompt/output samples, and sequence-length accounting alongside aggregate scores. [Corrected result](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1357769641132298321/1412933422191612024) · [Truncation issue](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1357769641132298321/1411140865585451139) · [Small-sample caveat](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1357769641132298321/1410763043373060106).

## Optimizer and architecture questions

Batch-size and learning-rate experiments motivated an analysis linking AdamW and Muon critical-batch behavior and square-root learning-rate scaling through steepest descent under different norms. The author later explored how weight decay might couple to critical batch size, explicitly presenting that as a hypothesis needing larger runs. The Discord discussion is valuable as a research direction but does not establish a universal optimizer law. [Initial analysis](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1357769641132298321/1447377689433477302) · [Follow-up hypothesis](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1357769641132298321/1457724741442994196).

A fast-model proposal tested starting with extremely sparse MoE activation and increasing the number of active experts during training. Initial trials did *not* recover the performance of a less-sparse model trained throughout at the higher activation level. The motivation was to get a realistically sized model into shape quickly for recipe exploration, not necessarily to use such a schedule for the final large run. [Negative result](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1357769641132298321/1518580389571067936) · [Motivation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1357769641132298321/1518584829883449476).
