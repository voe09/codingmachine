## Project direction

The general channel mostly carried cross-project synthesis; detailed experiments live in their technical channels. In 2025, Marin moved from an 8B base model to a 32B base release, while the team repeatedly emphasized that a strong base checkpoint was not yet a useful assistant. Better post-training data, RL environments, and evaluation were still needed. The retrospective treated open code, data, and intermediate research decisions as part of the deliverable, not only final weights. [2025 retrospective](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1354881461060243561/1457847114561229045) · [32B release and next steps](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1354881461060243561/1433121298049138782).

The 2026 agenda tied together larger MoEs and hybrid architectures, better data mixing and synthetic generation, RL across many environments, and more reliable heterogeneous compute. This was a dependency chain: scaling an architecture without a data and eval strategy, or building an RL stack without dependable inference and scheduling, would not by itself produce a competitive open model. A project update reported 18T collected tokens awaiting tagging, deduplication, and mix optimization. [2026 agenda](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1354881461060243561/1457847162783273202) · [Data pipeline update](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1354881461060243561/1506176347326779395).

The channel repeatedly returned to *data limits and evaluation validity*. Standard benchmarks were considered contaminated enough to motivate fresh-source perplexity and broader recent-data evaluations. A shared data-constrained pretraining study argued that increasing epochs or parameter count eventually overfits when available text is fixed, motivating synthetic data, data rewriting, and curriculum work. The group also discussed how an apparent weakness such as LAMBADA can reflect missing book or fiction data in the mixture, not merely a model architecture deficiency. [Early roadmap](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1354881461060243561/1374242193178493011) · [Data-constrained study](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1354881461060243561/1418674334955733122) · [LAMBADA data hypothesis](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1529509823413227611/1529692555267145794).

One concrete demonstration of scale transfer was the finished 1e23-FLOP MoE: 130B total parameters, 16B active, and 1T training tokens. Its reported Paloma macro loss of 2.234 was less than 1% from a preregistered 2.252 forecast based on runs 333× smaller; the team described about sixfold FLOP efficiency versus its dense Delphi formula. These are comparisons under that project evaluation and recipe, not a generic efficiency factor for all MoEs. [1e23 MoE report](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1354881461060243561/1506713859824816269).

## How community participation was intended to work

The group proposed several levels of contribution: test and characterize public checkpoints, add training or evaluation datasets, implement new methods for Speedrun, or tackle scoped research projects. It acknowledged that infrastructure access and accelerator availability limit which tasks an outside contributor can run; laptop-friendly evaluation ports and data work were offered alongside more ambitious long-context or code-dataset projects. The enduring lesson is to make contributions reproducible and specific enough to test rather than treating broad ideas as complete research results.
