## From a weak first model to measurable contact prediction

MarinFold used language-model-style training to predict protein contacts and eventually structure. The first quick `contacts-v1` model performed poorly. A broader hyperparameter sweep then produced a model above 0.4 contact R-precision on a 554-protein evaluation set, with a reported change in behavior after roughly 23B training tokens. Inference strategy mattered too: 100 sampled rollouts beat an ensemble over first-contact probabilities, which beat a single first-contact prediction in the cited comparison. The team also noted weaker performance on viral proteins, plausibly because the AFDB-derived training source excludes them. [Early update](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1516150256499163166/1518688580581458250) · [Sweep and inference results](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1516150256499163166/1521231463318818956).

The evaluation had to distinguish generalization from retrieving a similar training sequence. A sequence-alignment nearest-neighbor baseline was strong for proteins close to training examples, but had zero performance in the distant-identity bucket where MarinFold still folded some proteins. The result suggests learned signal beyond simple memorization, while also showing obvious headroom near the training distribution where nearest-neighbor retrieval won. Aggregate scores alone would have hidden this split. [Identity-stratified comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1516150256499163166/1520077768807678135).

## Representation and full-run ablations

Contact statements form an effectively unordered set, yet an autoregressive model serializes them. One proposed loss sought to avoid penalizing arbitrary contact order and orientation; the implementation ran end to end, but the cited update did not establish a structure-quality gain. A separate experiment replaced RoPE with width-3 causal token smearing to encode local proximity without relying on an arbitrary contact order. It looked promising at small scale but *lost* 0.0275 nats at production 1.5B scale across the full 152B-token schedule (2.9745→3.0021). This is a clear example of an appealing inductive bias failing a controlled large run. [Set-loss proposal](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1516150256499163166/1531345956321230975) · [Token-smearing negative result](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1516150256499163166/1547253368764899439).

Later work expanded synthetic structure training data using ProteinMPNN and reported a stronger model on de novo and legacy evaluations. Data filtering still required judgment: the team argued that filters might need loosening to retain hundreds of thousands of structures and that clustering by *interface* could be more informative than clustering individual chains when the same protein pair has multiple possible interactions. Those are dataset-design hypotheses, not established improvements from the channel record alone. [Later update](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1516150256499163166/1549131668957298799) · [Interface-clustering discussion](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1516150256499163166/1549420134765166654).
