## Design the transform experiment around a useful comparison

The project explored using a model to extract and rephrase web documents into more useful training text, potentially combining curation and extraction in one pass. The researchers first worked backward from a practical **eight-hour iteration cycle** instead of adopting the 200B–1T-token midtraining scale of earlier projects. A 4B thinking model produced about 70–100 tokens/second on one A100 in a long-context setting; the author estimated that four GPUs would generate only around six million tokens in four hours. TPU/vLLM inference was considered to increase throughput, but the channel does not give a measured TPU rate. [Iteration target](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462896888000024711/1474179605358186558) · [Generation throughput](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462896888000024711/1474181485320732683) · [TPU move](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462896888000024711/1474316104787562570).

Baseline choice depended on the claim being tested. A raw web source with corresponding WARC records would enable a matched raw-versus-transformed comparison; FineWeb-Edu and Nemotron Math were considered for that purpose. Once the desired claim became “better than DCLM filtering on CORE-style evaluations,” DCLM became the direct baseline. The author proposed iterating on separate WARC files and holding original DCLM out as a test set, to avoid tuning the transform specifically to the data on which it would be judged. A math-heavy source could still be a legitimate starting point if the evaluation was explicitly math-focused. [Baseline reasoning](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462896888000024711/1474537382610206750) · [Claim and DCLM choice](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462896888000024711/1474539056263335957) · [Holdout proposal](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462896888000024711/1474540321106034753).

## What the reported training results actually support

In a preliminary continued-pretraining/cooldown experiment, a **Nemotron-only** condition beat both mixtures that added DCLM and mixtures that added rephraser output. At approximately comparable added-token counts, the rephraser had an aggregate advantage over DCLM (350M transformed tokens versus 300M DCLM tokens), yet DCLM conditions won 7 of 11 individual tasks. At equal input-token counts, transformed data often did better, but DCLM produced far fewer output tokens, so that comparison answers a different efficiency question. The author noted answer-probability differences around 0.001 and did not regard this as a decisive headline result. It would overstate the discussion to say that rephrasing generally improved midtraining. [Preliminary results and caveats](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462896888000024711/1478952804830679142).

An SFT-oriented transform showed a different signal: extracting **question / reasoning / answer** tuples from mathhelpforum.com reportedly improved GSM8K and MATH. A later correction matters: those runs used already instruction-tuned Qwen checkpoints, not base models as initially believed. Improvement on top of post-training was surprising, but the group cautioned that a small model might trade away other capabilities to gain math performance; the Discord discussion contains no complete capability-retention study. Thus the SFT result cannot be used as proof about pretraining or about a broad quality gain. [Math SFT report](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462896888000024711/1481525022534139996) · [Checkpoint correction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462896888000024711/1481870933437190165) · [Tradeoff caveat](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462896888000024711/1481872675742810255).

## Extraction quality must include worst-case runtime

By July, the project was comparing a smaller/faster extraction pipeline with Justext-derived extraction and other published methods. One option was to classify paragraphs to keep or drop rather than regenerate the full page, reducing inference work if extraction quality held up. The channel reports an intended benchmark, not its result. [Comparison plan](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462896888000024711/1522147211625107508) · [Justext fork](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462896888000024711/1522292109732872222).

A single NASA publications page exposed a pathological case: Justext's line-level fuzzy deduplication ran for **eight hours** on that HTML page. The suspected cause was quadratic comparison across many paragraphs. A proposed change would switch to locality-sensitive hashing for inputs with more than 300 paragraphs. This is a concrete reminder that a cheap deterministic extractor can lose its cost advantage on outliers; a production transform needs tail-latency checks, not just average throughput. The conversation records the PR proposal, not a measured post-fix speedup.
