# #train-test-overlap

Archive coverage: 2025-05-07–2025-08-03 (UTC); 122 messages, including 28 thread replies. [Open channel in Discord](https://discord.com/channels/1354881461060243556/1369724540590227596).

## What was checked

The work began by adapting evaluation datasets to the `data-overlap` tool's input-and-references representation. Participants wanted one declared set of evaluations and a matching set of Hugging Face datasets, so the overlap audit would cover the benchmarks actually run. They listed reasoning and instruction evaluations, the DCLM Core base-model suite, and training sources including DCLM, Nemotron-CC, Dolmino, ProofPile, StarCoder, and FineMath. They kept the externally defined DCLM Core suite even where individual tasks seemed dated, to avoid choosing a convenient benchmark set after seeing results. [Overlap interface](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1369724540590227596/1369772258339324075); [eval/data alignment](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1369724540590227596/1369772807533367316); [suite choice](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1370489003534585919/1370499838092775485); [training-source list](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1369724540590227596/1373051029276463317).

The first output separated overlap in prompts from overlap in reference answers at multiple n-gram lengths and benchmark subsets, with per-example paths back to training files. In the reported 15-gram pass, about 40% of MMLU questions and 9% of answer options overlapped some checked training text; one electrical-engineering subset reached 30% question overlap. A manually traced example matched a verbatim MMLU physics question on a Quizlet page in FineMath, although the answer options differed. A quick source breakdown also flagged FineMath, DCLM, and Nemotron-CC, with a separate report putting Nemotron-CC at 60% of MMLU inputs and 22% of answer options. These are overlap statistics under a particular matching rule and corpus selection, not an estimate of score inflation. [Pipeline and MMLU example](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1369724540590227596/1373537663633588255); [source breakdown](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1369724540590227596/1373541396052443257); [Nemotron-CC numbers](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1369724540590227596/1373567941894869072).

Each `binary.jsonl` record linked an evaluation instance and its text to training-file paths. That made it possible to inspect whether a matched n-gram was a distinctive question, a common phrase, or a copied document. The group asked for a full evaluation-set × training-source matrix instead of relying on the most striking examples. They also noticed that ProofPile contained development and test splits in a pretraining source definition, prompting a separate check of what had actually been filtered. The channel does not record a final resolution of that ProofPile question. [Per-example trace](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1369724540590227596/1373564519825150022); [matrix request](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1369724540590227596/1373541439954489484); [ProofPile split question](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1369724540590227596/1373081240214704239).

## What overlap does and does not establish

The most dramatic report said 100% of GSM8K questions, and later 100% of answers, matched Dolmino. The follow-up found a `gsm8k/main/test`-named subset in Dolmino and asked whether the checker was counting GSM8K's own training split or the benchmark test split. The archived exchange does not resolve that mapping. A 100% aggregate match therefore warrants direct inspection of source documents and split boundaries before claiming that a trained model saw the test answers. [Question-overlap report](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1369724540590227596/1373561759704678420); [answer-overlap report](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1369724540590227596/1373562703125282816); [split ambiguity](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1369724540590227596/1373566242954154074); [checker question](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1369724540590227596/1373567641457000448).

Participants initially treated DCLM overlap as evidence that deduplication had failed, then corrected the distinction: deduplication removes repeated training documents, while decontamination removes material overlapping a specified evaluation. The DCLM discussion also clarified that experiments comparing a baseline with a filtered version did not imply the baseline was already decontaminated. A contributor familiar with that work reported that its smaller retraining experiments found some contamination but did not attribute their observed MMLU scores to it; the hero runs were not the retrained control. That is a reported causal finding for those experiments, not proof that all overlapping corpora are harmless. [Distinction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1369724540590227596/1373542205192409220); [baseline correction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1369724540590227596/1373543581545005138); [reported retraining result](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1369724540590227596/1373544515431956561).

The group compared its measurements with contamination analyses in other model reports and noticed that match length matters: an 8-gram result cannot be read as equivalent to its 10- or 15-gram audit. They also distinguished retraining on clean data from rescoring a fixed model after removing flagged evaluation examples. In the latter method, `score_total − score_clean` can even be negative if the flagged examples were harder than the clean subset; it does not measure the causal gain from having trained on them. Fresh held-out text and bits-per-byte were discussed as complementary signals, while acknowledging that any fixed text set can overlap training. [N-gram comparability](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1369724540590227596/1373556970006646856); [rescore interpretation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1369724540590227596/1373556690452217867); [negative difference](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1369724540590227596/1373554103027896341); [bits-per-byte caveat](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1373558276255649893/1373558796940476436).

## Processing cost

Decontamination over large shards could read from GCS directly or through `gcsfuse`, with cost depending on region and data movement. An August 2025 estimate put `gcsfuse` at about four times the local-storage cost but roughly five cents for one DCLM global shard under the stated conditions. Those figures describe that deployment, not current cloud pricing. [Cost analysis](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1369724540590227596/1401671179395268658).
