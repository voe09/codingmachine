# #evals

Archive coverage: 2025-04-30–2026-09-18 (UTC); 1,319 messages, including 410 thread replies. Every exported message body was reviewed chronologically. Seven bodyless posts had attachments unavailable in the text export. This is a synthesis of the Discord discussion, not independent verification of linked artifacts. [Open channel in Discord](https://discord.com/channels/1354881461060243556/1356487738840318002).

## Match the measurement to the model and the decision

The first evaluation conversations separated base-model completion and log-probability tasks from instruction-tuned models' generation, reasoning, code, and preference tasks. A multiple-choice benchmark such as HellaSwag can be useful for pretraining comparisons while saying less about how an assistant responds to a user. Proposed SFT suites included MMLU-Pro, GPQA, MuSR, MATH-Hard, BBH, IFEval, HumanEval, and preference tests; the group debated which scores should be internal diagnostics and which were worth reporting publicly. They also wanted a loss on good interactions rather than relying solely on fixed task accuracy. [Base versus SFT suite](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1370066594763378769); [internal and public metrics](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1371380812737679382).

Cross-model scores were repeatedly sensitive to benchmark implementation. OLMo's OLMES and LM Evaluation Harness sometimes used different variants of nominally identical tasks; BBH chain-of-thought and multiple-choice scores, for example, could not be compared as one number. A Marin BBH result also looked wrong because punctuation was stripped from one model's answer and not another's. The team moved toward matching the variant and post-processing before drawing a capability conclusion. An unexpectedly weak HumanEval score for Llama Instruct likewise prompted a rerun and concern about prompt formatting. The May 2025 launch-comparison counts changed as tasks were added and corrected, so the enduring lesson is the comparison procedure, not any one early win count. [Harness disagreement](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1371877324911280189); [BBH punctuation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1371728500243042354); [HumanEval concern](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1373401090221146154).

The pretraining battery grew from a small standard suite toward broad coverage. In October 2025, the proposal was to retain 19 established tasks, inspect more than 100 harness tasks for broken or biased results, add fresh-text loss, and record task metadata in a community-reviewed spreadsheet. The group explicitly wanted a selection principle that would prevent cherry-picking. By November, a curated 32-task comparison reported mean per-dataset ranks of Marin 32B 2.38, OLMo-2-32B 2.75, Qwen2.5-32B 2.19, and Gemma-3-27B-PT 2.59. A later suite found Marin's persistent deficits in math, code, and reasoning and cautioned that Qwen might have benchmark-specific training exposure. These are suite- and version-specific reports, not a universal model ranking. [Suite design](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1431136583721029642); [32-task result](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1435383466408808580); [gap analysis](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1441587733918257264); [stage-matching concern](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1507184887084482671).

Breadth kept increasing: one researcher reported 71 harness evals in January 2026, then 181 candidate tasks and 601 metrics in a single Qwen test job. These counts include separately configured sub-tasks and metrics, not necessarily that many independent abilities. A proposal for frequent, cheap pretraining checks favored multiple-choice or reference-solution log-probability tasks, reserving slower generation for final models. Another researcher cautioned that the log-probability of a correct MATH solution can change greatly with the model that wrote the reference solution, even if both solutions are valid. Thus a convenient proxy needs comparison against generated-answer accuracy, especially as models leave the near-zero-accuracy regime. [Candidate expansion](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1465481663411912824); [quick-suite proposal](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1466580244738871541); [reference-solution caveat](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1466643591744196709); [single-job metrics](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1467231595210477630).

## Perplexity as a broad proxy—and its limits

The central September 2025 question was whether evaluation could measure generalization rather than familiarity with a benchmark domain or answer format. One proposal was loss on recently created, high-quality natural text—new arXiv papers, code, news, and other material—with a versioned snapshot of Uncheatable Eval. Freshness helps with direct train-test overlap, but participants challenged the name: recent text can resemble older text, can be generated or copied, and can overrepresent particular domains or formats. Others argued that breadth of natural data may matter more than strict freshness. The proposed north star was therefore not simply “new text”; it was broad, representative text whose loss is cheap and stable and, if used as a proxy, demonstrably tracks abilities people care about. [Initial proposal](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1417394447129382943); [format concern](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1417396158476587050); [generalization concern](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1417714497824493608); [breadth argument](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1417722064961405119).

One validation plan put models' fresh-text loss on the x-axis and benchmark accuracy on the y-axis, across Llama, Qwen, OLMo, Marin, and scaling runs. A smooth relationship—even a sigmoid—would support using the cheaper measurement for hill-climbing. An initial plot appeared more correlated with some benchmarks than Paloma or C4 for its chosen models, but the author noted that fitting and model selection could explain part of the difference; instruction-tuned models were expected to break the base-model trend. The group also considered whether a broad loss should correlate with many downstream tasks rather than one cherry-picked task. [Proposed test](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1417572321199259751); [initial correlation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1418685458988269569); [basket criterion](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1417722226677125211).

Even when loss goes down, its composition matters. QA rewriting was reported to improve downstream scores without lowering Paloma loss in one cooldown setting; another participant had seen rewritten data improve in-distribution validation loss. They identified training mixture, cooldown stage, and format tokens as plausible reasons for the difference. Suggestions included tracking an IID held-out set with fixed sample count, per-token-type average loss so whitespace does not dominate, and document-level histograms of the difference between two models rather than only a corpus average. The exchange did not establish that IID loss alone can replace out-of-distribution or task evals. [Rewriting contrast](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1417897118013587516); [IID loss rationale](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1417924531506642945); [token-type loss](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1420889770921427107); [document-level proposal](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1425213862428414093).

Later tests exposed sharper limits. A February 2026 sweep found poor correlation between MATH pass rates and bits per byte (BPB) on reasoning traces, even after trying correct, incorrect, and unfiltered traces from different source models. Cross-family PPL was also considered vulnerable to tokenizer, preprocessing, and cooldown differences. The researchers discussed choosing which tokens to score, logit-rank or temperature-rescaling alternatives, and distinguishing a loss metric that predicts compute scaling from one that predicts final task accuracy. A May conversation found `choice_logprob` more aligned with multiple-choice accuracy than plain BPB, while the latter was smoother under compute scaling and sufficient for basic monitoring. None of these exchanges established one loss metric that works across all training stages and families. [Reasoning-trace test](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1470224087426138246); [cross-family caveat](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1471190520226254879); [temperature issue](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1498466558412849233); [MCQA metric distinction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1506918429175840831).

## Use evaluation to find missing data, not just rank models

In April 2026, a breakdown of coding-agent traces turned aggregate loss into a diagnosis. Marin 8B looked competitive on assistant prose and relatively good on tool-call spans, but much worse than peers on tool observations and final patches. In the reported test, additional trace context *worsened* Marin's patch prediction while helping the comparators. The authors warned that trace style and patch formatting differed across models, yet treated the gap as a reason to add tool-result, patch, and coding data before agentic RL. A separate “perplexity gap” survey found stronger Marin performance on edited English prose and weaknesses in non-English text, raw web artifacts, code, structured tables, and scientific notation. These surveys were intentionally exploratory and not decontaminated. [Agent-trace breakdown](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1498186539283382393); [long-tail corpus survey](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1498399225841455344).

The same discussion challenged the causal leap from “lower trace BPB” to “better agent after RL.” One researcher observed that task-trace BPB was sometimes less correlated with MATH performance than generic arXiv data, and that different fine-tuning recipes could have similar loss but different outcomes. Others argued that a model unable to model patches or tool results is still a worse foundation for coding agents, even if no single validation set predicts RL performance. Their practical compromise was a *basket* of natural diffs, commits, pull requests, code-world-model tests, and agent benchmarks, with loss as one diagnostic rather than a guarantee. [Proxy objection](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1498461632609783832); [natural-data direction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1498465112439259309); [unresolved proxy questions](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1498465142239789216).

Heatmaps made it possible to inspect *where* models assigned surprising probability, not just average BPB. A May update identified a large gap on JSON and arithmetic progressions; participants noted that some code slices were not held out and that apparent Qwen advantages could reflect training overlap. The weak regions clustered around whitespace, newlines, digits, and punctuation. A tokenizer analysis reported Marin's inherited Llama 3 tokenizer had different digit and multilingual segmentation from Qwen's, especially for Mandarin. Formatting mismatch and tokenizer choice are plausible contributors, but the chat did not isolate them from training-data differences. The group proposed both broader tokenizer training and targeted synthetic “basic skills” data; it also noted that quality tags should group candidate data for mixing, not discard every unfamiliar-looking sample. [Heatmap reading](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1504750674490036335); [tokenizer analysis](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1505571049066987642); [formatting explanation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1507123542872035480/1507137288684044368); [synthetic-skills proposal](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1512594109368369312); [tag-and-mix rationale](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1512597672396062960).

## Evaluation code is part of the result

Failures often originated outside the model. A 32B log-probability run repeatedly exhausted host RAM because of model-loading behavior; merely changing bf16 precision or moving checkpoint files did not solve the observed host-memory problem. Another run downloaded weights to `/dev/shm` despite a GCSFuse mount, then exposed a safetensors header error. These threads distinguish CPU RAM, shared-memory disk, device HBM, and TPU scratch memory; “OOM” alone did not identify the failing resource. For a separate device-memory issue, using the full v5p-8 resource configuration fixed a failing 32B run. [Host-RAM diagnosis](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1423177256108294165); [CPU-loading workaround](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1423209569240354837); [mount and weights issue](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1426302922341482568); [v5p-8 config](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1430988921378046013).

Prompt and grader behavior could reverse an interpretation without changing weights. AIME output improved after the chat template was correctly applied, but exact-match grading still missed answers embedded in otherwise good generations. A MATH spreadsheet number turned out to be `leaderboard_math_hard`, not the `minerva_math` variant another evaluator had run. A 0-shot Levanter HumanEval run approximately matched a vLLM run, illustrating how matched settings can cross-check infrastructure. Later, a requested 10-shot HumanEval configuration was silently overridden to 0-shot by the task config, while the few-shot prompt construction itself appeared to include test metadata. The GSM8K grader produced false negatives in sampled rollouts; a proposed internal corrected grader would coexist with the standard public one, because changing the official scorer would break historical comparability. [AIME template and extraction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1427789130460561528); [MATH variant mismatch](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1427686397149577257); [HumanEval cross-check](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1427746711526314055); [ignored few-shot setting](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1527176296763752589); [GSM8K grading trade-off](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1499112692399472781).

Other task-definition pitfalls appeared as the suite expanded. Aggregating MMLU together with unrelated tasks changed a macro average because it averaged tasks rather than examples; running MMLU separately was a workaround. Long-context tasks needed explicit length limits, truncation policy, tokenizer, and chat template; one HumanEval discrepancy was explained by harness left truncation masking a vLLM failure beyond the model's context window. Evalchemy's GPQA-Diamond variant placed the correct answer at D, raising concern about answer-position bias and parsing even though its zero-shot model ordering might still carry signal. Training on FLAN also raised a direct decontamination question for the benchmark suite. [MMLU aggregation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1420508798032019467); [long-context configuration](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1509803902638362625/1511408368215134320); [context truncation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1526513864697450526/1526631429872554167); [GPQA answer ordering](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1529631728568766484/1529632893067071711); [overlap concern](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1431136941646020638).

The proposed reporting standard therefore included exact dataset and task versions, prompt and answer processing, few-shot count, model stage, runtime and serving configuration, and enough saved generations to inspect surprising cases without exhausting artifact storage. New benchmark additions should be checked against a known model; corrections to erroneous items should have evidence and a distinct version so old results remain interpretable. The channel did not treat a benchmark PR or plausible-looking score as automatic validation. [Versioning and review](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1528226796729008288/1529535129498685471); [artifact-storage constraint](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1425344274035114044); [canonical policy announcement](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1534217574148083783).

## From task harnesses to agents and protocol policy

The framework discussion sought a maintainable route from pretraining checks to post-training evaluation. LM Evaluation Harness supplied log-probability and many generation tasks. Evalchemy was attractive for math and reasoning; HELM offered modularity and a results interface; Harbor handled interactive agent environments. Early TPU integration and version pinning were painful, and the task count grew faster than the original forked harness. In January, an upstream harness change made Torch optional on the cluster, opening a path away from a local fork; by June, contributors were coordinating a Marin Evalchemy fork and Harbor integration. The suggested ownership boundary was reusable prompts, extraction, grading, and per-example logs in Evalchemy, with checkpoint runs, budget sweeps, aggregation, and reporting in Marin. [Unified-stack question](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1461766966040596644); [framework trade-offs](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1463309011624398880/1463384984806232271); [upstream harness improvement](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1463769311192678420); [fork coordination](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1514733965070827641); [division of work](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1542743038483370029/1545134379448410112).

Agent evaluation added a new failure category: an environment or sandbox can fail independently of the model, and a long trace can time out or exceed context-recovery limits. A Harbor parity test on preemptible TPU was reported in July, but not yet for every model. August reruns of a GLM agent model saw many timeouts, and the team traced some failed long trajectories to a recovery summarizer exceeding its own output cap. They proposed distinguishing infrastructure errors from model errors and testing warmed sandbox snapshots for drift. A July candidate battery paired non-agentic MATH500, HumanEval+, MBPP+, MMLU, GSM8K, AIME24, GPQA-Diamond, and IFEval with agentic OT-TBLite, Terminal-Bench 2, and a small SWE-Bench sample; it was a v0 list, not a permanent standard. [TPU parity scope](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1523362280136380708/1523481069649793235); [v0 battery](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1529536143727657082/1529536151072014606); [recovery error](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1537867653836841090/1539143492008411187); [error classification proposal](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1527665113258131578).

For expensive or floor-scoring benchmarks, the group also sought *fresh proxy instances*: new examples with similar capabilities, difficulty, and format that preserve model rank ordering without copying the original benchmark. That is different from sampling a smaller subset of the original test. Proposed targets included SciCode and several Artificial Analysis index components; one public goal was to improve on that index over the following year. Proxy validity remained an empirical question, not something conferred by resemblance alone. [Proxy definition](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1525006979234271243/1525857732186607698); [target list](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1525006979234271243/1525055406194299041); [stated target](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1514646446446739556/1514825578497314977).

By September 2026, a training/evaluation mismatch had become a protocol-policy issue. A Harbor tokenization/continuation fix used in training was absent from the pinned Terminal-Bench evaluation stack. The proposed experiment was to evaluate a model trained with that fix both with and without it. The team considered two lanes: one frozen for cross-model comparison and one aligned to the current training stack for checkpoint selection, with both protocols disclosed if they produce different results. The canonical setup was to remain pinned pending evidence. Subsequent questions about default trace summarization and a 32,768-token input cap showed why those settings are part of an eval result: they can change timeout rates and handicap a longer-context checkpoint. A revised policy was linked in the channel, but the visible exchange did not settle every configuration question. [Mismatch report](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1547681351849869486); [two-lane proposal](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1547710721259868220); [policy revision](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356487738840318002/1549792456181022891); [context and summarization challenge](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1549792456181022891/1550017069368479785).
