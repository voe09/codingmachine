## Curate for a target, then check what else moves

Datashop used model-assisted labeling and filtering to make domain-focused training sets. The tutorial's labeling stage was shortened to about 15 minutes by sampling 1,000 examples and using Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, a faster way to iterate on the annotation workflow rather than a claim that this sample was enough for final data selection. A separate Q&A rewrite of science material was described as about 12B tokens and used in the Starling lineage. The channel does not itself provide a controlled comparison of the Q&A rewrite with its unrewritten source. [Tutorial change](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356484955852509245/1370528918527803473) · [Science rewrite description](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356484955852509245/1372714786696986765) · [Starling use](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356484955852509245/1372714366155231263).

A FineMath-oriented run showed why aggregate benchmark scores can obscure the intended effect. Overall MMLU was slightly lower with new data (0.595 control versus 0.591), while the mean of elementary-, high-school-, and college-math subsets rose from 0.347 to 0.383. The proposed interpretation was that math-heavy curation helped math but gave up some unrelated categories; the channel does not report a full category-by-category accounting. The experiment's initialization checkpoint also appeared absent from the dependency graph, a provenance issue when interpreting such comparisons. [Overall and math scores](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1373328034890776606/1373567891953422367) · [Missing dependency](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356484955852509245/1373356010864906394).

Medical filtering provided a second, more explicit example. Starting from DCLM, a model scored passages using a five-point rubric that progressed from merely mentioning medicine or biology to clear, detailed, educational explanations; question-answer formats were allowed. After annealing on the selected data, reported PubMedQA moved **0.754→0.758**, MedMCQA **0.386→0.452**, and HeadQA **0.359→0.391**. These are one run's benchmark results, not proof that the scoring rubric is optimal. Asked whether the rubric was actually followed and whether prompt wording mattered, the author said these still needed investigation, including whether the encoder could distinguish small rubric changes. [Rubric](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356484955852509245/1379887185284563035) · [Medical evals](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356484955852509245/1379684718089343028) · [Unresolved prompt sensitivity](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356484955852509245/1381076830655615167).

These examples support a narrower lesson than “better data improves every task”: targeted curation can change the mix of capabilities, and the relevant benchmark must match the intended domain while still checking regressions elsewhere. The channel also noted a practical inspection error—an initially shared link pointed to raw DCLM rather than the filtered medical output—showing why dataset paths and lineage should be verified before judging examples by eye. [Raw-link correction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1378170212343480480/1378171383921639514).

## Synthetic data, reuse, and release provenance

The group distinguished rewriting existing documents from unconstrained synthetic generation. Early synthetic rewrites were described as promising, whereas synthesizing from scratch without a verifiable reward was considered harder to trust. Another apparent change in the cooldown corpus—from DCLM toward Nemotron—was explained partly by **running out of DCLM**, not a finding that DCLM had become inferior; combining both was still suspected to be useful. [Rewrite assessment](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356484955852509245/1365358936030711900) · [From-scratch caveat](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356484955852509245/1365359209256058891) · [DCLM clarification](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356484955852509245/1365197167546863697).

One proposed use of several differently specialized Datashop cooldown models was to initialize distinct MoE experts. The motivation was an observation from prior work that copying one checkpoint into every expert may limit specialization and its FLOP savings. No MoE result from this proposal appears in this channel; it is an experiment idea, not a demonstrated Datashop benefit. [Specialized-expert proposal](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356484955852509245/1374422088043073686).

Dataset release also required tracking transformations and licenses. The team discussed uploading markdownified Wikipedia, arXiv, and Stack Exchange sources under their original licenses while separately deciding how to label the Q&A-rephrased subset and whether Llama's model license needed acknowledgement. A dataset/model card and stable checkpoint revisions mattered so readers could trace which data and base model produced a result. The conversation raises licensing questions but does not resolve them as legal conclusions.
