## Why intervene before post-training?

The early discussion contrasted removing potentially dangerous pretraining examples with rewriting them to teach safer behavior. Filtering could make a capability harder to recover from an open-weight model, whereas post-training refusal behavior may be removable by further fine-tuning. But deleting examples might also erase legitimate knowledge or generalization that common benchmarks do not measure. A participant reporting work on “deep ignorance” said standard benchmarks had not shown degradation with their filters, while explicitly acknowledging that unmeasured capabilities might suffer. Another participant challenged the blanket assertion that there must always be a safety/capability tradeoff; the channel did not settle it theoretically or empirically for Marin. [Filtering versus rewriting](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1406020028884717719/1406021249208946789) · [Tampering and unmeasured loss](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1406020028884717719/1410597284789489695) · [Challenge to universal tradeoff](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1406020028884717719/1412777579328442399).

The proposed research target was therefore a **tradeoff curve**, not a binary verdict on filtering: vary intervention strength, test dangerous capability, and check broad benign knowledge at relevant model scales. For biological safety, the group specifically wanted to reduce harmful capability without losing general biological understanding. Published studies were cited as leads, but their reported numbers were not a Marin replication. The planned 2025 collaboration did not get off the ground, according to the channel's July 2026 status; systematic Marin red-teaming was then discussed as a future activity. ## Default behavior is not robustness to weight changes

A community member later reported a Marin-8B versus OLMo-3-7B-Instruct red-team harness. They said it reproduced 13 rows of OLMo's published safety table within three percentage points. In their default-behavior comparison, Marin scored better on some role-play/jailbreak tests and worse on HarmBench, with the largest plain-request gap attributed to misinformation; the same broad pattern was claimed at 32B. These are the contributor's reported results from a particular harness, not an independently verified global safety ranking. [Harness and benchmark report](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1406020028884717719/1532209724190232578).

The same contributor reported that fine-tuning either open model on about 100 public examples moved attack success to roughly 99% within ten steps. The key distinction is between a model's default refusals for ordinary users and **tamper resistance** after someone changes its weights. The report argued that the former cannot establish the latter. The channel does not include enough experimental detail to infer that every safety behavior is equally removable, but the reported attack strongly motivated exploring pretraining-stage interventions. [Fine-tuning attack report](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1406020028884717719/1532208876919718039).

## Pretraining safety may also be brittle or entangled

Several speculative mechanisms were raised for making safety more durable. One external synthetic-persona approach reportedly lost its safety effect when the assistant token changed at post-training. “Gradient routing” or removable auxiliary modules were suggested as a way to isolate examples associated with an unwanted behavior, but the cited demonstrations concerned domain knowledge rather than a persona or tone; a cleanly separable “unsafe” module was not demonstrated here. Another contributor noted that rewriting/reasoning traces in pretraining may implicitly shape an assistant persona, desired or otherwise. Their suggested special persona tokens were a hypothesis, not a Marin result. [Assistant-token brittleness and module proposal](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1406020028884717719/1532232364854345918) · [Pretraining persona observation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1406020028884717719/1531681356675809281) · [Token hypothesis](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1406020028884717719/1532182158758772937).

The red-team report also observed more answers classified as misinformation during Marin-8B's final curated-data cooldown. The team explicitly did **not** conclude that the cooldown data caused a true safety regression. The reported metric was a WildGuard classifier label, and three explanations remained open: better instruction following might answer more of the evaluation prompts, longer or more polished writing might trigger the classifier, or the model might genuinely refuse less. Follow-up experiments were proposed but no resolution appears in the export.
