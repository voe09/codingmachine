# #gpu

Archive coverage: 2025-07-30–2026-09-18 (UTC); 119 messages, including 64 thread replies. Every exported message body was reviewed chronologically. [Open channel in Discord](https://discord.com/channels/1354881461060243556/1399998407657001062).

The first GPU onboarding exchange was about data placement, not a missing accelerator kernel. A collaborator with 32 H200s could not locate some Paloma/Proof-Pile files; the suggested working GPU Paloma path was inside the repository's `default_speedrun` function. A maintainer said Marin had previously achieved solid GPU MFU through 8–16 nodes, but some data-layer paths assumed GCS. Thus making a run portable to another GPU cluster required checking dataset URLs, formats, and permissions separately from JAX training performance. [GPU Paloma path](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1399998407657001062/1400001940200357898) · [Missing files](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1399998407657001062/1400019185072865333) · [Data-layer diagnosis](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1399998407657001062/1400144419990016020).

MoE performance did not extrapolate from a small model. A reported Grug d1024-ish profile reached 373k tokens/s against a 166k Megatron comparison, but at d5120/L8 Grug's ragged and ring variants reached only 14.2k and 24.4k tokens/s against 91.6k for a Megatron TE+DeepEP flash setup. The comparisons were not perfectly matched—the small-model Megatron flash result was missing—but the large-model reversal was too large to explain as a simple optimizer tweak. A later two-node profile put ring-based expert parallelism and Muon among the main costs; grouped matmul had headroom, but attention and cross-entropy were smaller step-time bottlenecks. The practical target was whole-step routing and communication, not just faster local matrix multiplication. [Scale-dependent numbers and caveat](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1399998407657001062/1509715606709211136) · [Profile breakdown](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1399998407657001062/1518855948100177991).

Muon exposed a layout conflict. In the FSDP-like training layout, expert matrices were split across data/replica axes and gathered for forward/backward; Adam-style elementwise updates could run on local shards, with gradient reduce-scatter partly hidden under backward. Muon's Newton–Schulz step instead wanted whole 2D matrices local to a device, encouraging a grouped expert-matrix layout. Moving the updated weights back to FSDP required another expert-weight-sized redistribution—estimated at roughly 121.875 GiB globally in that run—which did not disappear merely by adding nodes and was hard to overlap after backward. One two-node profile assigned about 500 ms of a 1,500 ms step to Muon, or around one third of step time. Smaller optimizer minibatches, more expert parallelism, and pipeline parallelism were considered ways to overlap or reduce the transfer, but early PP experiments did not beat FSDP. This was a measured problem for that GPU configuration, not proof that Muon is always inefficient. The TPU comparison did not resolve that GPU result. A first TPU AdamH/MuonH throughput comparison at d2560 on v4-1024 looked nearly equal, but its author retracted the initial metric as inaccurate and later reported about 1.47 million tokens/s for Muon while still awaiting a reliable loss curve. Participants had not profiled why the TPU path differed; they pointed to possible communication and XLA differences, as well as different expert counts, rather than establishing one cause. For the immediate GPU run, a planning meeting reported about 19.9 MFU on four nodes with SGD/Adam and set Muon aside because an estimated 10% step-count improvement did not repay the measured wall-time cost. This was a project decision under current kernels/interconnect, not a general optimizer-quality verdict. [Retracted TPU comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1518073966030487734/1519176407396257873) · [Revised TPU observation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1518073966030487734/1519259765627289681) · [Unresolved cause](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1518073966030487734/1518076926147821620) · [GPU decision](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1399998407657001062/1520200189690773564).

The subsequent GPU work targeted fused expert dispatch and more useful overlap. A prototype used NVLink to push token chunks toward target devices and fuse receiving work with expert GMM/SwiGLU; it showed promise in overlapping all-gathers, but the author explicitly said balancing communication and compute was unresolved and the combine half remained to be built. A later PGLE setting was reported to lift a 360B-A23B configuration to about 25 MFU despite Muon and several architectural features. Its author cautioned that the result did not imply multi-rack scaling was solved or that no bfloat16 speedups remained. Reported MFU was therefore configuration- and scale-specific; compare kernels, placement, batch, and interconnect before carrying a single number to another run.
