# #data-mixing

Archive coverage: 2026-01-20–2026-09-15 (UTC); 706 messages, including 259 thread replies. Every exported message body and thread reply was reviewed chronologically. Twenty-eight attachment-only posts had no text to evaluate; linked plots and external reports were not independently audited. [Open channel in Discord](https://discord.com/channels/1354881461060243556/1462895580064911522).

## The research question and its limits

The project sought data-mixture weights across training phases while accounting for finite corpora, repeat exposure, quality upsampling, and a changing pool of sources. A useful policy had to predict when a small, valuable domain should be introduced and how many epochs it could tolerate. It also had to work when the optimization metric and the actual model capabilities diverged. The work started with RegMix and OLMo 3 as baselines, then moved through two-domain experiments, a 39-domain Dolma swarm, model-scale tests, and production-pool swarms. [Scope](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1463172424257245349); [39-domain setup](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1485029900715819190).

An online controller was discussed but was not being deployed in the production training loop. A greedy policy can consume scarce high-quality data early even when it would be more valuable during decay. Log-linear response models do not directly capture saturation or overfitting from repeated exposure. Deterministic, high-throughput data loading was a separate concern for online changes. The team favored precomputed phase schedules for the current runs and left adaptive curricula as research. [Greedy-policy objection](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1476399039917457659); [current scope and loader concern](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1476399039917457659/1476399511822925936); [modeling discussion](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1476490868121669794).

## Controlled StarCoder experiments

The two-domain tests used Nemotron-CC as general text and StarCoder as the target code domain. C4 had been considered as common text but was smaller than StarCoder in that setup, defeating an intended non-exhaustible-base-data case. Even with only two domains, repeated StarCoder exposure bent the loss surface enough that linear and log-linear forms fit poorly. An observed good two-phase run used no StarCoder in phase one and 28.1% in phase two; a model-predicted and validated mix used 21.8% then 24.3%, about six StarCoder epochs in total. The exercise motivated parsimonious nonlinear features, including saturation and an overfit penalty, without assuming that learning value was identical across phases. [Domain choice and epoch effect](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1473598586473156799); [observed schedule](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1473599654825431060); [predicted schedule](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1473601294563545172); [phase-dependent concern](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1475968911563559156).

The March StarCoder comparisons reported lower programming-language bits per byte for a learned schedule. In two phases, proportional, UniMax, Olmix, and the learned mix scored 1.044, 0.972, 0.955, and 0.910 respectively; in three phases they scored 1.013, 0.903, 1.013, and 0.870. The learned schedules were fitted from 16 D-optimal runs; the reported Olmix fits used 116 and 160 runs. The three-phase Olmix solution collapsed back to proportional under its recommended KL penalty. These are outcomes for that metric and data, with different fitting conditions across methods. They do not establish a universal hierarchy of algorithms. [Two-phase breakdown](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1480799733529182241); [three-phase breakdown](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1480799818430545982).

## The Dolma swarm and measurement corrections

The next swarm used 39 domains: 13 Common Crawl topics split into high- and low-quality buckets, four other Dolma domains, and nine Dolmino domains. A 60M-parameter proxy trained on 1.2B tokens in an 80/20 warmup-stable-decay schedule; 238 mixtures plus proportional and UniMax baselines were run. Simulated epoching was scaled against a much larger 6.325T-token source budget. This was an extrapolation from a small-model probe, so the team checked both fit quality and whether the predicted mixture survived scale changes. [Swarm configuration](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1485029900715819190).

The first MMLU signal looked almost as variable across ten same-mixture seeds as across the entire set of different mixtures. One cause was discovered in the sampling path: `shuffle(D, seed)[:M]` changed which documents entered the downsampled subset for each seed, not only their order. Fixing the subset reduced variance. Training a 300M model on 3B tokens by itself did not remove the remaining variance, so repeated 60M runs with a fixed subset were considered more compute-efficient for reducing standard error. This episode matters when interpreting a swarm: a "seed effect" can contain a different training corpus unless sampling is controlled. [Initial variance](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1485030447548465383); [subset cause](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1485481049076273214); [improvement after fix](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1485447709786898443); [model-size comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1485826775782264863).

MMLU remained noisy at this scale even after the data fix. Scoring the probability of a verbalized option such as `A. Answer` reduced noise relative to scoring the bare letter. The team considered task difficulty and signal-to-noise ratio before using an eval as a mix objective; only ten tasks in one 300M sweep cleared the stated SNR threshold of 2.0. For tiny proxies, they proposed canonical-solution likelihood for generative tasks because sampled accuracy would sit near a floor. They also warned against selecting *only* metrics that correlate with a fixed downstream suite, which could steer away from broader language capability. [Verbalized-choice change](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1486488003185610832/1486783414601515058); [remaining MMLU noise](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1488315734739255306); [SNR count](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1499505079055552582); [metric selection](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1499203676462387240); [selection principle](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1498811239927906405).

The mixture surrogate evolved from early nonlinear forms into a group- or family-based saturation-plus-overfit model, then an effective-exposure DSP form. Grouping domains constrained parameter growth, at the cost of assuming some shared saturation behavior. By April, the reported many-domain uncheatable-eval BPB was 1.092 for proportional, 1.083 for UniMax, 1.079 for uniform/stratified, 1.069 for Olmix, and 1.036 for the learned mix. Its author noted that linear and LightGBM baselines had not yet been run for that comparison, and the gains were on the chosen perplexity objective. A same-mix ten-seed BPB standard deviation was estimated near 0.0014. [Model form](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1493051454510989393); [baseline numbers](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1493069906487410890); [unrun baselines](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1493050200061645001); [noise estimate](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1492813504762089582).

The perplexity advantage persisted when the mixture selected at 60M/1.2B was validated at larger scales, but did not transfer as strongly to downstream benchmarks. A joint model intended to optimize a different mixture at every model/token scale produced unconvincing optima, so the team used direct scale validation as a safer check. Later, a dense-model swarm was used to propose a mix for MoE runs; MMLU showed some positive scaling, but uniform factor weighting did not produce a consistently desirable capability balance. These messages support conditional utility for learned mixing and expose the objective and architecture-transfer gaps. [Cross-scale validation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1498809011305451583); [benchmark caveat](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1498809634964639835); [MoE factor test](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1501763753128230993).

An apparent shortcut was rejected by the training curves. Rank correlation between the first evaluation and final loss was only 0.309 at 20% of a 60M run and 0.225 at 13% of a 300M run. Mixture rankings crossed substantially, so running larger models for just the early fraction of a schedule would have discarded the late-phase signal. [Crossover figures](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1509261173889962188).

## Objectives make capability tradeoffs visible

The team experimented with an aggregate of task metrics using item-response-style capability factors. Removing GSM8K from the objective predictably lowered math weight, and removing the code factor drove coding buckets toward zero. That was a useful sanity check on controllability, not proof that the aggregate was well chosen. In MoE validations, code-related trends and MMLU did not all move together. A "quality" label could not be treated as a scalar multiplier: moving half of each low-quality bucket's proportional budget to its paired high-quality bucket did not uniformly improve HellaSwag, even with the high-quality buckets near 1.1 epochs. [GSM8K ablation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1501007243649355977); [code-factor ablation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1501274618076336189); [factor weighting concern](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1501763753128230993); [quality intervention](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1509835903516934216).

A viewer first exposed actual sampled mixtures and their benchmark percentiles, then allowed users to change mixture weights and inspect surrogate-predicted outcomes. The inverse request—set a desired benchmark percentile and ask for the unique mix—was underdetermined because many mixes can achieve similar predictions. A gradient-based steering interaction was added for local exploration, with acknowledged limits for large steps, nonconvexity, and unreliable individual-benchmark fits. Starting from proportional mixing made the cost of prioritizing code and math easier to see: proportional was strong on some general-text metrics within the swarm, while code and math lagged. These were predicted tradeoffs to validate experimentally, not a guarantee from the UI. [Sampled viewer](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1509372677138284736); [forward prediction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1509698504002506862); [gradient mechanism](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1510106601732706515/1511069235579715625); [local-step concern](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1510106601732706515/1511081274637553815); [proportional tradeoff](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1509706849459372212).

By late June, a side-by-side comparison separated changes to the *data pool* from changes to the *weights*. The newer pool appeared to yield a larger gain than retuning weights, particularly on code and arXiv-heavy uncheatable-eval BPB. It also traded some natural-language scaling for much better code scaling, which the code/math-focused mixing objective did not correct. An initial Nemotron comparison used incorrect simulated epoching; the author said correcting it should modestly strengthen the reported relative speedups because that baseline had only about two epochs at 10T tokens. The resulting table covered 111 grouped evaluations, but its scaling confidence intervals used only four slope points and many pairings were marked nonsignificant. These were reported analyses of scaling fits, not completed full-size model comparisons. [Pool-versus-mix inference](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1520123383138750595/1520140544926421136); [NL/code tradeoff](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1520123383138750595/1520205715682099272); [epoching correction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1520123383138750595/1520212028847947916); [table and CI explanation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1521595789783076985/1525146570410950706).

## Updating the pool and the surrogate

By July the raw data registry had reached about 23T tokens, including roughly 2T more code and 2T more PDF text; replacing older Stack v2 derivatives with Stack v3 raised the reported raw pool to about 25T. The pool size was pre-deduplication. An August global deduplication report counted 25.615T raw tokens and 18.710B documents, retaining 14.397B documents (76.95%). Its 19.710T retained-token figure was estimated by applying the *document* retention fraction to raw tokens, not a measured token count. A proposed end-of-year training budget at most 20T meant that quality filtering could remove some data outright if the estimate held. [July pool](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1527055120070742037); [Stack v3 update](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1532156537794068541); [deduplication counts](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1535103694612467723); [filtering headroom](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1532157043639718029).

Changing sources and quality classifiers made old bucket-weight experiments harder to reuse. One proposal represented documents with embeddings and a domain by the weighted mean of its document vectors, allowing a surrogate to consume content features even when source membership changed. To query a desired embedding mixture, a sampling distribution over actual documents would still need to be constructed. This was a proposed representation, not a demonstrated bucket-free optimizer. Architecture, target token budget, and simulated epoching also alter the config-to-loss function; an embedding alone does not encode them. [Embedding proposal](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1529004208253304883); [config objection](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1531462806308913194/1531556524609114202); [epoch-penalty response](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1531462806308913194/1531701560906879036).

Kernel ridge regression (KRR) and the effective-exposure DSP model performed similarly on shared held-out rank and RMSE measures, with KRR better on mean absolute error and Regret@1 in the reported comparison. Both severely underpredicted an extreme mix outside the swarm's support. Leave-cluster-out tests were fairly close to random-fold results for two reported targets, but did not establish far-out extrapolation. The group proposed optimizing within a distance radius of observed points, testing each promising predicted mix, and adding failures to the training set. Reuse of old swarms across new data, architecture, and token budget remained a hypothesis to validate with new runs. [Cluster holdouts](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1529340759507996853); [KRR versus DSP](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1529775938194116638); [support-radius proposal](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1531462806308913194); [transfer hypothesis](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1531462806308913194/1531498011891339284).

More parameters did not automatically buy a better practical schedule. In July, the predicted two-phase optimum was only modestly better than a predicted single-phase optimum under the same swarm compute budget, despite evidence that a larger phase advantage existed. Two phases add degrees of freedom and make a surrogate harder to fit at fixed sample count. Different MoE sparsity was also expected to change memorization and epoch penalties, so an old swarm's architecture could not be assumed interchangeable with the target run. [Phase-budget limitation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1528921100145266999); [modeling follow-up](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1528924530540679228); [sparsity concern](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1528910100495077510/1528927071261429800).

## The August pre-launch reversal

An August production mix used an updated pool partitioned into roughly 200 domain-by-quality buckets from 292 sources. One named bucket, “Emotions, Relationships, and Wellbeing,” appeared to receive extra weight partly because it contained many synthetic multiple-choice sentiment questions; the label alone did not explain the objective's preference. In a pre-launch ladder with simulated epoching, the new mix underperformed the old mix even though proportional mixing over the two pools suggested the updated pool had more code headroom. The team had run about 110 new Bayesian-search experiments to reduce the sample requirement, compared with a much larger earlier swarm. The new search improved quickly over proportional mixing but failed to beat the old mix. The suggested cause—insufficient pre-run experiments—was a diagnosis, not a proven root cause. This later result limits the earlier April success on the Dolma objective. [Bucket observation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1539141400212537445); [pre-launch outcome](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1539387191216709673); [run count and comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1539387191216709673/1539434793563852871); [old-mix comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1539387191216709673/1539434870110167080).

## Making the mixture reconstructible

Because some providers forbid redistribution, the group proposed publishing per-document metadata instead of rehosting every training document: provider dataset and document IDs, a Marin content hash, embedding, quality score and bucket, cluster, token count, and exact position within the token cache. Researchers with appropriate source access could then reconstruct bucket membership and order. Sources requiring hydration would link pinned code and configuration; an August PR documented three such sources, while others were said to be directly downloadable through the source registry. This protects provider licenses, attribution, and upstream takedown mechanisms such as Software Heritage's. The archive records a release plan and partial documentation, not proof that every metadata record was already published. [Metadata proposal](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1542677539799961770); [rehydration PR](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1543000558263345222); [license and takedown rationale](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1543322065484910652).

The final September note anticipated one more cooldown retune conditioned on the data already consumed. It described a limited phase-aware adjustment, not a solved online curriculum. [Cooldown plan](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1549550640710160466).
