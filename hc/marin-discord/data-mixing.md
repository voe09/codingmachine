# #data-mixing

Archive coverage: 2026-01-20–2026-09-15 (UTC); 706 messages, including 259 thread replies. [Open channel in Discord](https://discord.com/channels/1354881461060243556/1462895580064911522).

## What the group was trying to optimize

The mixing project asked how much of each dataset to use at each stage of pretraining, not just how to choose one static set of proportions. Repetition limits, quality upsampling, epoching, and separate training phases all affect the answer. A dataset's marginal value can change after the model has already seen it, and the best early-stage mixture need not be the best cooldown mixture. [Project scope](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1463172424257245349).

This led to a sustained debate about *online* mixture algorithms. A greedy controller can spend scarce high-quality data early even when that data would be more valuable late. Phase-dependent and cross-domain effects might be represented with time-varying coefficients, but a simple log-linear response model does not intrinsically capture saturation or overfitting from repeated exposure. The practical lesson is to test schedules and cumulative exposure, not infer a full curriculum from static per-domain validation losses. [Greedy-policy concern](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1476399039917457659) · [Modeling discussion](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1476490868121669794).

## Phase-aware experiments and what they do *not* establish

An early StarCoder study compared hand-built baselines with learned schedules. In the two-phase case, reported bits per byte were 1.044 for proportional mixing, 0.972 for UniMax, 0.955 for Olmix, and 0.910 for the learned schedule. In a three-phase case they were 1.013, 0.903, 1.013, and 0.870 respectively; the constrained Olmix solution effectively returned to the proportional share. These are results for that setup and metric, not a universal ranking of mixing methods. [Two-phase results](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1480799733529182241) · [Three-phase results](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1480799818430545982).

The later swarm work broadened the search to 39 domains and two phases. One 60M-parameter sweep used 1.2B training tokens, an 80/20 phase split, 238 candidate runs, and proportional and UniMax baselines; the proposed production mix also had to account for simulated epoching over a far larger token budget. That scale mismatch matters: a surrogate fitted to small runs is a hypothesis about a large run, and the baseline can remain hard to beat. Increasing the probe model from 60M to 300M did not automatically remove measurement variance; repeated small-model seeds were considered a more efficient way to reduce standard error. [Swarm setup](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1485029900715819190) · [Variance discussion](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1485826775782264863).

By July, the group noted that a two-phase optimum sometimes predicted little gain over a single-phase mix. Extra schedule parameters consumed a fixed swarm budget and made optimization harder; regularization and better surrogate fits were among the proposed remedies. The conclusion was conditional, not that phase-aware mixing was useless. [Two-phase analysis](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1528921100145266999) · [Follow-up](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1528922369291452657).

## Measurement and objective design

Mixture gains can be smaller than evaluation noise. Letter-token probabilities for multiple-choice tasks were particularly noisy in one sweep; scoring a verbalized choice reduced variance. The team therefore favored tasks that small probe models could actually distinguish and excluded hard, floor-prone frontier benchmarks from some swarms. They also warned against *only* selecting domains that correlate with downstream benchmarks: correlation can justify inclusion, but an objective built solely around a fixed suite risks sacrificing broader capabilities. [Metric comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1486640925328408669) · [Task selection](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1499201070063419432) · [Selection principle](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1498811239927906405).

Work on factor- or IRT-style objectives exposed a second problem: optimizing a mathematical aggregate does not guarantee a desirable balance of capabilities. Uniform factor weights were not sufficient in one experiment. A viewer therefore explored the forward question—choose a data mixture, then inspect predicted benchmark percentiles—rather than the ill-posed inverse of requesting a benchmark score and treating one mixture as uniquely determined. [Objective concern](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1501763753128230993) · [Viewer design](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1509698504002506862).

Kernel ridge regression and DSP surrogate models performed similarly on some held-out rank and error measures, with KRR better on mean absolute error and Regret@1 in the reported comparison. Both could badly underpredict an extreme mixture outside the swarm's support. The proposed workflow was to constrain candidates by distance from observed swarm points, run promising candidates, and add new measurements when predictions fail. Reusing old swarm data as datasets and buckets change was attractive, but required checking whether the embedding representation, architecture, and epoching assumptions still transferred. [Surrogate comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1529775938194116638) · [Support-radius proposal](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1531462806308913194).

## Reproducibility and remaining questions

Because some source datasets forbid redistribution, the group proposed publishing per-document provenance instead of rehosting content: canonical dataset and provider IDs, a Marin content hash, embedding, quality score and bucket, cluster, token count, and ordering information. Researchers with access to the original providers could then reconstruct the mix. This also preserves provider licensing and takedown paths. [Metadata proposal](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1542677539799961770) · [Licensing rationale](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1543322065484910652).

The September discussion anticipated a final cooldown retune conditioned on the data already consumed. That is a modest form of dynamic curriculum, not evidence that a complete online mixing policy had been solved. [Cooldown discussion](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462895580064911522/1549550640710160466).
