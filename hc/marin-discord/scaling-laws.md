# #scaling-laws

Archive coverage: 2025-05-19–2026-08-30 (UTC); 947 messages, including 357 thread replies. Every exported message body was reviewed chronologically. Six bodyless posts had image attachments unavailable in the text export. Linked papers, plots, and run artifacts are pointers rather than independently verified evidence. [Open channel in Discord](https://discord.com/channels/1354881461060243556/1356490712199462912).

## Scaling experiments and recipe transfer

The channel treats scaling laws as an experimental decision tool: run small IsoFLOP sweeps, locate the best model size and token count at each budget, and extrapolate a larger run. The same plots can answer different questions—forecasting loss for a fixed architecture, choosing a compute-optimal allocation, or comparing data and architecture recipes—and those questions should not be conflated. A later description of the hero-run workflow explicitly included a scaling ladder after the small sweep to check whether the forecast transfers. [Workflow discussion](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1524915671555899403).

The earliest scaling ladders compared different training corpora and code-focused small models. WSD-S cooldown and re-warmup made some small models' loss fall and then spike; the spike appeared less prominent at larger scale. Because the LR schedule deliberately caused the first drop, treating the pattern as an emergent optimizer failure would have been premature. A measured EMA/non-EMA gap looked roughly stable across scales but varied with hidden dimension and possibly untuned learning rate. Participants asked for cosine controls and matched cooldown lengths before concluding that a recipe transfers. [Small-model re-warmup](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1375167314483347646); [schedule correction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1375856044089016340); [EMA gap](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1375560675849535499); [cosine control](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1375170273610829915).

Experiment design was not just “fit a line.” A sweep generator selected model width, depth, batch size, and token horizon for each FLOP budget. Beta2 scaling and an LR cap stabilized runs in one July test; very small batches were initially skipped, potentially removing the left side of a low-budget IsoFLOP parabola. TPU-friendly dimensions did not need to be exact powers of two, but tiling and kernel support still mattered to realized speed. A later correction to the original suite's FLOP accounting moved checkpoint paths, making old and new ladder names non-equivalent. These details change both the apparent optimum and whether a nominally matched budget is genuinely matched. [Stable sweep](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1397264075687329853); [batch-size exclusion](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1413665402025939064); [dimension constraint](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1397367239304286289); [FLOP correction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1463588231856521492).

The measured optimum depends on the objective. An early IsoFLOP comparison suggested compute-optimal points on MMLU were steeper than those on generic web-data loss. Switching the decay phase from pretraining text to 100% FLAN raised C4 bits per byte in a test, illustrating a tradeoff between downstream instruction behavior and retained pretraining loss. In a separate anneal, a 70/30 Nemotron/DCLM mixture beat 100% Nemotron on both C4 English and MMLU choice-logprob; the team noted that prior Nemotron exposure and epoching could change the size of that effect. [Objective dependence](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1403157849713938512) · [FLAN transition](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1407483896643195002) · [Anneal comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1415738296356573288) · [Exposure caveat](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1415743093457551523).

The validation corpus can change what “data quality” and “compute optimal” seem to mean. An August 2025 report said that the suite roughly reproduced Llama IsoFLOP curves but implied a slightly more data-heavy extrapolation, about 20T rather than 16T tokens for a 405B-scale model; the author explicitly noted that the held-out set was not matched. A smaller Common Pile comparison also looked more data-heavy than Nemotron on one validation slice, while a later Dolma 3 mixture looked better on RedPajama but worse on Paloma macro BPB. The group warned that a C4 result might measure web-likeness more than generally better data. Separate MMLU loss curves suggested a different optimum from C4 and were noisy enough that more sweep density was needed. [Llama comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1401990602392080489); [Common Pile shift](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1402767346766188645); [Dolma 3 reversal](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1442691018020618393); [MMLU versus web optimum](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1403164093132574781).

That led to a practical “wind tunnel” question: can small, controlled runs rank candidate data mixes before the hero run? One comparison found Nemotron above Common Pile above DCLM on an MMLU measure, but the individual dataset's compute optimum affected the fit and comparison noise. A 70/30 Nemotron/DCLM anneal improved both C4 and MMLU choice log-probability across the scales tested, yet it did not isolate whether the gain came from different text, different extraction of similar source text, or relief from repeated Nemotron exposure. For deduplication, the group proposed a paired IsoFLOP control on raw duplicated Common Pile and the same corpus after dedupe, since all existing sweep corpora were already deduplicated; cross-dataset dedupe in a mixed corpus was the more realistic but harder follow-up. [Dataset ranking](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1405658112593105048); [per-dataset optima](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1405703176417579199); [anneal result](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1415738296356573288); [raw/dedup control](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1442689455554171071/1445867899863699620).

## A smooth loss can hide behind flat accuracy

CommonsenseQA appeared almost flat at chance accuracy in small DCLM and Dolma models, then jumped in an 8B model, spoiling a naive scaling forecast. The ensuing debate separated knowing the answer from knowing how to produce a letter in a multiple-choice format. Letter-only scoring can remain at chance until the model learns the test convention; unconstrained cloze scoring spreads probability across many possible surface forms. One intermediate prompt showed all choices but scored the entire answer strings, producing smoother MMLU loss without a large accuracy penalty in the reported test. The group considered normalized string log-probability, a dummy-context or mutual-information correction, and NLL-to-accuracy sigmoid fits. They also cautioned that a bug prevented the mutual-information variant from being assessed in that particular run. [CommonsenseQA discontinuity](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1392548038031052821); [format explanation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1392580670613885180); [whole-string variant](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1392579852233736454); [normalization concern](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1393116672046530610); [fit and bug](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1394479725866516561).

This was why one participant pushed back on a paper presented as showing that downstream scaling laws were unreliable: its comparison plotted unrelated-corpus PPL against hard task accuracy, not model scale against loss on the task. That setup can fail even if task loss scales smoothly and accuracy is a nonlinear readout of it. Others cautioned that smoothing the metric does not remove all information loss from restricted answer sets, and that knowledge-based QA may not generalize to non-knowledge skills. The proposed control was to teach the *format* using different content, or apply a realistic SFT mix to small ladder models, and then ask whether the resulting pretraining-to-posttraining forecast transfers. The latter matters because the intended end product is pretraining plus SFT and RL, not base-model multiple-choice prowess in isolation. [Critique of inference](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1400553653219234035); [other metric losses](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1404555657411039307); [format-control proposal](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1404563038526505061); [end-product objective](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1404565748721913996).

## Hyperparameter transfer and execution cost

Stability and optimizer transfer were part of the scaling question, not merely engineering details. AdamH transferred learning-rate settings in a width-scaling test even under standard parameterization, whereas an early μP attempt had shifting optima beyond the first scale. The researchers distinguished width stability from depth stability and noticed that a decay target expressed as a percentage of each run's peak LR could confound transfer. MuonH appeared to tilt compute-optimal allocation toward larger models trained for fewer tokens, but a 4B-parameter IsoFLOP comparison initially ran at about 28% MFU versus 52% for the existing recipe. Because that sweep shrank batch size as width grew, the team did not treat the overhead as representative of a realistically scaled large run. [AdamH transfer](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1431321832216334426); [μP difficulty](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1425888528910389460); [decay confounder](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1431323069661708423); [MuonH allocation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1466138092950913096); [throughput and batch caveat](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1466175167318458420).

The transfer recipe itself needed qualification. In June 2026, a reader trying to reproduce Delphi assumed beta1 and beta2 were fixed by the published scaling rule. The author clarified that reference-scale tuning should include them, that inter-batch mixture variation can make them data-loader dependent, and that a single-source shuffled DCLM run is a somewhat simpler case. A sparse beta grid helped a separate sweep's reported fit improve from R² 0.75 to 0.978 without divergent runs; this is one run's report, not proof of a universal setting. The `references` sweep script was a *how-to* example, not the exact Delphi reference run, whose precise configuration was no longer preserved in that file. A later response framed μP as one hyperparameter-scaling heuristic rather than an alternative that automatically accounts for horizon and batch-size effects. [Retuning rationale](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1513554893158223944); [reference clarification](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1516248627025285221); [fit improvement](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1520217019436498984); [μP and broader heuristics](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1543325551635464232).

Large suites also exposed an infrastructure confounder. Running hundreds of independent steps could exhaust the Ray head node's roughly 31GB RAM even though each training step ran on its own TPU worker. The status actors had been placed on the non-preemptible head node to avoid a different failure—controller death on a preemptible worker—so shrinking or batching the sweep masked the symptom without resolving that trade-off. If runs are silently absent or aborted, a visually clean IsoFLOP curve is not necessarily a complete experiment. [Head-node OOM](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1442689455554171071/1447914001957785620); [placement trade-off](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1442689455554171071/1447917271103307921); [per-run overhead](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1442689455554171071/1447988713408823439).

## Fitting methods, uncertainty, and forecasts

A long discussion compared the common two-stage Chinchilla procedure (“Approach 2”: fit a parabola at each FLOP budget, then regress its minima) with direct fitting of a loss surface (“Approach 3”) and variable-projection nonlinear least squares. The apparent parameter-count advantage of the two-stage method is misleading because it estimates several correlated parabola coefficients per budget before the final regression. In an initial simulation, direct fits had lower exponent variance. Follow-up ablations identified two reasons: narrow within-budget grids make parabola minima noisy, and equal-weight regression treats high-budget and low-budget minima as equally precise even though the parabolas flatten. Widening the grid and using variance-aware weighted least squares brought the two-stage estimate's standard deviation close to the direct fit in the symmetric simulation—about 1.08 times it—rather than the earlier roughly 1.6 ratio. That result depended on the simulated surface and noise model; it did not establish that either method always wins. [Initial efficiency puzzle](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1475498065333850224/1475498453705687184); [grid-width finding](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1475498065333850224/1476129589431635968); [weighting correction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1475498065333850224/1478329065386676254).

The group then checked the noise assumptions against Marin and outside datasets. In one outside collection, apparent variance spikes were traced to mixing different learning rates within the same compute budget, not random measurement noise. Across the cleaner subsets they did not find a clear monotone decrease in residual spread with budget, making constant additive noise a defensible working approximation, not a proven law. They also noted that minimizing *log loss* is not the maximum-likelihood objective under additive noise and can introduce a small bias. A published reanalysis estimated roughly $1.4 million of avoidable compute at a Llama-3-scale allocation under its assumptions; that was an analytical estimate, not a Marin bill or a demonstrated saving. [Learning-rate confounder](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1475498065333850224/1479248159673946192); [noise summary](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1475498065333850224/1478991368495042622); [log-loss distinction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1475498065333850224/1478626234668941433); [cost estimate](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1483148794232176851).

Direct multi-parameter fitting is not automatic either: reparameterization is important for numerical stability, and grid search helps avoid local minima. Variable projection was discussed as a way to remove linear parameters from the nonlinear search. A proposed residual bootstrap would preserve the original model-size/token-budget design, resample residuals, refit each candidate method, and obtain an interval for a forecast or allocation statistic; participants debated whether basing the resampling on the direct fit prejudged the comparison. Experimental design matters at least as much as the optimizer. WSD checkpoints at a fixed model size can supply many points, but they share initialization and hyperparameters, while independent runs cover a broader design space at greater cost. [Fitting considerations](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1475498065333850224/1479137523165954078); [bootstrap proposal](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1483148794232176851/1483981363131580478); [WSD correlation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1479520620357222621/1479525190936428665).

The Delphi case clarified why a method can forecast loss accurately yet still be a poor allocator of model size and tokens: forecasting an already selected run and finding the optimum are different targets, and Delphi's IsoFLOP setup was more controlled than some comparison datasets. An April 2026 leave-one-budget-out analysis initially claimed the two-stage forecast was roughly 10% sensitive versus 0.5% for a direct fit; a subsequent inspection found `scipy.curve_fit` had silently failed in two cases. With bounds and convergence checks, the reported difference narrowed to roughly a factor of two, not twenty. The original Delphi forecast remained impressively accurate, but that single success did not settle which fitter was more robust. [Delphi reconciliation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1488870819349270660); [numerical-failure correction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1488870819349270660/1489218928109682689).

The group also cautioned that small-scale data cannot identify a distant asymptote reliably, whether a power law through minima or a full Chinchilla form is used. One researcher saw a fitted plateau in 1e18–1e20-FLOP runs recede after better hyperparameter tuning; another argued that finite-data experiments legitimately require an asymptote and that better fits may still extrapolate poorly. A Nemotron ladder fit gave a token exponent of about 0.58, higher than a classic 0.5 heuristic; possible explanations included smaller-scale sweeps, vocabulary size, data properties, and recipe differences. The participants treated these as hypotheses rather than a new universal exponent. [Tuning and asymptote](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1469026808283533558); [finite-data caveat](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1469026359622893777/1469053458454810849); [0.58 fit](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1460390413860082019); [asymptote limit](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1488870819349270660/1489014955801055342).

## The end-to-end optimum can differ from the pretraining optimum

The July 2026 discussion made this explicit. The then-current hero-run procedure located minima of *pretraining loss* on small IsoFLOP sweeps, regressed those optima to a large budget, and used a scaling ladder up to roughly two orders of magnitude below the hero run to forecast its loss. That is disciplined for its chosen objective, but a model meant for SFT and RL needs different success criteria. One proposed bridge was pre/midtraining pass@256 on agent-relevant tasks, under the simplifying and unproven assumption that enough RL can convert that into pass@1. Even if that proxy worked, RL and deployment involve repeated inference: a smaller model trained for more tokens might cost more to pretrain at the same loss but save enough per rollout and serving request to be cheaper overall. The channel offered a 5× “overtraining” thought experiment, not a measured universal conversion rate. [Current procedure](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1524915671555899403); [pass@256 proposal](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1524917787837861971); [inference-cost argument](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1524919843147616337).

Learning-rate cooldown raises the same end-to-end issue. One participant argued that decaying pretraining LR all the way to zero may lower the stage-one loss while making later training less effective; others favored a smaller nonzero final LR and pointed to prior runs where 0.05–0.1 of peak was only slightly worse on immediate loss. The analogy to entering narrow loss valleys was presented as a mental model, not a demonstrated mechanism. The sensible proposed measurement was to compare downstream adaptation after checkpoints at different points on the decay, rather than declaring the lowest base-model loss the winner. [Stage-one versus final objective](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1513596973851086961); [prior decay comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1513596973851086961/1513681997095112755); [downstream probe](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1513596973851086961/1513626746497400892).

For sparse MoEs, a question about the old “20 tokens per parameter” rule got a deliberately qualified answer: total parameters are a closer starting count than active parameters, while sparsity, data quality, and post-training alter the optimum. Twenty times *active* parameters would severely undertrain a normally sparse model; twenty times *total* was offered only as a rough planning heuristic. A reported Marin 1e23-FLOP pretraining-only optimum was below 10 tokens per total parameter, underscoring that the rule is neither timeless nor model-independent. The final advice was to compare scaling recipes by ladder loss, stability, speed, and downstream outcomes, not to treat μP, Chinchilla, or any single coefficient as a complete theory. [MoE heuristic](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1534490091924488286/1534574989830000843); [Marin example](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1534490091924488286/1534575254331195593); [empirical comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1543326054683377744).
