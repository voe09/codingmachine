# #scaling-laws

Archive coverage: 2025-05-19–2026-08-30 (UTC); 947 messages, including 357 thread replies. [Open channel in Discord](https://discord.com/channels/1354881461060243556/1356490712199462912).

## Scaling experiments and recipe transfer

The channel treats scaling laws as an experimental decision tool: run small IsoFLOP sweeps, locate the best model size and token count at each budget, and extrapolate a larger run. The same plots can answer different questions—forecasting loss for a fixed architecture, choosing a compute-optimal allocation, or comparing data and architecture recipes—and those questions should not be conflated. A later description of the hero-run workflow explicitly included a scaling ladder after the small sweep to check whether the forecast transfers. [Workflow discussion](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1524915671555899403).

The measured optimum depends on the objective. An early IsoFLOP comparison suggested compute-optimal points on MMLU were steeper than those on generic web-data loss. Switching the decay phase from pretraining text to 100% FLAN raised C4 bits per byte in a test, illustrating a tradeoff between downstream instruction behavior and retained pretraining loss. In a separate anneal, a 70/30 Nemotron/DCLM mixture beat 100% Nemotron on both C4 English and MMLU choice-logprob; the team noted that prior Nemotron exposure and epoching could change the size of that effect. [Objective dependence](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1403157849713938512) · [FLAN transition](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1407483896643195002) · [Anneal comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1415738296356573288) · [Exposure caveat](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1415743093457551523).

Stability and optimizer transfer were part of the scaling question, not merely engineering details. Smaller WSD models showed a re-warmup loss spike that seemed to diminish with scale. Beta2 scaling with a learning-rate cap stabilized one IsoFLOP sweep. AdamH transferred hyperparameters in a width-scaling test even under standard parameterization, while a decay target expressed as 10% of each run's peak learning rate was identified as a possible confounder. MuonH appeared to tilt compute-optimal allocation slightly toward larger models trained for fewer tokens in the tested regime. These are empirical observations that require scale- and recipe-specific checks. ## Fitting methods, uncertainty, and forecasts

A long discussion compared the common two-stage Chinchilla procedure (fit a parabola at each FLOP budget, then regress its minima) with a direct fit to all observations. The apparent parameter-count advantage of the two-stage method is misleading because it estimates several correlated parabola parameters per budget before the final regression. The critique examined statistical efficiency, bias, noisy-data simulations, and model/token misallocation. A Llama-3-scale reanalysis estimated roughly $1.4 million of avoidable compute under its assumptions; this was an analytical estimate, not a Marin bill or a proven saving. [Method debate](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1475498065333850224/1477063060094058727) · [Cost estimate](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1356490712199462912/1483148794232176851).

Direct multi-parameter fitting is not automatic either: reparameterization is important for numerical stability, and grid search helps avoid local minima. Variable projection was discussed as a way to remove linear parameters from the nonlinear search. Experimental design matters at least as much as the optimizer—failed TPU tasks can truncate a sweep, and WSD checkpoints at a fixed model size can supply multiple data points, though they are not a substitute for coverage across architectures. [Fitting considerations](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1475498065333850224/1479137523165954078) · [Sweep failures](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1442689455554171071/1447601015279849564) · [WSD collection](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1479520620357222621/1479524956831486057).

The Delphi case clarified why a method can forecast loss accurately yet still be a poor allocator of model size and tokens: forecasting an already selected run and finding the optimum are different estimands, and Delphi's IsoFLOP setup was more controlled than some comparison datasets. The group also cautioned that small-scale data cannot identify a distant asymptote reliably, whether a power law through minima or a full Chinchilla form is used. For later MoE planning, a rough token-to-parameter heuristic depends on whether one counts total or active parameters and on post-training goals; it is not a universal compute-optimal ratio.
