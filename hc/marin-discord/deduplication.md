# #deduplication

Archive coverage: 2025-11-21–2026-08-31 (UTC); 270 messages, including 179 thread replies. Every exported message body and reply in this channel was reviewed. [Open channel in Discord](https://discord.com/channels/1354881461060243556/1441211384279994529).

## What should deduplication preserve?

The central question was not simply how many documents could be removed. Deduplicating a mixture of curated datasets can lower the effective weight of a valuable document that several sources independently selected. The team therefore planned to **annotate duplicate groups**, leaving downstream experiments free to drop, retain, or reweight members. This also permits ablations of exact versus fuzzy dedup and of deduplication order relative to quality filtering and mixture construction. A contributor explicitly challenged whether fuzzy dedup still helps after strong quality filtering; the response was to test that rather than assume it. [Mixture concern](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1441211384279994529/1443154063263928351) · [Metadata and ablations](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1441211384279994529/1443295848363589722) · [Annotate first](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1441211384279994529/1443387660315001003).

The implementation plan began with making the existing **paragraph-level exact** pipeline reliable. The team then reported reliable document- and paragraph-level exact deduplication and a merged Rust exact implementation; fuzzy deduplication and its Rust implementation were still incoming in December 2025. A separate discussion cautioned that common document IDs could simplify tagging and group-bys, but the then-current ID path might use a Bloom hash whose collision properties deserved scrutiny at billions of documents. Moving every source to Parquet was not a prerequisite for the first milestone. These were implementation concerns, not evidence that a collision had occurred. [Milestone scope](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1443391124394872913/1443405151980359874) · [December status](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1448479051571527892/1448481289723187312) · [ID concern](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1443391124394872913/1443398372055715870) · [Parquet priority](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1443391124394872913/1443392771741319238).

## Scale, cost, and reproducibility

In an early whole-Nemotron-v1 exact pass, 418,385,816 of 10,349,538,071 records were marked duplicates (4.042%), with subset rates from about 3.3% to 5.7%. The first reported full-corpus fuzzy pass found about **18% fuzzy duplication**, conditional on its matching parameters. With 512 workers, connected-components bootstrap took about 12 hours; ten subsequent iterations took roughly 40 minutes each. These figures describe different methods and runs and should not be combined into a single dedup rate. Early training ablations showed a signal, but the researcher wanted more sensitivity checks before drawing a firm quality conclusion. [Exact counts](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1441211384279994529/1501299053114425436) · [Fuzzy run](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1441211384279994529/1501299398561235025) · [Ablation status](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1441211384279994529/1501300822502412288).

A later us-central2 run processed **15,089,658,952 documents**, excluding FineTranslations. Of these, 10,311,311,396 (68.33%) had no detected duplicate and 4,778,347,556 (31.67%) belonged to some duplicate cluster. Keeping one canonical per cluster would retain 11,731,886,618 documents (77.75%) and remove 3,357,772,334 (22.25%). The team described this as roughly 15T input tokens becoming 12T after deduplication. The 31.67% cluster-membership rate is not the removal rate: it includes the canonicals. Nor is the 22.25% a general rate for all web corpora. [Run counts](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1441211384279994529/1508887247796178985) · [Token estimate](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1441211384279994529/1508888271994880020).

Adding new datasets prompted a reproducibility-versus-latency debate. The existing algorithm chooses a canonical globally, so previously computed pairwise comparisons cannot simply be reused as an incremental final answer. A fresh pass avoids a deeply nested `dedup(dedup(A, B), C)` lineage and may cost little more if dedup does not shrink the corpus dramatically. But researchers wanted to add a ~1B-token dataset to a large mix and run an ablation the same day. Precomputing exact or SimHash hashes, then grouping globally—or scanning an in-memory hash set for a small new dataset—were possible future shortcuts. The team deferred the optimization until it could measure the actual 10T-scale cost. [Research requirement](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1463590986910404660/1463612648951255162) · [Reproducibility tradeoff](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1463590986910404660/1463608037850742784) · [Hash proposal](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1463590986910404660/1463610516755845246) · [Current canonical constraint](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1463590986910404660/1463610988606787848).

External systems such as Dolma/duplodocus, DataTrove, decon, and Data Prep Kit were investigated as prior art. Marin favored reusable hashing/filtering **library operations** while retaining control of orchestration and GCS layout: an earlier Dolma integration had required copying data into its expected directory structure. The team's fixed pool of smaller machines also could not be exchanged for a single 1 TB node, and duplodocus still had a terminal connected-components bottleneck. GPU/TPU-accelerated fuzzy matching was floated as future work, not reported as an implemented solution. [Tools surveyed](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1441211384279994529/1441408894604939288) · [Library preference](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1441211384279994529/1441598751977373821) · [Compute constraint](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1442622031672905760/1442632652099158136) · [Connected components](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1442622031672905760/1442635034841317386).

Two systems details mattered at this scale. A Ray `wait` default of `fetch_local=True` could pull generator-produced objects to the driver when the pipeline only needed a completion signal; the group proposed disabling that default for its workload. For repartitioning and rechunking, Ray object-store values could not be streamed partially, and placing intermediates directly in the store weakened fault tolerance. Rather than build a brittle all-streaming shuffle, the group leaned toward periodic durable materialization, possibly after conversion to a partially readable format. An early physical-plan attempt also assumed compressed JSON files could be concatenated as if they were ordinary chunks; that assumption needed correction. [Ray default](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1441211384279994529/1445176523022794905) · [Proposed change](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1441211384279994529/1445176523022794905) · [Shuffle constraints](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1441211384279994529/1446939577712382168) · [Reliability choice](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1441211384279994529/1446939822014074931) · [Compressed JSON caveat](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1441211384279994529/1446654660306800743).

## What a duplicate cluster does—and does not—tell us

Inspecting clusters revealed many templated or synthetic documents. A per-document LLM filter sometimes passed them because each page contained plausible-sounding prose; viewing several cluster members together exposed the repeated template. Yet a templated family can also encode distinct useful facts, and sampled Q&A clusters raised a preservation concern. The team discussed keeping the longest or newest member, choosing by quality, or asking a model to rewrite/extract useful material from a group. At the time the canonical was chosen randomly. Rewriting could be more informative but expensive across billions of documents and unstable as clusters change; no controlled comparison in this channel established that it beats cheap heuristics. [Template example](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1508892390037917888/1508901874487529502) · [Per-document filter failure](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1508892390037917888/1508935675330691103) · [Q&A caveat](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1508892390037917888/1509263137210372359) · [Current random choice](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1508892390037917888/1511283579815071875) · [Rewrite cost](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1508892390037917888/1509274465786396904).

The discussion also distinguished **deduplication** (redundant training data) from **decontamination** (training/evaluation overlap). The old Dolma-based n-gram/Bloom-filter approach had produced many false positives. A newer repository `rbloom` implementation had, according to its original contributor, not yet been quality-tested or run at scale. A known GSM8K leak into Dolmino was proposed as a positive-control dataset: a decontamination implementation should catch that known overlap before being trusted on larger corpora. This was a proposed validation criterion, not a reported pass. [False-positive history](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1501571926412492831/1501602265658818632) · [Untested implementation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1501571926412492831/1501622858235773079) · [Dolmino control](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1501571926412492831/1501602818254045286).

For the newer pipeline, the team discussed a broad, pluggable evaluation-target list rather than one frozen benchmark list, including lm-eval-harness tasks, AA, Paloma, and emerging evaluation baskets. Document-level removal was considered a simpler conservative start than span-level excision, with an explicit request to inspect the removed training documents. One run reported decontaminating about **6 million documents, or 0.04%**, but its author suspected substantial false positives; the number alone does not establish precision. By July 2026 the team said production data was decontaminated against Paloma and that side outputs allowed contamination to be traced to sources, but there was no dedicated DCLM/Nemotron-CC contamination report in the conversation. ## Open research question

Lexical near-duplicate removal may still leave semantically redundant examples that induce similar gradients. A contributor suggested measuring how redundancy changes scaling-law predictions and the effective amount of unique training data; the team welcomed this as more valuable research than speed alone. The channel records an issue and a proposed experiment direction, **not** a completed Marin result on semantic collisions or revised scaling laws.
