# #marin-32b

Archive coverage: 2025-04-30–2026-06-13 (UTC); 972 messages, including 103 thread replies. [Open channel in Discord](https://discord.com/channels/1354881461060243556/1367246647062564995).

## Stabilizing the 32B run

At 0.5 trillion tokens, the new 32B had lower reported C4 English loss than Marin's 70B after 1.5 trillion tokens. By 1.5 trillion tokens, the channel estimated 0.702 bits per byte for the 32B against an approximately 0.727 regression estimate for OLMo 2 32B, while noting the tokenizer difference. This was a promising loss comparison, not a controlled proof that the recipe would scale to every model size. The team proposed a Pythia-like grid of model sizes and budgets once the recipe settled, and discussed a Phoenix-style cooldown and re-warm instead of treating cooldown as the end of training. [Early comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1367247090165747843); [BPB estimate](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1375555018597470268); [controlled-suite proposal](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1367285215038541917); [cooldown and re-warm](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1374987539571802113).

The channel is a case study in diagnosing training instability without assuming every loss spike has the same cause. Participants proposed replaying and ablating the batches before update-norm blow-ups, inspecting optimizer-state changes, measuring dead neurons, and running cooldowns on checkpoints before and after a spike to see whether it damaged downstream quality. Data instrumentation was proposed alongside gradient instrumentation because distribution shifts could be involved. These were investigation plans, not established causal explanations. [Investigation plan](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1379675233610502217) · [Data hypothesis](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1377684208205430814).

The first diagnostic pass itself needed correction. The run already clipped the global gradient L2 norm to 1, contrary to an initial recollection that this was an infinity-norm limit. Its logged “total norm” was the sum of per-component norms rather than the L2 norm of the whole parameter tree, exaggerating comparisons to other runs. After correcting the logger, typical gradients were below 0.2. That ruled out some apparent cross-model norm differences; it did not explain the loss spikes. In a detailed incident, update and Adam first-moment oscillations appeared before the large loss increase, while the second moment changed much less. Some layers reacted later than others. The team therefore considered skipping an entire optimizer step *including momentum-state updates*, or clipping the update itself, instead of only shrinking the incoming gradient. [Existing clip](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1377370871580201140); [norm correction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1377519702917517363); [update/first-moment observation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1379674897109876748); [momentum-state requirement](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1375641421180047422).

The team lowered the gradient clip to 0.2 and tried skip-step. One spike was mitigated but still took many steps to recover; a later severe spike survived those measures. A first update-clipping implementation then proved ineffective because its condition could not engage on the large run. The replacement clipped update norms relative to a rolling 128-step mean plus three standard deviations, later tightened to two, reflecting that absolute update scale changed over training. Across 2,500 steps in one June report, about 130 steps were spent in or recovering from spikes. The causal picture stayed mixed: a replay at step 80,000 and a Muon branch were hard to rescue, while identical fluctuations across optimizers on shared data argued that some shorter blips were data-linked. “Data” and “optimizer” were hypotheses for different events, not mutually exclusive diagnoses for one universal spike type. [First mitigation and limit](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1378056742344327248); [severe spike](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1378462665558462606); [corrected update clipping](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1381040055757307976); [two-sigma follow-up](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1382242625092063313); [spike cost](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1379674760493011055); [shared data fluctuation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1383231224348410049).

A QK-normalization warm-start from the existing checkpoint caught up to the Adam baseline in loss and initially looked much less spiky. That was evidence for a workable mid-run architectural change, not a clean QK-norm-only ablation: learning-rate re-warm and other changes affected the loss curve, and the Muon comparison also depended on Adam-handled layers and their learning rates. A July spike after QK norm recovered, and evaluation loss continued down; the team judged one such event in roughly 700 billion tokens tolerable while continuing to suspect data-order or repeated-token effects. [Warm-start result](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1387111361024233564); [learning-rate interpretation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1385016394713927720); [July correction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1389642222932201643); [eval-loss follow-up](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1389642909850275953).

The run also exposed operational measurements that could be mistaken for model behavior. MFU fell from roughly 54% to 45% after an apparent software change and returned to about 56% after a later JAX upgrade; the precise cause was not isolated. A missing W&B trace briefly looked like a stopped run until an offline-mode environment setting was found. When trying to evaluate on hundreds of short-lived workers, sporadic Hugging Face downloads made the evaluation harness unreliable, motivating cached or offline datasets. These did not change the training objective, but they controlled whether a long run could be monitored and compared. [MFU sequence](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1399426635307946126); [offline logging](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1394368256592052404); [distributed evaluation downloads](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1385312207688171643/1385313056619237487).

At roughly four trillion tokens, the group planned a cooldown with a revised high-quality data mix and easy perplexity evaluation, deferring generation tasks until inference was reliable. An August loss change raised a separate suspicion that the dataset had accidentally epoched; the loader then doubled data without reshuffling between epochs. The midtraining/cooldown run used QK norm and AdamC and was initially smooth. In September, however, train loss jumped to a new floor even with QK norm, AdamC, and Z-loss. Early theories about decay to zero were corrected—the schedule already stopped at 10% of peak LR—and a flat-LR replay did not remove the event. Changing the mix also initially appeared not to help. The decisive limitation was that held-out eval loss had no corresponding jump, so the team reconsidered shuffle or train-data order instead of treating the checkpoint as damaged. [Cooldown plan](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1394526375255343165); [epoch suspicion](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1409020954411143269); [initial cooldown status](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1414654958258622528); [decay correction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1416328692904366161); [flat-LR replay](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1416545490769678367); [eval-loss correction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1417095581247082618).

A later cooldown combined a new mix with a Feistel shuffle and improved macro Paloma and code loss versus the prior cooldown. Stack v2 Python data was added mid-run, and the team explicitly could not separate mix, code-data, and shuffle contributions to the lower loss and slower parameter-norm growth. It is an observed *recipe* improvement, not an isolated shuffle result. [New cooldown](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1427396223572246578); [code addition](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1427396329276964946); [attribution caveat](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1427397585274015765); [loss comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1429440989092642826).

## Data choices and interpreting model quality

The recipe was constrained by rights as well as performance. Nemotron CC-v2 licensing was flagged as potentially incompatible with Marin's open-release goals, despite interest in using it rather than repeating older data. The channel did not settle the legal interpretation; the operational choice was to avoid assuming the dataset could be used. The group also discussed using OpenThoughts reasoning data in post-training and a larger, not-yet-annotated OpenThoughts 4 prompt collection. Its author described roughly five million prompts and a teacher-response generation task, not an existing Marin-trained 32B result. Participants debated which teacher to use; a stronger benchmark model was not assumed to be a better teacher without testing. [License concern](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1413766160150954075); [OpenThoughts offer](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1433586926937571408); [prompt scale](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1433586926937571408/1433604880785735780); [teacher caution](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1433586926937571408/1436460126189125843).

The October GSM8K investigation shows why a base model's “score” must include its prompt and data provenance. Levanter inference reported 79.76 on `gsm8k_cot_llama` with an eight-shot chain-of-thought style, whereas the plain `gsm8k` task gave about 55.27 under its five-shot/default formatting. The team checked model-export parity as a possible cause, but the prompt variant explained much of the apparent discrepancy; the Qwen export concern was kept open separately. A data audit then found GSM8K *test* examples in Dolmino Math already tokenized into the cooldown source, despite a filter intended to remove them. Dolmino's `Question/Reasoning/Answer` rendering differed from the benchmark's `Question/Answer/####` rendering. The team replaced Dolmino Math with MegaMath and reran the cooldown rather than using the contaminated generation score as a clean capability claim. [Two GSM8K settings](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1422982643749748828); [plain-task score](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1422996977842393170); [export concern](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1422986080113922058/1422988451028144242); [contamination finding](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1422998188440293497); [format difference](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1422998399166451713); [replacement](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1422998651361427617).

That audit also exposed a broader measurement choice. One participant proposed “benchmaxing” by sourcing training data for named reported evaluations; another preferred measuring general reasoning, extrapolation, and long-context skills and using benchmark perplexity as a diagnostic without training directly for the leaderboard. The conversation did not resolve a universal metric, but it made data contamination, prompt sensitivity, and the distinction between evaluation *metric* and *task distribution* explicit. [Metric-versus-task distinction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1434321042415030372); [benchmark-data caution](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1434157177341214720/1434157330651549727).

The model appeared strong on several pretraining-loss comparisons: a posted September-data table gave mean perplexity 5.76 for Marin 32B against 6.6 for OLMo 2 32B across six scientific, news, code, and Wikipedia sets. A separate benchmark suite ranked the finished Marin variants differently: Bison had mean reciprocal rank 0.39, Mantis 0.44, and Qwen2.5-32B Base 0.54. These are different measurements, not a contradiction to hide. They support narrower statements about distributions and protocols, not that one model dominates every peer. A Llama-Nemotron discussion likewise found little AIME signal from small models without explicit reasoning-style prompting, reinforcing that prompt and model stage belong beside the score. [Perplexity table](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1423212820207763466); [rank comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1432164181699727380); [AIME caveat](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1432821367430053968).

Even obtaining the final evaluation checkpoint was nontrivial. A Ray TPU topology change caused one job to request 513 host actors when 256 were expected; the multi-host evaluator then hung because that evaluation path did not use the needed Ray TPU setup. An apparent malformed Safetensors checkpoint was ultimately a wrong cache/path selection: the true GCS shards were about 9 GB each, while the selected mounted files were about 2 GB. The model loaded on a v5p-8, after which dependency packaging still needed a workaround. These are release-pipeline failures, not evidence about model quality, and explain why early evaluations arrived piecemeal. [Topology mismatch](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1420937243568111717); [evaluator limitation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1421232509127233586); [wrong-path correction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1421254536747552808); [v5p load](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1421574654157652068).

The model was later released, with GGUF quantizations reported in June 2026. The channel leaves a reproducible set of cautions: record exact data order and optimizer state around instabilities, audit loggers before diagnosing norm changes, compare train loss with held-out loss, state prompt variants, and rerun contaminated stages. Sharing every spike checkpoint was proposed as open-science infrastructure, but the team estimated around $50 in egress for one full 32B optimizer checkpoint and favored reconstructing token order from the deterministic loader or targeted access over mass upload.
