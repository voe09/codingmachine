# #marin-32b

Archive coverage: 2025-04-30–2026-06-13 (UTC); 972 messages, including 103 thread replies. [Open channel in Discord](https://discord.com/channels/1354881461060243556/1367246647062564995).

## Stabilizing the 32B run

The channel is a case study in diagnosing training instability without assuming every loss spike has the same cause. Participants proposed replaying and ablating the batches before update-norm blow-ups, inspecting optimizer-state changes, measuring dead neurons, and running cooldowns on checkpoints before and after a spike to see whether it damaged downstream quality. Data instrumentation was proposed alongside gradient instrumentation because distribution shifts could be involved. These were investigation plans, not established causal explanations. [Investigation plan](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1379675233610502217) · [Data hypothesis](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1377684208205430814).

An initial clipping version was found ineffective; the next proposal combined skip-step behavior with clipping updates against a sliding historical mean plus three standard deviations. The reasoning was that loss spikes often followed update spikes, although not every update spike was harmful. Later, a QK-normalization warm-start caught up to an Adam run and was reported stable. The channel does not isolate one definitive cure for every earlier spike. [Clipping revision](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1381040055757307976) · [QK-normalization status](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1387111361024233564).

The subsequent midtraining and cooldown stages used AdamC and were reported to run without the earlier spike pattern. A later cooldown combined a new mix with a Feistel shuffle and improved macro Paloma and code loss versus the prior cooldown, but the team explicitly could not separate the mix change from the shuffle change. This is a useful experimental-design caution: when a large run changes multiple recipe components, quality gains should not be attributed to a single component without an ablation. ## Data choices and interpreting model quality

The recipe was constrained by rights as well as performance. Nemotron CC-v2 licensing was flagged as potentially incompatible with Marin's release goals. The group also discussed using OpenThoughts data in later post-training and the possibility of a larger, not-yet-annotated OpenThoughts 4 release. A dataset that improves loss is not automatically usable in an openly reproducible model. [License concern](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1413766160150954075) · [OpenThoughts discussion](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1433586926937571408).

The model appeared strong on several pretraining-loss comparisons, including a posted table of lower perplexity than OLMo 2 32B on sampled scientific, news, code, and Wikipedia sets. That does not settle all downstream capabilities. A separate rank comparison placed Marin 32B variants differently—Bison at mean reciprocal rank 0.39, Mantis at 0.44, and Qwen2.5-32B Base at 0.54—under that particular evaluation suite. The channel also highlighted that GSM8K training-example formatting differed from benchmark formatting, and a Llama-Nemotron comparison found little AIME signal from small models without explicit reasoning-style prompting. The general lesson is to keep protocol, task, and model stage attached to any quality claim. [Perplexity table](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1423212820207763466) · [Rank comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1432164181699727380) · [GSM8K format](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1422998399166451713) · [AIME caveat](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1367246647062564995/1432821367430053968).

The channel later reported public availability and practical access paths, including GGUF quantizations. Those announcements are useful pointers to artifacts, but the enduring learning here is the sequence of stability diagnosis, recipe changes, and protocol-specific evaluation rather than a blanket claim that the release outperformed every peer.
