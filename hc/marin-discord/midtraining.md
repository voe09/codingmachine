# #midtraining

Archive coverage: 2026-03-17–2026-09-11 (UTC); 328 messages, including 183 thread replies. Every exported message body and reply in this channel was reviewed. [Open channel in Discord](https://discord.com/channels/1354881461060243556/1483266366772351067).

## Continued pretraining on the Delphi scaling ladder

The central experiment took **fully annealed Delphi checkpoints** and trained further on Nemotron CC-math mixed with replayed pretraining data. The team called it “midtraining,” while acknowledging that this particular setup was closer to continued pretraining than swapping the data mix during the original annealing phase. Prior ability to forecast *learning-rate annealing* without changing the data mix did not imply ability to forecast this new-data phase. A proposal to hold optimizer state and all settings constant while replacing only the cooldown mix was a separate, more literal midtraining test; early attempts crashed, and this channel does not report its result. [Terminology and forecasting distinction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1491282178716209264/1491920742516785163) · [Main setup](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1483266366772351067/1501637620588089424) · [Optimizer-state comparison](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1501637620588089424/1501660447563059321) · [Attempt status](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1483266366772351067/1507852049591697558).

For the 1e20, 1e21, and 1e22-FLOP ISO-FLOP models, new training tokens were set to **20% of each model's pretraining tokens**. The sweep compared pretraining-replay/math ratios of 67/33, 50/50, and 33/67, with linear learning-rate schedules at 0.33×, 0.5×, 0.67×, and 0.83× the pretraining peak. More math and lower learning rate looked favorable for held-out math loss, while more replay better protected general Paloma loss. The author explicitly treated perplexity as a noisy proxy, not a downstream math score. These were recipe comparisons within that ladder, not a universal optimal math fraction. [Sweep and tradeoff](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1483266366772351067/1501637620588089424).

An end-to-end follow-up took those checkpoints through SFT hyperparameter search and Evalchemy. It reported MATH-500 increasing roughly **2→45** across a 3e18→1e22 best-cell ladder, with 33% replay/67% math generally beating more replay-heavy cells. Base-model MATH-500 pass@128 reportedly rose from 10.4 before midtraining to 79.6 after it, suggesting the math data supplied more than a pass@k-to-pass@1 conversion. Conventional NLP scores did not appear to collapse, but the largest midtrained/SFT model was still described as only comparable to a modern ~2–3B instruct model on general NLP. SFT without math midtraining reportedly did little for math. The Delphi models were not overtrained and had little math in their original mix, so comparisons with external instruct models need FLOP, data, and evaluation context. [SFT ladder and caveats](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1483266366772351067/1517650624915836928) · [Pass@128 observation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1517650624915836928/1517651368654012526) · [General NLP qualification](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1517650624915836928/1517652241555787936).

## Forecasting failed partly because “held out” was not independent

Early attempts to forecast the midtraining endpoint were difficult. Within a fixed learning-rate/mix/scale cell, about half the run was needed to predict final validation loss within 0.02 nats; fitting across cells did not help. Extrapolating from smaller scales to 1e22 missed by about 0.1 nats, and the 1e22 run also accidentally used 3.4% warmup rather than the intended 10%. Those observations initially suggested a hard scaling problem, but the later audit changed their interpretation. [Forecast and warmup report](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1483266366772351067/1507658133844263036).

Nemotron CC-math contained documents duplicated under different formatting, and the “held-out” math validation sample had been constructed by shuffling within that corpus. After building a cleaner validation set, the author reported the scale-loss mismatch falling from **19% to 3%**. An ISO-token comparison also made the math-loss scaling smoother. This did not make the training recipe itself invalid; it meant validation loss had not measured independent generalization as intended. The practical proposal was to split at document level, then decontaminate validation against training until enough clean validation examples remained. The group still regarded 3% as imperfect and cautioned that some plotted or issue scores were stale. [Duplicate diagnosis](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1483266366772351067/1521289209279676648) · [19%→3% correction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1483266366772351067/1521289471494983743) · [Cleaner split proposal](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1483266366772351067/1521290749545549854) · [Record caveat](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1483266366772351067/1521407119423836262).

## Benchmark gains need contamination and decoding audits

A separate 10-gram overlap audit against Nemotron CC-math found overlap for **2.43% of GSM8K test**, **1.95% of GSM8K train**, and about **28% (141/500) of MATH-500**. Manual inspection found many matching math documents contained both the problem and solution. Thus the high MATH-500 scores cannot be read as clean generalization from this Discord evidence alone, even though GSM8K overlap looked much lower. The benchmark *version*, the SFT datasets, and the exact overlap definition mattered; one discussion noted missing dataset links in the experiment issue before they were added. [Overlap audit](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1483266366772351067/1523154815599902791) · [MATH count](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1483266366772351067/1523033758947606740) · [SFT data-link correction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1523162004041371742/1523269386729947257).

The Snowball MoE follow-up showed the **opposite** kind of evaluation artifact. An early MATH-500 score near 16% looked low relative to its GSM8K score, but trace inspection found that about **87% of its MATH-500 errors hit the generation-token cap** before producing a final answer. Raising the limit to 32K yielded **271/500 = 54.2%** in a rerun. The model was also very verbose and sometimes looped after an answer instead of emitting EOS. Packing during pretraining, cooldown, SFT, and decoding settings were floated as possible causes; the channel did not isolate one. A benchmark configuration can therefore suppress a capable reasoner, just as data overlap can inflate another score. [Truncation analysis](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1483266366772351067/1528114941360340993) · [32K rerun](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1528114941360340993/1528172950857978006) · [EOS observation](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1483266366772351067/1528178329193283724) · [Possible causes](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1528178329193283724/1528189549191106733).

## From ladder experiment to a reusable post-training stack

The Snowball 67B-A2B cooldown supplied a working chain of chat templating, midtraining/SFT, serving, and evaluation. One reported **700,000-example SFT took under an hour** and evaluation around 30 minutes. The initial caveat was missing Levanter DenseMixer support for router adjustment; a later controlled A/B found no benefit in that setting, reducing the immediate reason to maintain a separate SFT stack. One A/B does not establish that DenseMixer is useless across all sparse models or batch-size regimes. [Snowball handoff](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1483266366772351067/1527726088799649953) · [DenseMixer A/B](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1483266366772351067/1528706452162609215) · [Possible regime dependence](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1528706452162609215/1528793111566745671).

The 1e23 Delphi extension was stopped after about **38% of its planned 20%-of-pretraining token budget** because preemption made the full run too slow. After minimal SFT it appeared strong relative to the 1e22 post-trained model, and RL reward trends were positive, but the author said only a train-on-test setting drove benchmark gains at that scale. The team explicitly warned that an incomplete token budget should not be fitted as an ordinary point in a scaling law. A proposed canonical evaluation would compare pretrained checkpoints after a fixed amount of SFT and RL, making future handoffs and cross-run comparisons less anecdotal. [1e23 outcome and limits](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1529063345460875444/1529064898108330114) · [Scaling-law warning](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1529063345460875444/1529154726388568287) · [Canonical metric request](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1529063345460875444/1529155233035452636).

Other directions stayed at proposal stage. Failed and successful RL trajectories might be reused for continued training if labeled with their outcomes, so the model can learn from error states without treating failures as desired completions; no Marin experiment on that recipe is reported here. The OpenThoughts discussion also identified naturally occurring agent traces as scarce and suggested converting real code-review/commit cycles, while worrying that too many synthetic rollouts might collapse diversity. These are hypotheses about data supply, not measured midtraining gains.
