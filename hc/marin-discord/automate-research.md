## Research workflow and useful agent tasks

The proposed model was a human-directed research loop: researchers state a testable question in a GitHub issue, an agent implements and runs experiments, records intermediate evidence, and receives feedback before a result becomes a durable conclusion. Natural-language interaction is an interface to code, data, and measurements, not a replacement for them. One concrete candidate was porting a PyTorch or NanoGPT method into Grug/JAX with a comparable test, which is more useful than merely summarizing the paper. [Workflow vision](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462884917292699669/1462893070809960459) · [Porting task](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462884917292699669/1464705505690783754).

Agents could complete substantial engineering tasks—one report described a multi-host inference refactor and a threefold ragged-paged-attention throughput improvement—but the channel also supplied a strong caution against taking agent explanations at face value. A claimed RunAI loading slowdown measured the entire startup through XLA compilation. Raw logs showed RunAI transfer at about 1 GiB/s versus roughly 300 MiB/s for a Levanter/fsspec path, reversing the narrative. The methodological lesson is to define the measured interval, inspect logs, and reproduce the comparison before crediting an agent's causal story. [Inference report](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462884917292699669/1469544188403257488) · [Measurement correction](https://marin-discord.pages.dev/#1462884917292699669/1486094524139835432).

The discussion also exposed coordination limits: high-volume hill-climbing created thousands of agent threads, while long-lived research needed a harness and durable records outside transient chat state. The group questioned whether current agents mostly accelerate search over known knobs or can generate genuinely new hypotheses. The archive raises that distinction; it does not resolve it.
